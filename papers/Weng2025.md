## Key Findings

1. **Bistability as the amplification mechanism.** The DC homing subsystem (Eqs. 3–6) exhibits a bistable regime bounded by saddle-node bifurcation curves (cusp at approximately u, [DC_PT] = (1.32, 0.28)×10³/μl·day). Under daytime vaccination (ZT7, mouse active-phase onset), the trajectory of soluble CCL21 and lymph-node DCs transiently crosses the saddle point and enters the high-state basin of attraction before returning to the low stable state as peripheral DCs decay. Under nighttime vaccination (ZT19), trajectories remain in the low-state basin. This single passage through the high state translates into approximately threefold higher BMDC homing to draining lymph nodes at ZT7 vs ZT19 and a corresponding ~threefold increase in activated CD4+ T cells — reproducing the experimental mouse data.

2. **Vaccine-type-dependent reversal of optimal timing.** For endotoxin-adjuvanted and DC-based vaccines, ZT7 (daytime) is optimal because CLOCK-BMAL1 peaks near ZT6 and maximally drives CCL21-mediated DC migration. For nucleic-acid-adjuvanted vaccines, the CLOCK-BMAL1 complex additionally suppresses TLR9-mediated DC activation at ZT7; the competing repression dominates, shifting the optimum to ZT19. The model predicts that anti-HAV + CpG or SARS-CoV-2 RNA vaccines should be more efficacious when administered in the late afternoon/evening — qualitatively matching published human observations for BNT162b2 (mRNA, stronger in late afternoon) and inactivated SARS-CoV-2 (stronger in morning).

3. **Long-term persistence of timing differences.** Adaptive immune scores ($\varepsilon_{adaptive}$) retain circadian variation for weeks post-vaccination in simulation, matching experimental reports that antibody-titer differences between morning and afternoon cohorts persist for at least one month. The model provides a mechanistic explanation: the bistable transition early in the homing phase locks in a higher effector/memory pool that continues to oscillate with CLOCK-BMAL1-driven trafficking rhythms.

4. **Sensitivity analysis.** The parameters with the greatest impact on the adaptive response are the clock-sensitivity thresholds for DC activation (K_CB^APCm) and for CCL21-mediated homing (K_CB^CCL21), confirming that the clock-homing coupling — not downstream T/B cell dynamics — is the rate-limiting control point.

5. **Model validation.** The model reproduces: (a) circadian mRNA oscillations of Bmal1 (peak CT0), Cry (CT20), Ror (CT19), Rev-erbα (CT8) from CircaDB; (b) CCL21 peak at CT9 in lymph nodes; (c) lymphocyte peak migration at CT12 matching Druzd 2017; (d) LPS-induced pulsatile IL-6/TNF-α with biphasic IL-10 from rat data; (e) dose-dependent and time-dependent BMDC homing and CD4+ T-cell expansion in mice (Ince 2023).

## Limitations

1. **Mouse/rodent calibration only.** All experimental validation uses mouse ZT data (Zeitgeber time relative to the light cycle). Human circadian phasing differs, and the CT-to-clock-time mapping is not established for human vaccination contexts. Whether the bistable regime boundaries are preserved in human DC biology is unknown.

2. **Single-sex calibration.** The model was calibrated on male-mouse data and does not account for sex hormone effects on BMAL1, CCL21, or TLR expression. Predictions for women may be systematically biased.

3. **No individual variation.** Parameters are fixed population means. Inter-individual variation in circadian period, amplitude (shift workers, chronotypes, jet-lagged travelers), and immune baseline is not modeled. The width of the optimal timing window under realistic circadian variation is an open question.

4. **Vaccine-timing predictions are qualitative.** The model correctly predicts the direction of the timing effect for the three vaccine types, but quantitative antibody-titer or T-cell-frequency differences should not be taken as precise predictions until independently replicated with the same adjuvant/adjuvant formulation in controlled human studies.

5. **Abstract clock model.** The circadian clock module is adapted from Abo & Layton 2021 (a single-cell model); tissue-level clock heterogeneity, desynchrony, and circadian disruption are not represented.

6. **No model code released in preprint form.** Code is listed as available at https://github.com/npw913/Circa_immune_simulation, but the repository's state at preprint posting [UNVERIFIED — not confirmed accessible].

7. **Preprint status.** This is an unreviewed bioRxiv preprint. Modeling choices, parameter-fitting procedures, and mechanistic interpretations have not yet undergone peer scrutiny.
