## Key Findings

**1. Afternoon stimulation produces stronger LN expansion (ZT7 > ZT19).**
Total LN cellularity after FITC painting or BMDC injection is significantly higher when the stimulus occurs during the day (ZT7) compared with the night (ZT19). The difference emerges at 24 h and is sustained at 48 h. The plateau in dose-response of injected BMDCs at ZT19 (requiring 10× more cells to match the ZT7 daytime response) suggests a threshold mechanism consistent with bistability (see q37).

**2. DC migration to draining LNs is diurnally rhythmic.**
More BMDCs drain to the popliteal LN when injected at ZT7 than at ZT19 (quantified by confocal imaging). 2P-IVM shows that CD4+ T cells within the LN also migrate faster during the day. A mathematical model combining DC homing and T cell infiltration predicts maximal DC–T cell interaction probability in the afternoon, providing a functional rationale for the timing window.

**3. Rhythmic ICAM-1 expression on HEVs is BMAL1-dependent in endothelial cells.**
*Icam1* mRNA and ICAM-1 protein on HEVs are higher after daytime FITC painting than nighttime. ChIP assays confirm BMAL1 binds the *Icam1* promoter (E-box regions R3/R4) with greater occupancy during the day. Endothelial-specific BMAL1-knockout mice (BMAL1ΔAEC) fail to upregulate ICAM-1 rhythmically after stimulation, and their LNs show arrhythmic, blunted expansion. Anti-ICAM-1 antibody treatment abrogates the time-of-day difference in LN cellularity.

**4. TNF from the draining LN drives the ICAM-1 rhythm.**
*Tnf* mRNA is rhythmically elevated in LNs after FITC painting during the day (but other cytokines and chemokines tested are not). Anti-TNF treatment reduces ICAM-1 and renders LN expansion arrhythmic. TNF is proposed as the paracrine signal linking DC arrival to HEV ICAM-1 upregulation.

**5. CD4+ T cell autonomous circadian rhythms amplify and sustain the response.**
In T cell-specific BMAL1-knockout mice (BMAL1ΔTcell), CD4+ T cell counts in LNs following BMDC injection remain rhythmic early but proliferation differences are lost. LN cellularity differences at ZT7 vs. ZT19 are significantly reduced by 48 h. Ex vivo, CD4+ T cells from the LN at ZT7 show greater proliferative capacity than at ZT13/ZT19. Mass spectrometry of LN CD4+ T cells at ZT1 vs. ZT13 reveals PCA separation driven by immune regulation and metabolic (lipid, ketone, amine) clusters; BMAL1ΔTcell T cells lose most of this proteomic divergence.

**6. Mathematical modeling validates multi-stage rhythmicity as essential for sustained LN expansion.**
The integrated model (ID4) shows that rhythmicity in both homing and proliferation is required to maintain elevated LN cellularity; ablating either component individually reduces fold-change, and ablating both collapses it to arrhythmic baseline. Model predictions agree with BMAL1ΔTcell and BMAL1ΔAEC experimental data (Figs. 4c–f). The model also identifies the afternoon as the optimal window for DC–T cell interactions.

**7. Vaccination timing translates into durable adaptive immune differences.**
- **Hepatitis A (HAV/HAVRIX):** ZT7-vaccinated wild-type mice have significantly more germinal center B cells at day 14 and significantly enhanced antigen-specific serum IgG titers and IL-2-producing CD4+/CD8+ T cells at day 28. BMAL1ΔTcell mice lose the timing advantage for T cell responses; integrin blockade abolishes both humoral and cellular differences.
- **SARS-CoV-2 (RBD):** CD4+ and CD8+ T cell responses are significantly stronger when vaccination occurred at ZT7 vs. ZT19 at day 28; effect requires functional T cell BMAL1 and intact integrin-mediated homing.

**8. Key mechanistic model (Fig. 4d):**
APC (DC) migration → rhythmic LN recruitment (Lymph Node Response I: activation) → rhythmic CD4+ T cell proliferation sustained for weeks (Lymph Node Response II) → long-term memory and reactivation. Clock control at step 1 (DC trafficking via BMAL1ΔAEC) and step 2 (T cell proliferation via BMAL1ΔTcell) are independently necessary; loss of either attenuates the downstream chain.

## Limitations

- All experiments are in mice (C57BL/6N). Murine circadian phase is inverted relative to diurnal humans (ZT7, active-phase offset for mice, corresponds to early morning for humans, but the optimal window is at the active-phase onset in both). Translation requires careful attention to human chronotype and shift-work status.
- Only CD4+ T cells are examined for cell-intrinsic proteomics and proliferation rhythms; whether CD8+ T cell-autonomous clocks contribute independently is not directly tested at the mechanistic level (though CD8+ functional readouts at day 28 do show vaccination timing effects).
- Vaccination experiments use a single dose at a single site; the interaction between circadian timing, adjuvant type, and dose is not systematically explored. The TNF–ICAM-1 mechanism may differ for adjuvants that directly activate the inflammasome or toll-like receptors at different expression-phase windows.
- Mathematical models are fitted to data with 3–14 mice per group and pooled across 2–5 independent experiments; parameter identifiability is assessed via profile likelihood, but the confidence intervals on the predicted optimal timing window (the grey shaded region in Fig. 1h) span several hours.
- The BMAL1 conditional knockouts are constitutive (BMAL1ΔTcell) or tamoxifen-inducible (BMAL1ΔAEC); compensatory developmental changes in either lineage cannot be excluded.
- Only ZT7 and ZT19 are used as the two principal time points for most comparisons; intermediate ZT values (ZT1, ZT13) are used in some assays but the full time-course resolution is limited.
- The study does not examine sex as a biological variable in the immune-timing analyses; both sexes are used in mixed groups.
