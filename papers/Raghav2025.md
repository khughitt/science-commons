## Key Findings

**Epidemiology**

- CUP accounts for 2–4% of all cancers; ~37,370 new US cases estimated in 2025; worldwide incidence is declining (2–15 per 100,000 person-years) owing to improved diagnostics and broader molecular profiling.
- Histology: adenocarcinoma (59%), poorly differentiated or undifferentiated carcinoma (31%), squamous (9%); multiple-site metastases in 33% of patients.

**Pathobiology — the CUP-specific model**

- CUP is proposed to arise from an aggressive prometastatic clone that seeds distant sites early during tumorigenesis, before the primary tumor becomes clinically detectable.
- The primary site then undergoes immune elimination (regression) or immune equilibrium (dormancy), leaving no identifiable lesion — a trajectory distinct from the immune-escape model of metastatic cancer of known primary.
- Chromosomal instability (CIN) and distinct molecular profiles distinguish CUP from matched-origin metastases, suggesting a core biology not reducible to origin-site misclassification.

**Prognostic factors**

- Poor prognosis markers: male sex, poor performance status, adenocarcinoma histology, high metastatic burden, liver or peritoneal involvement, high neutrophil-lymphocyte ratio.
- Adverse molecular markers: KRAS or NRAS mutation, CDKN2A deletion.
- Favorable subsets (~20% of cases) include presentations with clinicopathological patterns analogous to known primaries (e.g., axillary adenopathy in women → breast, CK20+/CDX2+ → colorectal, peritoneal carcinomatosis in women → ovarian).

**Diagnosis**

- Immunophenotyping (IHC panel, average 8.8 stains per case) remains the diagnostic mainstay; accuracy is high for well-differentiated tumors but limited for poorly differentiated ones.
- ~67% of patients have insufficient tissue remaining for molecular profiling after IHC workup.
- Tissue-of-origin molecular assays (gene expression, methylation, ctDNA) achieve 65–99% accuracy in prediction; AI-based pathology tools (e.g., Lu et al., *Nature* 2021) may represent the next frontier.

**Treatment**

- Both site-specific therapy (mimicking treatment of the putative primary) and empirical platinum-based chemotherapy are guideline-acceptable for unfavorable CUP.
- RCTs comparing site-specific versus empirical therapy show mixed results: the Fudan CUP-001 trial showed improved PFS with a 90-gene expression assay-guided therapy (median 9.8 vs. 7.3 months, HR 0.70; P=0.29), while the CUPISCO phase 2 trial showed a PFS benefit for molecularly guided therapy after disease control.
- Tissue-agnostic targetable alterations are present in a meaningful fraction of CUP; screening for BRAF V600E, NTRK/RET fusions, HER2 amplification, dMMR/MSI-H, and TMB-high is clinically actionable.
- Current major guidelines (NCCN, ESMO) do NOT recommend routine molecular profiling for tissue-of-origin identification given limited evidence of outcome benefit over conventional diagnostic approaches; clinical trial participation is strongly encouraged.

## Limitations

- The review does not include systematic meta-analytic pooling; evidence quality assessment is narrative.
- RCT evidence for site-specific therapy remains limited and inconclusive: most trials are underpowered, with non-significant primary endpoints.
- The pathobiological model (immune elimination / dormancy of primary) is plausible but not mechanistically proven; it is presented as a working hypothesis without direct experimental support reviewed.
- The review does not cover pediatric CUP or CUP arising in immunosuppressed patients.
- Molecular profiling coverage of actionable alterations is variable; blood-based ctDNA profiling is discussed as an emerging alternative when tissue is insufficient but evidence is still early.
- Approximately two-thirds of patients lack sufficient tissue for molecular profiling after standard IHC, a major practical limitation not fully resolved.
