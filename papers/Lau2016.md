## One-Sentence Summary

A crowdsourced NIH Jamboree exercise assembled multi-study gene expression compendia for five autoimmune diseases in human and mouse, then performed pan-disease meta-analysis revealing conserved interferon and immune-activation signatures shared across SLE, RA, MS, and DM1 — and across species.

## Key Findings

1. **Substantial pan-disease signature overlap among autoimmune diseases.** Meta-analysis of 39 human CGPs (comparison group pairs) across DM1, MS, RA, sarcoid, and SLE identified large numbers of differentially expressed genes per disease: RA had the most (1,316 UP / 1,550 DOWN), SLE 1,183 UP / 500 DOWN, sarcoid 2,513 UP / 2,381 DOWN, MS 971 UP / 1,106 DOWN, DM1 762 UP / 766 DOWN (PFP ≤ 0.05). Odds-ratio matrices (Jaccard-based) show disease pairs sharing substantially more genes than expected by chance, especially within species. Cross-disease ORs among SLE, RA, and pan-disease were among the strongest (OR >> 1 for both UP and DOWN gene overlaps).

2. **Interferon signalling is the dominant pan-disease UP signature in humans.** Pan-disease enrichment of human UP genes (KEGG + Reactome, BH-corrected p ≤ 0.05) returned interferon signalling pathways (Interferon Signalling, Interferon gamma signalling, Interferon alpha/beta signalling), cytokine signalling in immune system, hematopoietic cell lineage, complement and coagulation cascades, and innate immune system as the most enriched terms (top 20 shown in Figure 4A). Chemokine receptors bind chemokines was the only globally enriched term in DOWN human pan-disease genes.

3. **Conserved cross-species signatures are exclusively from UP genes.** When requiring statistical significance in both human and mouse (post-BH correction), all conserved pan-disease terms were derived from UP gene sets. Conserved enriched pathways included: Interferon gamma signalling, GPCR ligand binding, chemokine receptor–bind chemokines, integrin cell surface interactions, cytokine signalling in immune system, hematopoietic cell lineage, and antigen processing and presentation. The authors note this pattern may reflect increases in monocyte/immune cell frequencies in blood driving the pan-disease UP signals.

4. **Intra-species overlap exceeds cross-species overlap.** Pairwise odds-ratio matrices show gene signature overlap between disease pairs is systematically stronger within species than for the same disease matched across human and mouse. This suggests mouse models capture the broad immune response but diverge at the gene-level detail.

5. **Individual disease enrichments replicate known biology and flag some novel associations.** SLE UP genes are enriched in interferon pathways (well-established). RA UP genes include TNF signalling, osteoclast differentiation, complement and coagulation cascades, and — more unexpectedly — a "Malaria" KEGG term driven by genes CR1, GYPA, ICAM1, PECAM1, and TLR4, which the authors link to hydroxychloroquine's Toll-like receptor effects. Platelet-related pathways are enriched across multiple autoimmune diseases. MS DOWN genes are enriched in extracellular matrix organization (human and mouse both).

6. **Effect-size validation confirms rank-based meta-analysis results.** Cohen's *d* rankings from individual CGPs are largely concordant with OMiCC's RankProd (rank-based) meta-analysis output, supporting robustness of the DE gene identification across methodologies (though some discordant genes exist; Figure S1).

7. **Crowdsourcing produced a usable but imperfect dataset.** 86 human CGPs were submitted; 39 survived QC (removal for incorrect annotations, no case/control split, Jaccard overlap > 66% with another CGP). Mouse CGPs were harder to construct and had lower quality overall, with stimulated-cell studies being a major contaminant despite explicit exclusion criteria. The authors conclude that a second review day during the Jamboree would substantially reduce post-hoc QC burden.

## Limitations

- **Microarray only; no RNA-seq.** All CGPs use microarray platforms. RNA-seq would provide better dynamic range and fewer normalization artifacts; the project's Track A DEG preference is RNA-seq first with microarray as fallback.
- **Blood/PBMC-only restriction.** Choosing PBMCs/WB ensures cross-study comparability but excludes tissue-resident biology relevant to disease pathogenesis (e.g., pancreatic islets in DM1, CNS in MS). Pan-disease blood signatures may reflect immune-cell trafficking and activation rather than primary disease mechanisms.
- **Sarcoid and Sjögren's effectively dropped.** No samples passed QC for sarcoid (mouse) or Sjögren's (either species), and sarcoid is retained for human only. Pan-disease claims rest on a subset of the intended diseases.
- **Overlapping samples inflate OR statistics.** The same GEO sample can appear in multiple CGPs (Jaccard overlap < 66% threshold allows substantial sharing). This inflates the pairwise odds ratios in Figures 3B-C — the "overlap" may partly reflect sample duplication rather than biological concordance.
- **Volunteer QC heterogeneity.** Compliance with the "no stimulated cells" criterion was imperfect, particularly for mouse CGPs. Post-hoc QC removed many CGPs but the remaining ones are still a convenience sample of what the 29 volunteers happened to annotate.
- **No correction for study/platform batch effects.** Meta-analysis is performed across studies with different platforms and cohorts; RankProd's rank-based approach is robust but does not remove platform-specific gene-coverage differences (common genes across platforms vary widely: 5,644–16,012 common genes per disease).
- **Mouse-to-human validity.** The paper itself cites prior work showing mouse inflammatory models poorly mimic human transcriptomic responses (ref 6 in paper: Seok et al. 2013). The cross-species conserved signature being restricted to UP genes and interferon pathways is consistent with selective, not comprehensive, conservation.
