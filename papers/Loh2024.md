## Key Findings

### The hallmarks taxonomy (Figure 2)

The paper distinguishes four structural layers:

| Layer | Contents |
|---|---|
| **Defining hallmarks** | Tumor initiation, self-renewal, multipotency |
| **Emerging hallmark** | Plasticity — cancer stemness as a cellular state |
| **Enabling attributes** | Therapy resistance, metastasis (derived from defining hallmarks) |
| **Associated features** | Niche interaction, immune escape (intertwined but not direct CSC hallmarks) |

This structure is directly analogous to Hanahan's hallmarks-of-cancer layering (hallmarks → enabling characteristics → TME features), adapted for the CSC domain. Importantly, the paper explicitly states "CSCs and cancer stemness will be used interchangeably from hereon, with the understanding that CSCs are not hard-wired and do not necessarily represent a cell type."

### Defining hallmark 1: Tumor initiation

Operationally defined as the ability to engraft and form tumors upon transplantation into immunodeficient hosts (xenotransplantation assay). The earliest characterized CSCs were CD34+CD38− cells in human AML, engraftable into NOD/SCID mice. The hallmark encompasses the capacity to overcome environmental barriers and establish tumor growth in a new microenvironment — framed as the adaptive potential of CSCs. Tumor initiation and self-renewal are treated as overlapping but distinct facets; tumor initiation stresses tumorigenicity (engraftment, overcoming barriers), while self-renewal stresses the sustaining of the CSC pool.

Molecular regulation highlights: chromatin remodeling (IKAROS/IKZF2 in LSCs), RNA editing (ADAR1 in CML and GBM, suppressing miRNA biogenesis to drive stemness genes), m6A modification (YTHDF2 and IGF2BP2 as opposing regulators of oncogenic mRNA stability), Wnt/β-catenin programs (BEX1, MLL1, KRAS-driven epigenetic remodeling), Hedgehog-PTCH1 axis, and metabolic rewiring (OXPHOS vs. glycolysis, amino acid and fatty acid metabolism as CSC fuel sources). CSCs from different cancer types may preferentially use OXPHOS (pancreatic) or glycolysis (via mcPGCK1 in liver CSCs) depending on metabolic context.

### Defining hallmark 2: Self-renewal

The capacity of CSCs to perpetually replenish the CSC pool while maintaining stem-like properties. Genetic ablation of CSC lineages consistently reduces tumor growth; CSC-sustaining organoids can be maintained long-term ex vivo. Molecular regulation overlaps heavily with tumor initiation programs; key additional signals include stemness transcriptional circuits (OCT4, SOX2 and related factors), Wnt/β-catenin (MLL1/KMT2A as essential for Wnt-dependent intestinal tumorigenesis), and chromatin accessibility control by LSKS/IKZF2 (myeloid differentiation gene silencing concurrent with self-renewal gene opening).

p53 is highlighted as an increasingly recognized regulator of the CSC-to-non-CSC boundary: p53 loss permits MYC-driven cancer stemness maintenance and removes a key brake on self-renewal programs.

### Defining hallmark 3: Multipotency

CSCs can generate multiple lineages within a tumor, thereby driving intratumoral heterogeneity. Examples across cancer types: PDAC CD9^high CSCs generate both mesenchymal and epithelial lineages; T-ALL PD-1+ LSCs give rise to PD-1+, Lin−CD3+c-Kit^Mid, and PD-1− progeny; SORE6+ breast CSCs generate both SORE6+ and SORE6− cells. Multipotency is treated as a mechanism by which a small CSC subpopulation governs cellular diversity and adaptive therapeutic responses.

The multipotency → metastasis link is elaborated: CSCs transdifferentiate into endothelial-like cells (vasculogenesis), pericytes (angiogenesis), and other cell types to meet contextual demands during the metastatic cascade. This phenotypic flexibility is framed as an enabling attribute of metastasis deriving from multipotency.

### Emerging hallmark: Plasticity (cancer stemness as a cellular state)

This is the conceptually most significant section for this project. The authors argue that three modes of plasticity operate:

1. **Dedifferentiation** — differentiated cancer cells revert to a progenitor-like CSC state
2. **Blocked differentiation** — cells are maintained in a partially differentiated, progenitor-like state
3. **Transdifferentiation** — cells switch to a different developmental program (different tissue-specific traits)

The paper explicitly cites the Hanahan 2022 "New Dimensions" paper (Hallmarks of Cancer: New Dimensions) as the source that elevated "unlocking phenotypic plasticity" as a hallmark, and frames the plasticity emerging hallmark of CSCs as mechanistically overlapping with — but narrower than — Hanahan's full plasticity hallmark.

The TME is centrally implicated in inducing non-cell-autonomous plasticity: CAFs (via HGF, IL-6, IL-8, Wnt signaling), T cells (via IFN-γ driving non-CSC to CSC conversions), neutrophils (via TGF-β), and diverse stromal cells provide context-dependent signals that reprogram differentiated cancer cells to CSC states. This "induction by the niche" framing directly positions stemness as a contextual state rather than a cell-intrinsic fixed property.

Cell-intrinsic induction also documented: stress signals (hypoxia, nutrient deprivation, chemotherapy, oxidative stress, isolation stress), LPAR4-induced stemness in PDAC by paclitaxel, PQL phenotype in CRC post-chemotherapy, MEX3A expression defining a latent LGR5^high/MEX3A^high population.

### Enabling attribute: Therapy resistance

CSCs are framed as the primary driver of therapy resistance and post-treatment relapse. Mechanisms enumerated:
- Quiescence (slow-cycling LGR5+ colon CSCs, Lgr5^high p57+ CRCs)
- Drug efflux (NKG2D ligand-negative LSC subpopulation amplified leukemic initiation and resistant to cytarabine)
- Preservation of genomic integrity (ROSlow LSCs with higher HK2 interact with DNA response proteins)
- Signaling pathway rewiring (JAK1/STAT3 in CD133^high lung CSCs under AXL inhibition; Wnt/β-catenin and Hippo in liver CSCs under lenvatinib)
- Metabolic reprogramming (fatty acid oxidation in CD24−CD44+ breast CSCs; OXPHOS/amino acid rewiring in venetoclax + azacitidine resistance in AML LSCs)
- Cellular plasticity (non-CSC to CSC conversion under stress; PD-1+ persister cells with CSC features in T-ALL)

Immunotherapy resistance is separately elaborated: CSCs show elevated PD-L1 expression, impaired antigen presentation (LCOR downregulation), T cell immune exclusion (CD276/B7-H3 upregulation), and TGF-β-mediated suppression of cytotoxic T cell activity. The paper argues that the stemness state itself is a major driver of reduced immunotherapy response, beyond individual immune-evasion markers.

### Enabling attribute: Metastasis

CSCs deploy multipotency to execute the full metastatic cascade by shuttling between functional states (high EMT/invasiveness for dissemination; CSC re-establishment for colonization). Notably, evidence suggests metastasis-initiating cells (MICs) are partially distinct from primary-tumor CSCs: Lgr5− cells (not Lgr5+ CSCs) seed blood-borne metastases in CRC; L1CAM+ disseminating cells have an EMP1+ / LGR5-low phenotype. The overlap and distinction between CSC and MIC programs is an unresolved question the authors flag explicitly.

### Associated feature: Niche interaction and immune escape

The CSC niche is composed of CAFs, macrophages, T cells, neutrophils, endothelial cells, neurons, adipocytes, and glial cells (Figure 5), all exchanging cytokines, growth factors, EVs, and direct-contact signals. CAFs (especially CD10+GPR77+ CAFs in breast cancer) are the most documented niche supporters of stemness. CSCs reciprocally remodel the niche — secreting POSTN to polarize macrophages, driving angiogenesis (VEGF, CD44v6), and shaping immunosuppressive TME via CCL2, TGF-β, PD-L1, CD47, VCAM1 expression on CSC surfaces.

Immune escape mechanisms include: CSC-mediated instigating immunosuppressive cell types (FcεRIα+ macrophages via IL-33; MDSCs via YAP/CXCL5; regulatory T cells via PRC1/CCL2), and direct immune killing evasion (NK cell impairment via NKGCD2 absence; CD47 "don't-eat-me" signal; SIRP-γ^high anti-phagocytosis in lung CSCs).

### Future directions and open questions

The authors flag:
- Whether CSC markers are cancer-type-exclusive or overlapping (LGR5+ cells also CD133+?)
- Whether there is plasticity by which CD44+ can transform into CD133+ (marker state transitions)
- What regulates the abundance of each CSC subtype during tumor progression
- Need for single-cell profiling combined with genomic, proteomic, metabolomic, and spatial data to capture CSC evolutionary dynamics throughout malignant progression

## Limitations

- Almost all mechanistic CSC data comes from cell lines, xenograft models, or GEMM experiments; limited in vivo human validation of most molecular mechanisms.
- Xenotransplantation assay (the "gold standard") conflates tumor-initiation potential with assay-specific survival capability (resistance to dissociation, immune environments of immunodeficient hosts) — a methodological limitation the paper explicitly acknowledges but does not resolve.
- The taxonomy of defining/emerging/enabling/associated does not have a formal operational criterion distinguishing enabling attributes from associated features — the boundary between therapy resistance (enabling) and immune escape (associated) is somewhat arbitrary.
- The paper treats plasticity as an "emerging hallmark," placing it in a separate tier from defining hallmarks, but does not provide a formal criterion for when a hallmark should be promoted from emerging to defining.
- MIC vs. CSC distinction is flagged as unresolved but not analyzed — the paper does not synthesize the contradictory evidence into a testable framework.
- The majority of niche interaction data concerns CAF-CSC interactions in breast, colon, and liver cancers; the generalizability across cancer types with different stromal architectures is assumed rather than demonstrated.
- Does not engage with evolutionary population genetics or quantitative models of CSC dynamics; all evolutionary arguments are qualitative.
