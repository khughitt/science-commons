## Key Findings

**Scale of seasonal gene expression:**
- 5,136 unique genes (≈23% of the protein-coding genome tested) showed seasonal differences in BABYDIET PBMCs (genome-wide significance P ≤ 1.52 × 10⁻⁰⁶).
- 2,311 genes were summer-expressed (mean fold change ≈1.2572); 2,826 were winter-expressed (mean fold change ≈1.3150).
- 1,697 genes showed seasonal expression in the T1D UK replication cohort; 791 (UK/Ireland), 1,257 (Australia), and 409 (US) seasonal genes in the asthma cohorts.

**Hemispheric inversion:**
- Summer-expressed genes in Northern Hemisphere cohorts showed increased expression during the Southern Hemisphere summer (December–February) in Australian asthmatic patients — confirming a solar/environmental rather than calendar driver.
- Icelandic cohort was an outlier: fewer common seasonal genes and a distinct pattern, consistent with near-24-hour daylight attenuating photoperiodic signaling.

**Circadian clock genes are seasonally regulated:**
- 9 of 16 circadian clock genes tested showed seasonal variation in BABYDIET PBMCs including *ARNTL* (BMAL1), *CLOCK*, *CRY1*, *CSNK1D*, *CSNK1E*, *NR1D2*, *RORA*, *TIMELESS*, and *NFIL3*.
- *ARNTL* (BMAL1) mRNA peaked in summer months (June–August; 1.5097-fold difference between winter low and summer high; ANOVA χ²₂, P = 1.04 × 10⁻²³). Expression was independent of time-of-day sampling (morning vs. afternoon clinics), ruling out circadian confound.
- The glucocorticoid receptor *NR3C1* was strongly positively correlated with *ARNTL* (Spearman ρ = 0.819, P = 5.05 × 10⁻¹⁹).

**Hormone receptor seasonality:**
- *ADIPOR1* (adiponectin receptor) and *ESR2* (estradiol receptor) were more highly expressed in summer; *CUL5* (anti-diuretic hormone pathway) was also higher in summer.
- Pro-inflammatory prostaglandin receptors (*PTGDR*, *PTGIR*, *PTGER4*) and leukotrienes (*CYSLTR1*) were more highly expressed in winter.

**Cellular composition drives transcriptomic seasonality:**
- Expression of seasonal genes correlated strongly with the expression of 13 cell-type-specific marker genes, implicating shifts in PBMC composition as a dominant mechanism.
- In the Cambridge BioResource FBC cohort (n = 7,343), total white blood cells, lymphocytes (P = 2.11 × 10⁻¹¹), monocytes (P = 9.14 × 10⁻³⁰), basophils (P = 2.74 × 10⁻⁰⁶), eosinophils (P = 0.00235), neutrophils (P = 2.74 × 10⁻²⁷), and platelets (P = 2.02 × 10⁻¹²) all showed seasonal variation; mean corpuscular volume (MCV, P = 1.32 × 10⁻²¹) also varied.
- HCT (haematocrit) was the only FBC parameter that was not seasonal.
- In The Gambia (n = 4,200), seasonal cell count patterns were completely distinct from UK patterns and peaked during the rainy season (June–October), coinciding with peak infectious disease burden.

**Pro-inflammatory winter immune state in Europe:**
- WGCNA identified 8 winter-expressed co-regulated modules and 3 summer-expressed modules.
- Winter modules were enriched for B-cell receptor (BCR) signalling (P = 3.39 × 10⁻¹⁰), FcR-gamma-associated processes (P = 4.45 × 10⁻⁰⁶), lysosomes, chemokine signalling, phagosome/bacterial infection pathways.
- Summer modules were enriched for RNA transport (P = 1.70 × 10⁻⁰⁷) and RNA degradation (P = 1.02 × 10⁻⁰⁵).
- *IL6R* mRNA was elevated in winter (P = 9.33 × 10⁻¹²); circulating soluble IL-6R protein was also elevated in winter in BABYDIET/BABYDIAB serum (P = 2.74 × 10⁻¹¹).
- Circulating CRP levels were elevated in winter in the ASCOT cohort (3,412 hypertensive, non-dyslipidemic donors; P = 5.81 × 10⁻⁰⁶).

**Vaccine-relevant immune gene seasonality:**
- *TLR7* (P = 4.22 × 10⁻¹⁶), *TLR8* (P = 3.70 × 10⁻⁰⁹), and *DDX58* (RIG-I, P = 9.65 × 10⁻²⁰) were winter-elevated — innate antiviral sensing genes.
- *TNFRSF17* (plasma cell survival factor, P = 1.30 × 10⁻¹²), *OAS1*, *OAS2*, *STAT2*, *POU2AF1*, and *CD27* were all seasonal; *CD27* was correlated with antibody response to meningococcal vaccine (MCV4).
- Antibody responses to rabies, typhoid, and pneumococcal vaccines were reported to vary by month of administration.

**Adipose tissue seasonality:**
- 4,027 genes were seasonally expressed in subcutaneous adipose tissue of 856 UK women (TwinsUK).
- *IL6ST* (gp130, P = 2.55 × 10⁻⁰⁸) and *IL6R* (P = 1.49 × 10⁻⁰⁸) were among the most seasonal.
- 1,213 genes were common to both adipose and PBMC data sets.
- *PPARG* (thiazolidinedione target for T2D) was seasonal in adipose (P = 3.75 × 10⁻⁰⁹).
- Metabolic pathways were among the most seasonal in both tissues.

**Common seasonal genes across cohorts:**
- 147 genes were common to all five datasets (BABYDIET, T1D, Australia, USA, UK/Ireland), representing a conserved core seasonal transcriptome.
- *ARNTL* was the most common seasonal gene (Fisher's method χ²₁₀, P = 6.73 × 10⁻⁵⁷).
- The strongest common seasonal gene across all data sets (excluding Iceland) was *C14orf159* (winter-expressed, P = 3.93 × 10⁻⁶⁶); *UPF0317* (encoded by *C14orf159*, regulated by oestrogen receptor α) was also highly conserved.

## Limitations

- **Mechanism not established:** The paper demonstrates correlation between temperature/sunlight hours and gene expression (linear regression, Supplementary Figs 2, 4) but does not isolate the causal environmental Zeitgeber. Daylight and temperature are confounded.
- **Cellular composition as proximate cause — but not fully separated:** The authors argue that cell-type composition drives the majority of transcriptomic seasonality, but do not fully disentangle composition changes from within-cell-type transcriptional changes. Purified cell-type-specific studies are recommended as a follow-up.
- **Iceland anomaly unexplained:** The Icelandic cohort showed fewer common seasonal genes and a distinct pattern. The authors attribute this to near-24-hour summer daylight disrupting photoperiodic signaling, but this remains speculative.
- **Sex stratification absent:** The BABYDIET cohort enrolled children; no sex-stratified seasonal analysis is reported despite the seasonal regulation of estrogen receptor genes, leaving the interaction between seasonal and menstrual-cycle immunity uncharacterized.
- **Disease-enriched cohorts:** BABYDIET and T1D cohorts enrolled children at genetic risk for T1D; whether seasonal gene expression in these cohorts generalizes to healthy adults is addressed only partially by the Cambridge BioResource FBC data (cell counts, not transcriptomics) and the TwinsUK adipose data.
- **Age confound in Gambian cohort:** The Gambian cohort included both adults and children (4,200 total); age-specific seasonal patterns were not reported.
- **Vaccine timing data are observational:** The connection between seasonal immune gene expression and vaccine efficacy relies on correlations and cross-study comparisons, not a direct seasonal-randomization trial.
- **No longitudinal within-person data:** All analyses are cross-sectional with respect to individuals (repeated samples across individuals, not tracked individuals over multiple years). Season × individual interaction (some people showing larger vs smaller seasonal amplitude) is not characterized.
