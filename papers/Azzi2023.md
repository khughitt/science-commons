## Key Findings

**1. YAP is a direct transcriptional activator of CRY1.**
- LATS1/2 KO (which releases YAP/TAZ from inhibition) significantly increases CRY1 mRNA and protein; siRNA knockdown of YAP reduces CRY1 in both WT and LATS1/2 KO backgrounds.
- YAP binds the CRY1 proximal promoter (ChIP-qPCR; constitutively active 5SA-YAP shows markedly enhanced signal); this binding is TEAD-dependent, particularly TEAD2.
- CRY1 luciferase reporter activity is ~5-fold induced by WT-YAP overexpression.
- CRY1 ranks among the top YAP target genes in published ChIP-Atlas data from multiple cell lines.
- TAZ also activates CRY1, but with weaker effects than YAP.

**2. TEADs are required for YAP-induced CRY1 transcription.**
- Combined inducible knockdown of TEAD1/3/4 plus siRNA against TEAD2 abolishes CRY1 upregulation.
- Pharmacological TEAD inhibition (MGH-CP1) reduces CRY1 protein under low-density conditions (when YAP/TAZ are normally active).

**3. CRY1 forms a negative feedback loop on the Hippo pathway.**
- CRY1 knockdown (shRNA, HEK293A) increases YAP and TAZ mRNA and protein, decreases MOB1A/B levels, and elevates expression of YAP transcriptional targets (CTGF, CYR61, AREG, THBS1, LAMB3) — both at low and high cell density.
- CRY1 directly occupies the YAP promoter (ChIP-qPCR) and the CRY1 luciferase reporter driven by the YAP promoter is ~1.6-fold elevated in CRY1-depleted cells.
- These effects are partially YAP-dependent: co-knockdown of CRY1 and YAP rescues the elevated target gene expression seen in CRY1-depleted cells.
- In breast cancer line MDA-MB-231, CRY1 knockdown dramatically reduces MOB1A/B protein, increases YAP/TAZ target genes, and reduces YAP Ser127 phosphorylation (a marker of YAP inactivation), indicating active YAP hyperactivation.

**4. CRY1 loss is correlated with YAP overexpression across cancers.**
- GEPIA analysis of TCGA data: significant positive Spearman correlation between YAP and CRY1 RNA levels across all cancers (R = 0.36); specifically in breast cancer (R = 0.5, p = 1.32×10^-35).

**5. YAP/TAZ perturbation alters circadian oscillation.**
- In dexamethasone-synchronized MCF10A cells, constitutively active YAP (S127A) abolishes PER2 oscillation and dramatically elevates CRY1 mRNA throughout the circadian cycle; wild-type YAP overexpression has moderate but significant effects on CRY1, BMAL1, and CRY2.
- In U2OS cells, shRNA knockdown of YAP or TAZ reduces CRY1 transcript levels and dampens PER2 and BMAL1 oscillation amplitude. CRY2 oscillations are also altered.
- TEAD inhibition (MGH-CP1) also disrupts BMAL1 and PER2 oscillation.

**6. CRY1 depletion in breast cancer cells enhances DNA damage.**
- CRY1 knockdown in MDA-MB-231 increases colony formation, activates DNA damage response genes, and enhances DNA damage markers (Figures S5, consistent with YAP/TAZ hyperactivation driving growth and genomic stress).
- Combination of TEAD inhibitor (MGH-CP1) + CRY1 activator (KL001) shows synergistic anti-proliferative effects.

## Limitations

- All experiments are in vitro (cell lines); no in vivo validation in a whole-organism or tissue model of the clock-Hippo crosstalk. The paper explicitly notes this as a limitation.
- The feedback loop asymmetry (YAP has a stronger effect on CRY1 than CRY1 has on YAP) is noted but mechanistically unexplained. CRY1 overexpression moderately reduces YAP/TAZ; it is unclear whether this reflects sub-saturating CRY1 levels, time-of-day dependency, or a genuinely weaker regulatory arm.
- Whether CRY1 represses YAP transcription directly (via its role as a transcriptional repressor binding the YAP promoter, shown by ChIP-qPCR) or indirectly (via MOB1A/B upregulation activating LATS1/2) is not fully disentangled; both mechanisms appear to operate.
- CRY1's role in regulating MOB1A/B is established here at the mRNA and protein level but the transcriptional mechanism (direct CRY1 binding to MOB1A/B promoters, or indirect) is not characterized.
- Results in breast cancer cell lines may reflect cancer-specific regulatory contexts; the generalisation to normal tissues, other cancer types, or in vivo physiology requires further study.
- TCGA correlation data (YAP-CRY1 co-expression) are observational and cannot distinguish direct YAP regulation of CRY1 from confounding co-expression patterns.
- No distinction between free CRY1 and CRY1 within the PER-CRY repressor complex in terms of which pool mediates the Hippo feedback — this is relevant to Q18's concern about assembly-state vs. total protein level.
