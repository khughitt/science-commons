## Key Findings

### Transcriptional and genomic heterogeneity is already high in precursors

- cPCs in MGUS already display ITH comparable in magnitude to NDMMs by BPD metrics, while SMM samples show somewhat lower ITH than MGUS or NDMM — suggesting the very early disease stage and the late expansion stage are both highly heterogeneous, and an intermediate "compression" may occur at SMM (Figure 2B–C; small cohort caveat applies).
- High-risk genomic events such as chromosome 1q gain significantly increase ITH in both HY and non-HY subtypes.
- BCR clonal complexity (top-1 clonotype dominance, intra-sample subcluster diversity) is similar between HY and non-HY, implying genomic subtype does not dictate clonal complexity per se.

### HY and non-HY subtypes are transcriptionally and immunologically distinct throughout progression

- HY cPCs overexpress LAMP5, SULF2, SLAMF1, FAM30A, and ITM2C relative to non-HY. Non-HY cPCs overexpress CCND1, CKS1B, SDF2L1, LY6E, DUSP1, CD99, LIME1, and CTHRC1.
- CytoTRACE score distributions differ: HY samples skew toward higher (less differentiated) scores vs. non-HY, suggesting subtype-specific differentiation trajectories that persist from MGUS through NDMM.
- Non-HY samples show increased CD8+ T cell proportions and a cytotoxic TME; HY samples show a pro-inflammatory monocyte-enriched TME with upregulation of IL1B, CCL3, CXCL8, interferon-induced genes, and NFKB inhibitor genes.
- ICAM1 — an intercellular adhesion molecule involved in NK and myeloid cell recruitment — is differentially elevated in non-HY cPCs throughout disease progression, with expression positively correlated with CytoTRACE (more differentiated cells). Differential cPC–TME ligand-receptor interactions between HY and non-HY are predominantly ICAM-mediated.

### Clonotypic and transcriptional axes evolve semi-independently

- Cells within a single BCR clonotype (i.e., cells sharing identical V(D)J) can harbor divergent CNA profiles and distinct transcriptional subclusters. Conversely, cells with distinct BCR clonotypes can occupy convergent transcriptional states.
- The MGUS01s case study (linear evolution, non-HY) shows polyclonal cells (C0, C1) that are transcriptionally distinct from clonal cells (C2–C4) that share the same dominant BCR clonotype but differ in CNA accumulation and gene expression along pseudotime. This dissociation implies transcriptional state changes can precede or trail BCR clonal sweeps.
- In the MGUS06s case (branching evolution), 9 PC clusters share a common ancestor (C2 is polyclonal root) but diverge into three independent lineages with distinct CNA profiles and expression programs. Branching was detected at MGUS stage, indicating that clonal diversification is not restricted to late-stage disease.

### Linear vs. branching evolution as a stage-associated but not deterministic pattern

- Across the cohort, 14 cases showed linear evolution and 16 showed branching evolution (per Figure S5A labeling; Fisher's exact test p = 0.70 for cytogenetics independence, p = 0.057 for disease stage association).
- Linear evolution predominates at early/low-burden stages (MGUS); branching emerges as tumor expands (SMM, NDMM). This is interpreted as reflecting increased phenotypic adaptability when a larger population is under selection.
- Both patterns are observed at MGUS, indicating that branching is not a stage-specific threshold phenomenon.

### Pseudotime-linked transcriptional programs along differentiation

- Along the pro-B → PC axis, CNA scores and BCR clonal dominance increase progressively, while CytoTRACE score changes are non-monotone and subtype-specific.
- "Terminally" differentiated PC cluster C4 (in MGUS01s linear case) upregulates ASS1, PTP4A3, and PRPSAP2 — genes validated to increase with progression in an independent cohort (MGUS → SMM → MM). ASS1 upregulation is associated with arginine biosynthesis, immune evasion, and resistance to arginine-depletion therapies.
- Pathway enrichment in late-stage clusters (C3/C4 vs. C2) includes hypoxia, apoptosis, glycolysis, PI3K/AKT/mTOR, TGF-beta, TNF/NFkB, and P53 signaling.

### TME remodeling begins in precursors and is genotype-dependent

- Decreased mature B cells, NK cells, and CD14+ monocytes and increased CD8+ T cells are observed along the MGUS → SMM → NDMM axis, consistent with prior bulk studies.
- TME shifts are detectable even at MGUS, implying microenvironmental co-evolution begins before symptomatic disease.
- HY and non-HY samples show divergent TME compositions that are unlikely to be explained by disease stage alone — genotype-specific TME remodeling is proposed as functionally relevant.

## Limitations

- No longitudinal (serial) samples per patient; all comparisons are cross-sectional inferences of dynamics, not direct observation of clonal change over time. Linear vs. branching patterns are pseudotime reconstructions, not measured progression trajectories.
- Small numbers of NDMM (n=7) and RRMM (n=1) samples limit comparison with late-stage and treated disease; linear-to-branching transition statistics are marginal (p=0.057) and require replication in larger cohorts.
- CNA inference from scRNA-seq (inferCNV) is indirect and subject to detection limits; the paper acknowledges improved accuracy over FISH for some events but does not have WGS ground truth for all samples.
- The integrative cPC identification method, while benchmarked, has not been independently validated in external single-cell MM datasets.
- The dissociation of clonotypic and transcriptional axes is interpretively open: the paper cannot distinguish within-clone transcriptional plasticity from selection among transcriptionally distinct micro-subclones with identical BCR sequences.
- TME remodeling results (HY vs. non-HY differences) are hypothesis-generating and explicitly noted as requiring functional validation.
- The study lacks matched treatment exposure data for the vast majority of patients; no experiment tests how therapy shapes the evolutionary patterns observed.
- CytoTRACE scores are calculated per sample and may not be comparable across patients or genetic subtypes without normalization.
