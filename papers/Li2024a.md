## Key Findings

**1. Noncancerous breast tissue has well-organized circadian gene expression.**
CYCLOPS 2.0 ordering of time-stamped Manchester samples achieves good quality metrics (Statsmooth = 0.75, Staterror = 0.015). Core clock gene acrophases in noncancerous tissue closely recapitulate mouse mammary gland timing. Circadian gene expression includes ~2,000 genes; inflammatory, EMT, and estrogen responsiveness pathways show circadian orchestration.

**2. Luminal A tumors have dampened but persistent circadian rhythms — distinctly stronger than other breast cancer subtypes.**
Luminal A tumor Zstat (11.04) is significantly higher than luminal B (P < 0.01) and comparable to a weak but intact clock. Basal/TNBC Zstat (4.98) suggests essentially absent clock organization. All 4 patient-derived luminal A tumor organoids tested showed statistically significant but weakened BMAL1-Luc circadian rhythms; TNBC organoids lacked consistent rhythms.

**3. Tumor rhythms are reprogrammed: EMT and angiogenesis cycling is increased in luminal A versus noncancerous tissue.**
GSEA comparison of amplitude ratios (luminal A vs noncancerous) identifies HALLMARK_EPITHELIAL_MESENCHYMAL_TRANSITION (FDR Q = 0), HALLMARK_E2F_TARGETS (Q = 4e-02), and HALLMARK_ANGIOGENESIS (Q = 9e-02) as increased in luminal A; HALLMARK_TNFA_SIGNALING_VIA_NFKB (Q = 0) and HALLMARK_ADIPOGENESIS (Q = 2e-03) are decreased.

**4. CYCLOPS magnitude (CMag) varies widely among luminal A samples and is a prognostic index.**
CMag distribution in luminal A is broad with a long tail (Fig. 4B). High-CMag (top tertile) tumors show significantly higher 5-year patient mortality compared to the other two tertiles (P = 0.04, ANOVA; confirmed in a logistic regression model controlling for patient age and known metastasis at diagnosis, P < 0.05). The relative risk increase associated with high CMag is approximately 1.5-fold over the baseline established by the other covariates.

**5. High-CMag tumors have markedly increased amplitude of EMT pathway gene cycling.**
PER3, CRY1, DBP, NFIL3, TEF, and TGFBR2 show differential cycling amplitude by CMag tertile (BHq < 0.05). EMT genes MMP2, ITGB1, VIM, ECM2, TWIST1, SERPINE2 and ITGA5, ADAM12 all cycle with higher amplitude in high-CMag samples. EnrichR/GSEA confirms EMT as the pathway with the most pronounced CMag-dependent amplitude change.

**6. Tumor clock phase in high-CMag samples correlates with matched noncancerous tissue phase, suggesting retained entrainment coupling.**
High-CMag luminal A tumor sample phases generally match their noncancerous paired tissue phases; a correlation between tumor and noncancerous sample phases is apparent (Fig. 4E), implying stronger rhythms reflect more coherent, possibly host-entrained clocks. Low-CMag tumor phases are less correlated with their pairs.

**7. Clock disruption reduces cell invasion; clock stabilization reduces invasiveness — causal link established.**
BMAL1 knockdown by shRNA in MCF-7 cells abolished circadian BMAL1-Luc rhythms and inhibited cell invasion into 3D collagen matrix (P < 0.001). CRY1/CRY2 stabilizer KL001 at 1.25 µM also suppressed invasiveness (P < 0.05). BMAL1 knockdown increased Ki-67 abundance (P < 0.0001) and cell proliferation (P < 0.0001), demonstrating that clock loss in luminal A cells compromises invasion despite increasing proliferation.

**8. ERα regulates circadian clock function in luminal A cells.**
ERα knockout in MCF-7 cells disrupted clock gene expression; E2 and ERα-selective PPT synchronized circadian clocks dose-dependently. ER status stratifies breast cancer tumors by clock function — ER-positive samples show stronger clock correlation patterns than ER-negative.

## Limitations

- **Single cancer subtype and institution:** Primary time-stamped biopsies were collected from one UK centre (Nightingale Breast Centre, Manchester); luminal B and TNBC had very small N in the time-stamped cohort. Generalizability to other subtypes, ethnicities, or healthcare systems is unverified.
- **TCGA biopsies have unknown collection time:** Despite CYCLOPS 2.0's batch/covariate correction, the absence of time-of-biopsy metadata for TCGA means the circadian ordering relies entirely on the cross-sectional structure. The degree to which non-circadian biology (e.g., tumor heterogeneity, cell composition) drives the CYCLOPS solution is not fully characterized.
- **Cross-sectional prognosis association (TCGA):** The CMag–survival association is retrospective and cross-sectional. Confounders not captured by PAM50 subtype, age, and metastasis status could contribute. Prospective validation in an independent cohort with independent biopsy timing is explicitly called for by the authors.
- **CMag seed gene list (~70 genes):** CMag depends on CYCLOPS seed genes (human orthologues of broadly cycling mouse transcripts). A smaller gene panel might suffice for clinical application; the authors note this but do not identify a minimal sufficient set.
- **Cell-line model (MCF-7):** MCF-7 is ER+ luminal A but immortalized; the invasion phenotype in 3D collagen matrix may not fully model in vivo tumour-microenvironment dynamics. Primary luminal A cells showed similar trends (SI Appendix) but sample size is small.
- **Mechanism of CMag → poor prognosis directionality:** The authors acknowledge the counterintuitive finding that *high* rhythm strength predicts *worse* survival. Possible interpretations: (i) high-CMag tumors have more rhythmically organized EMT machinery enabling temporally gated invasion; (ii) high-CMag tumors are more responsive to entraining cues (sleep, feeding, light) that promote metastasis; (iii) CMag is a proxy for a correlated aggressive phenotype. The paper cannot distinguish these from TCGA alone.
- **Host-vs-tumor clock contribution:** The paper notes explicitly that CYCLOPS informatics cannot separate cell-autonomous tumor rhythms from rhythms imparted by host circulating signals (hormones, ANS, body temperature). The causal cell-autonomous experiments in MCF-7 cells provide mechanistic support but cannot resolve the in vivo mixture.
- **ER → clock pathway:** ER ablation and exogenous E2/PPT experiments are conducted in cell lines only. Whether ER-mediated clock regulation operates similarly in intact human breast tissue is inferred but not directly tested.
