## Key Findings

### The restructured framework (four dimensions, Figure 2)

1. **Hallmarks (functional capabilities, nine total)** — the nine acquired capabilities are unchanged from 2022: sustaining proliferative signaling, inactivating growth suppressors, resisting programmed cell death, establishing replicative immortality, inducing/accessing vasculature, activating invasion and metastasis, deregulating cellular metabolism, evading immune destruction, and unlocking phenotypic plasticity. KRAS mutated in ~30% of all tumors, MYC amplified/rearranged in ~40%; 193 oncogenes and 143 TSGs catalogued in COSMIC.

2. **Enabling phenotypic characteristics (five, Figure 3)** — loss of genomic integrity, non-mutational epigenetic reprogramming, tumor-promoting inflammation, **innervation** (newly elevated to canonical status), and **polymorphic microbiomes** (newly elevated to canonical status). Hanahan argues both innervation and microbiomes are now supported by sufficient evidence to be added to this tier.

3. **Hallmark-conveying cells of the TME (seven classes, Figure 4)** — cancer cells, endothelial cells and pericytes, CAFs, macrophages and neutrophils, additional hallmark-facilitating immune cells, **neurons and their axonal projections** (newly added), and **senescent cells of multiple origins** (newly added). Senescent cancer cells and accessory cells contribute hallmark capabilities via SASP paracrine signaling; their dual tumor-suppressing and tumor-promoting roles remain incompletely resolved.

4. **Systemic interactions (fourth dimension)** — aging and obesity are elaborated as paradigm cases; aging tissues accumulate latent oncogenic mutations, increase plasticity, and generate a pro-tumorigenic senescent cell burden. Obesity-associated adipokine imbalance (leptin/adiponectin) and immune dysregulation are linked to multiple hallmarks.

### What is new relative to 2011/2022

- **Phenotypic plasticity (2022 addition) elaborated extensively:** intra-lineage plasticity (dedifferentiation, shuttling between differentiated and CSC states) and trans-lineage plasticity (EMP/EMT; conversions to squamous, neuroendocrine, vascular endothelial, or senescent cell phenotypes) are both now documented. TP53 activity is explicitly framed as a constraint on dedifferentiation and lineage plasticity; its loss enables both.
- **Innervation as enabling characteristic:** peri- and intra-tumoral innervation (autonomic sympathetic/parasympathetic and somatosensory) modulates multiple hallmarks; bona fide synaptic connectivity between neurons and cancer cells is documented in glioblastoma, pancreatic adenocarcinoma, and brain-metastatic small cell lung cancer.
- **Polymorphic microbiomes as enabling characteristic:** gut, skin, lung, uterine cervix, urinary tract, and oral cavity microbiomes are all implicated. Species-specific effects on hallmarks (especially immune evasion) are established; fecal transplants from ICI-responding patients can enable ICI response in non-responders. Microbial dysbiosis involving mutagenic bacteria enhances genomic instability.
- **Senescent cells (multiple origins) newly canonicalized as TME cell class:** senescent cancer cells, CAFs, endothelial cells, TAMs, and TANs each contribute SASP-driven hallmark paracrine signaling. Pharmacological senolytic ablation of senescent cells in multiple model systems reduces tumor-promoting function.
- **Four-dimensional framework:** the 2000–2022 two-dimensional (hallmarks + enabling characteristics) view is reorganized into four distinct conceptual dimensions, integrating TME cell diversity and systemic context as formally equal partners.
- **Hallmark co-targeting as therapeutic corollary:** the central translational argument — clinically illustrated by VEGF/VEGFR + ICIs and by bevacizumab + olaparib — is that hitting mechanistically independent hallmarks simultaneously raises the bar for adaptive resistance by requiring the cancer to evolve resistance to multiple independent mechanisms concurrently.

### Evolutionary dynamics emphasis

Hanahan explicitly frames tumor progression as multistep tumorigenesis followed by **adaptive evolution** under selective pressure: hallmark capabilities are acquired sequentially, their temporal order varies by tumor type, oncogene context, and tissue of origin. Plasticity is framed as a primary engine of adaptive evolution and therapy resistance — "non-Darwinian adaptation" through epigenetic state switching enables cancer cells to rapidly adopt evasive phenotypes without mutation. The concept of cancer as an "outlaw organ" operating through multistage tumorigenesis to adaptive evolution is the organizing metaphor.

## Limitations

- Narrative synthesis without systematic literature search criteria — citation selection reflects one author's perspective and may over-represent certain subtypes or mechanisms.
- The four-dimensional framework, while conceptually organizing, remains largely descriptive; it does not provide quantitative predictions about which hallmarks are acquired in which order, or with what probability.
- The co-targeting therapeutic argument is supported by two clinically validated examples and one speculative example (Figure 5C); the generalizability of the logic to other hallmark combinations is asserted rather than demonstrated.
- Evolutionary dynamics are framed qualitatively; no formal population-genetics or game-theoretic modeling is engaged. The boundary between non-Darwinian plasticity and Darwinian clonal selection remains conceptually blurry.
- Senescent cell contributions remain incompletely resolved — the paper acknowledges that the pro- vs. anti-tumorigenic dichotomy "remains a mystery" even for cells from the same tumor type.
- Microbiome effects on cancer hallmarks are described as "partially illuminated" and "remain to be fully illuminated" — prevalence data and functional mechanisms are still predominantly in mouse models.
