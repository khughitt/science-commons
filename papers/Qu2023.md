## Key Findings

### Circadian heterogeneity vs. clock-gene requirement
- Hep3B cells cycle robustly by *Bmal1*-Luc bioluminescence; HepG2 shows high-amplitude but rapidly damping oscillation; Huh7 does not cycle reliably. Despite this heterogeneity, all three lines require BMAL1::CLOCK for sustained proliferation — the oncogenic function is decoupled from oscillator competence.

### Knockdown phenotype
- siRNA targeting *Bmal1* or *Clock* in all three HCC lines caused a marked, dose-progressive reduction in relative cell number over 8 days (p < 0.001 vs. scramble).
- Flow cytometry showed increased Annexin V / PI double-positive cells (apoptosis) and a shift from G1 toward G2/M arrest upon *Bmal1* or *Clock* knockdown.
- shRNA-mediated xenograft knockdown of *Bmal1* or *Clock* strongly reduced tumor weight (Ctrl ~0.8 g vs. *Bmal1* shRNA ~0.1 g vs. *Clock* shRNA ~0.05 g; p < 0.001).

### Transcriptional targets — Wee1 and p21
- *Bmal1* knockdown significantly reduced *Wee1* mRNA in all three lines. BMAL1 ChIP-seq shows direct binding at the *Wee1* gene promoter in both mouse liver and Hep3B cells (E-box).
- *Bmal1*-knockout mouse livers show rhythmic loss of *Wee1* and *Ccnb1* (down-regulated) and gain of *p21* and *Myc* oscillation compared to wild-type.
- *Wee1* knockdown alone phenocopies *Bmal1*/*Clock* knockdown: apoptosis induction and partial G2/M arrest. *Wee1* overexpression partially rescues the growth inhibition caused by *Bmal1*/*Clock* knockdown.
- REV-ERBs (activated by BMAL1::CLOCK) suppress *p21* transcription via the RORE element. *Bmal1* knockdown up-regulates *p21*, and concurrent *p21* knockdown partially rescues growth inhibition.

### Mechanistic model (Fig. 5L)
BMAL1::CLOCK binds E-boxes to drive *Wee1* transcription (anti-apoptotic) and activates REV-ERBs which in turn suppress *p21* via RORE (anti-cell-cycle-arrest). The two arms are divergent and cooperative: *Wee1* controls the apoptosis branch and *p21* the G2/M-arrest branch. G2/M arrest from *Ccnb1* down-regulation is a secondary consequence but does not alone account for the growth inhibition.

### Clinical and therapeutic framing
- WEE1 inhibitors (e.g., AZD1775/MK1775) are already in clinical development; authors suggest these may be particularly relevant for BMAL1::CLOCK-high HCC. *p21* and *Wee1* are proposed as molecular markers for stratifying clock-targeted therapies.

## Limitations

- All in vitro work uses three HCC cell lines; inter-line variability is noted but the mechanistic dissection focuses heavily on Hep3B. Generalizability across HCC subtypes (viral, NAFLD-driven, etc.) is not tested.
- The xenograft model uses immunocompromised (NU/J nude) mice, removing immune surveillance as a variable; clock-immune interaction in HCC is not addressed.
- Circadian transcriptomic data from HCC patient specimens are referenced (citations 29–34) but not reanalysed; the claim that *Bmal1*/*Clock* are "indispensable" is extrapolated from cell lines and one xenograft model.
- The rescue experiments (p21 siRNA + Bmal1 siRNA; Wee1 OX + Bmal1 siRNA) show partial rather than complete restoration of proliferation, consistent with but not proof of the two-arm model.
- Chronotherapy implications (e.g., optimal dosing time for WEE1 inhibitors in HCC) are mentioned speculatively but not tested.
- The study does not examine whether the BMAL1::CLOCK oncogenic function requires interaction with liver-specific pioneer factors (HNF4A mentioned in passing), which could limit translatability across cancer types.
