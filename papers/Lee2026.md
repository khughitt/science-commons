## Key Findings

**ecDNA is tissue-type-specific.** ecDNA-consistent CN distributions were found in GBM, SCLC, and EGFR-mutant NSCLC, but not in any of 72 HGSOC or HR- breast cancer cases despite numerous HLAMP events in those tumour types — the largest and most systematic demonstration of tissue-type specificity of ecDNA to date. Authors interpret this as evidence that ecDNA formation or maintenance may depend on a permissive tissue context.

**ICamp diversifies via two punctuated routes, not gradual drift.** Of 435 ICamp events, 270 (62%) showed multi-peak CN distributions indicative of subclonal diversity. Within-case analysis revealed two principal modulation mechanisms:
- *Numeric modulation* (57% of subclone pairs): arm-level or chromosomal aneuploidy amplifies or reduces CN while preserving the amplicon architecture; most frequent in WGD backgrounds (42% of numeric events).
- *Structural modulation* (35%): BFB cycles, chromothripsis, and complex rearrangements remodel the amplicon structure in a subclone-specific manner.
Both routes produce sizable subclones with distinct oncogene dosage and transcriptional state (demonstrated for CCND1 in HGSOC case OV-052, where subclone C, which had lost CCND1 amplification via aneuploidy, showed a distinct expression program including upregulated interferon-alpha response and antigen-presentation machinery).

**ecDNA supports both branched and convergent evolution.** In 5/11 ecDNA cases, dominant parental ecDNA species coexisted with lower-frequency variant forms (daughter ecDNAs derived by internal rearrangements), consistent with ongoing branched evolution. In contrast, GBM case GBM0510 showed convergent evolution: EGFR amplification was independently acquired on three structurally distinct ecDNA species (e1-e3) in mutually exclusive subclones, all sharing most SNVs — indicating late, parallel independent ecDNA formation at the same locus.

**ecDNA species can carry multiple co-amplified oncogenes.** In EGFR-mutant NSCLC case EL001, ECADeMix identified chimeric ecDNA species carrying EGFR, CCND1, CDK4, and MDM2 simultaneously via chromothripsis-linked segments. In GBM0721, MDM4 and EGFR resided on separate ecDNA species (co-selected but not co-located); cells with high expression of both showed high proliferative features (mitotic spindle, Ki-67), while cells with low expression of both showed elevated ROS signalling.

**Single-cell ecDNA detection substantially corrects bulk ecDNA calls.** At the case level, 9/11 scWGS-identified ecDNA cases were also called ecDNA-positive by Amplicon Architect (81% concordance). However, AA called 30/82 ecDNA-negative cases (37%) as ecDNA-positive — predominantly in HGSOC and HR- breast cancer where ICamp CN distributions mimic cyclic bulk genome-graph patterns. Overall case-level kappa = 0.22, indicating poor agreement. Single-cell CN distributions can also distinguish actively maintained ecDNA from historical ecDNA footprints chromosomally re-integrated as HSRs.

**Quantitative scale.** 507 HLAMP regions identified; mean CN standard deviation 43.1 for ecDNA vs. 3.32 for ICamp (vs. 0.575 for stable control loci TP53/RB1). Chromothripsis-initiated ICamps showed modestly but significantly greater variance than other ICamp initiators (SD 3.65 vs. 3.14; p=0.0253).

## Limitations

- **Clinical tumour coverage is narrow.** Four cancer types; 93 patients (heavily weighted toward HGSOC n=61). No colorectal, prostate, neuroblastoma, hepatocellular carcinoma — cancer types with known ecDNA prevalence in the literature. Generalisability of the tissue-type specificity finding is not yet established.
- **Pre-treatment bias.** Most clinical tumours collected prior to systemic therapy, but PDXs and some cases were post-chemotherapy. Treatment effects on ecDNA prevalence cannot be cleanly separated from intrinsic tissue-type effects.
- **Minimum cell count threshold.** At least 30 high-quality single-cell CN profiles required for eicicle ecDNA detection. Low-cellularity samples or rare ecDNA events in low-frequency subclones may be missed; authors acknowledge this as a limitation.
- **No longitudinal sampling.** Evolutionary trajectories are inferred from static multi-region single-cell snapshots. Timing of ICamp initiation vs. modulation events, and the tempo of ecDNA acquisition, are inferred indirectly from phylogenetic reconstruction, not observed directly.
- **Bulk AA discordance is a challenge, not a full validation.** The kappa of 0.22 shows substantial disagreement between scWGS and AA, but the ground truth in clinical samples without FISH cannot be completely established; some AA positives that scWGS calls ICamp may represent very low-frequency ecDNA below scWGS detection threshold.
- **ecDNA fitness contribution.** The paper establishes that ecDNA and ICamp generate distinct ITH patterns and have transcriptional consequences, but quantitative fitness coefficients for ecDNA vs. ICamp are not estimated.
- **Preprint status.** Not yet peer-reviewed as of the date of this summary.
