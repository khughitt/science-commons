## Key Findings

### Seasonal patterns

- **Neutrophil counts:** Highest in winter (peak January; mesor 4.10 × 10^9/L, amplitude 0.80 × 10^9/L, p < 0.001), lowest in summer (trough July). Significantly and negatively associated with day length in all three regression models (Model 3 coefficient −0.014 [95% CI −0.018, −0.011] per hour of day length).
- **Lymphocyte counts:** Peaked in spring (acrophase April; mesor 1.93 × 10^9/L, amplitude 0.03 × 10^9/L, p < 0.001), troughed in autumn (October). Positively associated with day length in all three models (Model 3 coefficient +0.004 [95% CI 0.003, 0.005]).
- **CRP:** Peaked in winter (acrophase January; mesor 2.23 mg/L, amplitude 0.10 mg/L, p < 0.001), lowest in summer (trough June). Significantly and negatively associated with day length in all three models (Model 3 coefficient −0.004 [95% CI −0.007, −0.002]).
- **Monocytes:** No significant seasonal pattern in the fully adjusted model (Model 3 coefficient not significant).
- **Antibody titres:** No seasonal or diurnal variation detected in seropositivity or antibody titres to any of the 20 antigens tested.
- **Independence from vitamin D:** The seasonal associations with day length persisted after adjusting for serum vitamin D, indicating that circulating 25-OH-D does not mediate the photoperiod effect on CRP, neutrophils, or lymphocytes in this dataset.

### Diurnal patterns

- All WBC and CRP markers showed significant daytime variation (p < 0.001 for all, except CRP morning segment before breakpoint).
- **WBC total:** Monotonically increasing across the day, breakpoint at 14:34; counts lowest at 9am (~5.9 × 10^9/L) and highest at 19h (~7.25 × 10^9/L).
- **Neutrophils:** Increased from morning, reached plateau after breakpoint at ~14:37; range ~3.64 (9am) to ~4.34 (15h) × 10^9/L.
- **Monocytes:** Increased until breakpoint at ~13:16, then decreased across the afternoon; range ~0.42 to ~0.51 × 10^9/L.
- **Lymphocytes:** Monotonically increasing throughout the observation window (9am–7pm); range ~1.65 (9am) to ~2.26 (19h) × 10^9/L. Breakpoint at 16:07.
- **CRP:** Peaked around midday (breakpoint at ~12:43, afternoon decline); range ~2.12 to ~2.31 mg/L.
- **Adjustment robustness:** Daytime changes in WBCs and CRP were in most cases independent of lifestyle and environmental factors in the fully adjusted model. The only exception was the morning ascending segment of CRP, which did not retain significance after adjustment.

### Notable null finding

No seasonal or diurnal variation was found in antibody titres to any of 20 common infectious antigens, despite adequate power. Cross-sectional single-sample design precludes detecting within-individual variation in antibody response after vaccination or infection; this null finding does not rule out functional variation in vaccination efficacy.

## Limitations

1. **Cross-sectional design:** A single blood draw per participant. Within-person variation cannot be separated from between-person confounders; individual chronotypes and seasonal phase could produce artefactual population-level patterns if they are non-randomly distributed across the calendar.
2. **Daytime sampling only (9am–7pm):** No nighttime samples. The study cannot establish full 24-hour rhythms or confirm circadian (vs. ultradian or masking) mechanisms. Described as "daytime variation" throughout — the term "circadian" is used interpretively, not proven.
3. **Healthy-volunteer selection bias:** Restricted to UK Biobank participants who self-reported good health; 5.5% response rate. Cohort is 91–94% White, aged 37–73 years; findings may not generalize to other populations, ethnicities, or age groups (children, elderly, non-Western).
4. **Self-reported covariates:** Physical activity, sleep duration, chronotype, ethnicity, and shiftwork were self-reported; misclassification errors possible.
5. **Antibody null finding limitations:** Cross-sectional single-sample design cannot detect within-individual temporal variation in antibody response kinetics (which requires longitudinal sampling post-vaccination/infection). Serostatus is a binary/continuous titer marker of past exposure, not functional immune response.
6. **Effect sizes are small at individual level:** Authors note the seasonal and diurnal effects are likely to be of clinical significance at the population level for disease transmission, but not for individual patients. The seasonal CRP amplitude of 0.10 mg/L is well below clinical decision thresholds.
7. **Seasonally invariant vitamin D association with CRP:** The interaction between vitamin D and BMI in the CRP model, and between vitamin D and sex in WBC models, introduces complexity in interpreting the full pathway.
