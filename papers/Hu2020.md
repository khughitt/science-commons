## Key Findings

### Timing of systemic spread

- Across all three cancer types, metastatic seeding is estimated to precede clinical diagnosis by years [UNVERIFIED — exact medians confirmed from PMC-available text]:
  - **Colorectal cancer:** ~4.1 years before diagnosis (IQR 3.2–4.6) [UNVERIFIED exact IQR]
  - **Lung cancer:** ~3.6 years before diagnosis (IQR 2.8–3.7) [UNVERIFIED exact IQR]
  - **Breast cancer:** ~2.7 years before diagnosis (IQR 1.1–3.5) [UNVERIFIED exact IQR]
- Breast cancer seeds later (closer to detection) than CRC and lung, consistent with its generally slower growth kinetics.
- These estimates imply that at the time of clinical diagnosis in CRC and lung, the metastatic clone has been expanding for several years — i.e., the window for presymptomatic systemic intervention may be narrow.

### Clonality of seeding

- **Monoclonal seeding:** ~76% of metastases (n=151 of the metastasis set) [UNVERIFIED exact count; fraction confirmed from PMC-available source text]
- **Polyclonal seeding:** ~24% of metastases overall
- In untreated distant metastases, polyclonal seeding was observed in ~29% (20/70) [UNVERIFIED exact]
- In treated metastases, polyclonal seeding was reduced to ~10% [UNVERIFIED exact], suggesting that treatment selects for or reveals a single dominant resistant subclone — consistent with a treatment-imposed competitive bottleneck.

### Treatment-driven divergence in metastases

- Treated metastases harbour private "driver" mutations at high rates: 71%, 75%, and 53% in CRC, lung, and breast, respectively [UNVERIFIED exact figures]
- Untreated metastases show substantially lower rates of private drivers: ~19%, 22%, and 22% [UNVERIFIED exact figures]
- This pattern indicates that treatment selects for pre-existing resistant subclones that diverge from the primary clonal architecture after seeding, not that treatment induces new drivers de novo.

### Cross-cancer comparison

- CRC seeds earliest; breast seeds latest; lung is intermediate.
- The pattern is broadly consistent with cancer-type-specific growth rates and progression timelines.
- Despite differences in absolute timing, the qualitative architecture (early seeding, monoclonal predominance, treatment selection) is shared across all three cancer types, suggesting the dissemination process is governed by common evolutionary constraints rather than cancer-type-specific mechanisms.

## Limitations

1. **Single-biopsy assumption:** The timing estimator uses a single metastatic biopsy and a single primary biopsy per patient. As AlBakir2023 demonstrated in NSCLC, single-region primary sampling can misclassify late-diverging cases as early in ~83% of cases. Hu2020's early-seeding estimates in CRC and breast should be interpreted with this sampling caveat in mind — they may overestimate the prevalence of true early seeding.

2. **Gompertzian growth model dependency:** The absolute divergence times (e.g., 4.1 years for CRC) are conditional on the Gompertzian growth model being correct and on assumed tumour growth parameters. Deviations from Gompertzian kinetics (e.g., punctuated growth bursts, dormancy periods) would shift the estimates. [UNVERIFIED — whether sensitivity analyses for non-Gompertzian kinetics were reported]

3. **WES depth and clonality resolution:** WES-based CCF estimation has limited resolution for detecting minor subclones at low frequency. Polyclonal seeding rates (24%) are likely an underestimate because co-seeding clones at low CCF will be missed — consistent with the observation that more deeply sampled cohorts tend to detect more polyclonal cases.

4. **Treatment confounding of clonality:** The treatment-polyclonality association is observational. Treated metastases may have fewer polyclonal detections because treatment eliminates minor co-seeding clones, because treated patients have different disease characteristics, or because sampling of treated metastases captures post-selection snapshots. Causal direction is not established.

5. **Three cancer types only:** CRC, lung, and breast are among the most common solid cancers but are not representative of all tumour types. Whether early-seeding timing is universal (e.g., in pancreatic, ovarian, or hematologic contexts) is not addressed.

6. **No immune context:** WES-based inference captures the genomic evolution of cancer cells but not the immune microenvironment at primary or metastatic sites. Whether early-seeding clones evade immune surveillance differently from late-seeders is not tested.
