## Key Findings

- Under baseline conditions, SK-N-BE(2) cells are dominated by the ADRN phenotype.
- Cisplatin treatment increases the MES fraction; drug holiday restores ADRN dominance; a second cisplatin cycle again increases the MES fraction.
- Adding background ADRN/MES transition rates does not materially improve fit.
- The cell-state model with treatment-induced transitions fits worse than the cell-type model.
- The authors interpret the dynamics as frequency-dependent interactions among distinct cell types rather than rapid phenotypic switching.
- They suggest neuroblastoma may be a candidate for adaptive therapy if treatment can be paused before ADRN competitors are eliminated and restarted when ADRN frequency recovers.

## Limitations

The study uses one neuroblastoma cell line in vitro.
It does not test in vivo dynamics, additional cell lines, evolving resistance within ADRN cells, or longer repeated treatment schedules.
CD44 is a proxy marker, so conclusions depend on how well CD44 tracks ADRN/MES state in the tested context.
