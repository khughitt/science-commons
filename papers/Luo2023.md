## Key Findings

### Simulation benchmarks

- TreeMHN consistently and substantially outperforms genotype MHN in both precision and recall for recovering the true off-diagonal entries of Θ across all tested configurations: n ∈ {10, 15, 20} mutations and N ∈ {100, 200, 300, 500} trees.
- For trajectory probability estimation (KL divergence from ground truth), TreeMHN outperforms genotype MHN, REVOLVER, and HINTRA across all n and N tested; HINTRA performs worst.
- The advantage over genotype MHN is explained by the ordering information in the trees resolving network identifiability problems: multiple MHNs can share the same binary-genotype likelihood, but fewer share the same tree likelihood.
- Performance improves with N and is robust to phylogenetic uncertainty (tested by adding noise to trees).

### AML results

- DNMT3A has the highest baseline fixation rate among the 31 genes, followed by IDH2, FLT3, NRAS, NPM1, TET2 — consistent with their known frequency in AML.
- Key clonal exclusivity: FLT3 strongly inhibits NRAS fixation in the same lineage (θ_{NRAS,FLT3} strongly negative). Directionality is asymmetric: subclones with FLT3 are much less likely to subsequently accumulate NRAS than vice versa. GeneAccord had identified NRAS and FLT3 as clonally exclusive but could not provide directional strengths.
- Also detected: PTPN11 and IDH2 clonal exclusivity; DNMT3A → NPM1 → NRAS three-way co-occurrence associated with poor prognosis.
- Most probable trajectory: DNMT3A alone (8.6%), followed by NRAS alone (7.55%), FLT3 alone (7.21%), reflecting their high baseline rates.
- Ordering DNMT3A → NPM1 is more probable than NPM1 → DNMT3A, consistent with prior reports.
- Next-event prediction: for 7 of 9 new events in consecutive longitudinal samples, TreeMHN assigns higher probability than 94% of alternative events; median percentile rank ~97% vs. baselines ranging from 63–97%.

### TRACERx NSCLC results

- Detects stable interdependency signals among 18 mutations after stability selection (Supplementary Fig. S19).
- KRAS, TP53, and EGFR effects on other genes dominate, consistent with their roles as early/initiating events.
- Exclusive relationship between oncogenic drivers KRAS and EGFR confirmed and directional.
- EGFR-mutant tumors with co-occurring TP53 have higher genomic instability and shorter progression-free survival after EGFR TKI therapy (confirmed by external clinical evidence).
- Co-mutations in KRAS/TP53 predictive of favorable PD-1 inhibitor response.

### Breast cancer results

- Combined network across all subgroups identifies TP53 and PIK3CA as early clonal events with high baseline rates and co-occurrence.
- PIK3CA and PIK3R1 are mutually exclusive, suggesting sufficiency of PI3K activation via either driver.
- Subgroup-specific networks (e.g., NF1 associations in hormone receptor-negative tumors; PIK3CA + GATA3 co-occurrence restricted to HR+ tumors) provide novel clinical-context-specific insights.
- In metastatic triple-negative subgroup, combination of TP53, PTEN, and PIK3CA/PIK3R1 ranks higher — consistent with known PI3K-AKT pathway hyperactivation driving proliferation and reduced survival.
- Most probable trajectories confirm CDH1 → PIK3CA for HR−/HER2− samples (also found by HINTRA and RECAP), and add new co-occurrence and ordering signals.

### Prediction performance (AML, retrospective)

- Evaluated on 1370 pairs of rooted subtrees and their downstream events from the 123 primary trees.
- TreeMHN median percentile rank: ~97th percentile; significantly better than genotype MHN (baseline: ~71st), genotype MHN (consensus: ~81st), genotype MHN (weighted: ~94th), REVOLVER (~94th), and frequency-based baseline (~90th) (all Bonferroni-corrected Wilcoxon signed-rank tests significant at p < 10^-80).

## Limitations

- **No back-mutations or clonal loss:** TreeMHN assumes all mutations in a tree are monotonically acquired and never lost. It cannot model subclones that were present earlier but absent at sampling, which is the core concern of h008 (oscillatory clonal dynamics). The inferred trajectories reflect only the observable tree structure at one time point.
- **Unknown sampling time:** Expected waiting times are relative to an unknown sampling rate (λ_s). Absolute timing of events is not recoverable without longitudinal data specifying T_s. The Discussion notes this explicitly and flags longitudinal data as the remedy.
- **No subclone size modeling:** Mutation rates in the model are rates of fixation in subclones, not weighted by subclone fitness or size. Larger subclones contribute more to observed mutations but receive no additional weight. The authors note that jointly inferring mutation rates and fitness values may be challenging but has promise.
- **Infinite-sites assumption (ISA) by default, but relaxed at gene level:** TreeMHN formally assumes each mutation occurs at most once per lineage. In the AML application, the authors work around this by summarizing at the gene level (same gene = same mutation), allowing the same gene to appear in independently evolved subclones (parallel mutations). This is biologically more realistic but discards allele-level information.
- **Observation / ascertainment bias:** Like all cancer progression models applied to cross-sectional clinical cohorts, TreeMHN is exposed to the observation-promotion bias flagged in the h006 discussion: driver mutations that trigger clinical emergence (e.g., TP53 in NSCLC, EGFR) can appear to have high baseline rates partly because their presence motivates patient diagnosis and sequencing. The oMHN approach (Schill2023) is cited in the Discussion as an important extension but is not implemented.
- **No epigenetic or expression events:** The model operates on somatic mutation trees only. Non-mutational adaptation (phenotypic plasticity, epigenetic reprogramming — central to h005, h011) is entirely outside the scope; the method cannot distinguish selection-driven convergence from plasticity-driven convergence at the epigenetic level.
- **Computational scaling:** The parameter space is O(n²) in the number of mutations; with stability selection over a regularization grid, computation can be slow for large n. The tree-level complexity is bounded by the maximum number of subtrees in any single tree (not the total number of mutations), which is typically manageable but can be large for highly branching single-cell tree cohorts.
- **Phylogenetic method dependence:** TreeMHN takes tumor trees as given input and does not model tree uncertainty. The quality of inference depends on the upstream phylogenetic method (SCITE, SPRUCE, etc.) and is sensitive to systematic errors in tree reconstruction.
