## Key Findings

**LSC operational definition.**
LSCs are operationally defined by three criteria: (1) functional — unlimited self-renewal and multi-directional differentiation potential demonstrated by serial transplantation; (2) phenotypic — surface markers CD123, CD33, CD47, CD44, and CXCR4 (used clinically to select trial populations); (3) positional — residence in a quiescent (G₀) state within the BMM niche. The paper acknowledges that LSCs are not functionally homogeneous: Hope et al. data show hierarchically distinct LSC classes differing in renewal capacity, and Cozzio et al. show LSCs can derive from HSCs and from more mature progenitors (granulocytic/monocytic-restricted), making cell-of-origin a spectrum rather than a fixed identity.

**Origins and AML two-hit model.**
LSC genesis requires co-occurring Type I mutations (kinase signalling: FLT3-ITD, c-KIT, BCR-ABL) and Type II mutations (transcription factor disruption: AML1/ETO, PML/RARα, MLL fusions). Epigenetic mutations (DNMT3A 12–35%, IDH1/2 ~20%, TET2 ~10%, ASXL1 ~20%, EZH2 4%, HDAC 2%) tend to arise early and occur simultaneously, while signalling-pathway mutations arise later and are found in distinct subclones — consistent with stepwise clonal diversification rather than a single transformation event.

**Clonal evolution driven by the BMM.**
The paper frames AML as a continuous, multi-step clonal evolutionary process. Key points:
- Preleukemic mutations (e.g., in CD34+ progenitors) survive induction chemotherapy, confirming these cells as a reservoir for relapse — supporting the clonal evolution model over de novo transformation at relapse.
- Corces-Zimmerman et al. identified four patterns of clonal evolution to AML relapse: (1) treatment-refractory primary disease; (2) further evolution of a dominant clone; (3) outgrowth of a subclone present at diagnosis; (4) evolution from a preleukemic HSC.
- ROS generated by OXPHOS metabolism in LSCs (vs. glycolysis in normal HSCs) is proposed as the primary mutagenic driver of genomic instability and clonal diversification. NOX-family oxidases, FLT3-ITD, BCR-ABL1, and chronic inflammation all feed this ROS elevation.
- The BMM provides both selective pressure (oxidative stress, hypoxia) and protective buffering (CXCL12/CXCR4 retention, VLA4–fibronectin adhesion, Notch/Jagged signalling, BCL-2 upregulation) — creating an evolutionary ratchet where LSCs that survive chemotherapy do so in a niche-protected quiescent state.

**Niche–LSC interaction mechanisms (measured by co-culture, PDX, GEMM):**
- CXCL12/CXCR4 axis: PI3K/AKT/mTOR survival signalling; CXCR4 expression correlates with oxygen partial pressure.
- VLA4–fibronectin from MSCs: activates PI3K/AKT/BCL-2, conferring cytotoxic drug resistance.
- E-selectin–CD44 (endothelium): activates Wnt and NF-κB.
- Notch/Jagged: quiescence maintenance.
- HIF-1α (hypoxia): promotes angiogenesis, CXCR4 upregulation, survival.
- Exosome-mediated crosstalk: LSC-derived exosomes suppress DKK1 and SCF/CXCL12 in BMSCs, remodelling the niche to favour LSC retention over normal haematopoiesis.
- LSCs co-opt adipocyte lipolysis (fatty acid oxidation, FAO) as an alternative metabolic fuel, especially in the adipocyte-rich BMM.
- AML cells activate JAK/STAT signalling in both blasts and BMSCs via inflammatory cytokines, amplifying ROS and proliferation.

**LSC-as-state vs. LSC-as-fixed-population.**
The review does not explicitly frame this as a state/population debate, but its evidence implicitly supports a state framing in several ways: (1) LSCs can derive from multiple progenitor levels (HSC, CMP, GMP), so "LSC" is not a fixed lineage position; (2) metabolic adaptation (FAO substituting OXPHOS when Bcl-2 inhibitors are applied) suggests LSCs escape by phenotypic adaptation rather than pre-existing clonal selection alone; (3) the "niche protection → quiescence → chemoresistance" loop implies that cells entering or leaving the niche transit between drug-sensitive and drug-resistant states. However, the review does not cite lineage-tracing or scRNA-seq evidence that would directly test whether LSC marker states are reversible in AML. The framing remains predominantly hierarchical-fixed rather than explicitly plastic.

**Therapeutic targeting landscape (Table 2).**
Three strategic axes: (1) surface antigens (CD123 — IMGN632/Tagraxofusp/Flotetuzumab; CD33 — Lintuzumab; CD47 — Magrolimab; CD123×CD3/CD33×CD3 bispecifics); (2) mutant-gene products (FLT3 — Gilteritinib Phase 1, Crenolanib Phase 3, Quizartinib Phase 1 paediatric; DNMT3A — Azacitidine/Decitabine; IDH1/2 — Ivosidenib/Enasidenib; HDAC — Vorinostat; ASXL1/EZH2 implied by epigenetic targets); (3) intracellular signalling (NF-κB — Bortezomib; Hedgehog — Glasdegib; Wnt — Daunorubicin). ROS/metabolic targets include NOX inhibitors (Setanaxib/GKT137831), CAR IX/XII inhibition (FC531), FAO inhibitor avocatin-B, and NAMPT inhibitors. CXCR4 antagonists (Plerixafor, BL-8040, LY2510924) are in trials as chemosensitisers by dislodging LSCs from protective niches.

## Limitations

- Entirely narrative review; no original data, no meta-analysis, no systematic evidence grading.
- Mechanistic evidence is predominantly from in vitro co-cultures and mouse models; human in vivo validation of most niche-interaction claims is absent or minimal.
- The paper does not distinguish between LSC-as-state and LSC-as-fixed-population; the question of whether LSC marker expression is reversible is not addressed.
- Clonal heterogeneity within the LSC compartment (hierarchical LSC classes with different renewal capacities) is acknowledged but not developed — the review treats LSC as a relatively unified entity for therapeutic targeting purposes.
- The ROS-as-mutagen → clonal evolution mechanism is described as an important driver but the quantitative contribution relative to replication errors or exogenous mutagens is not assessed.
- Drug resistance patterns in Tables 2 represent trials ongoing at publication (2022); outcomes data are not yet available for most Phase 1/2 entries.
- The review does not engage with single-cell transcriptomic data on LSC state heterogeneity (e.g., Velten et al. type studies), limiting the plasticity framing.
