## Key Findings

### Discriminating measures

| Measure | CSN-EG (r) | CSN-GO (r) |
|---|---|---|
| R | 0.31 | 0.18 |
| R_l | 0.28 | 0.17 |
| p_c^r | −0.11 | **−0.56** |
| p_c^t | 0.34 | 0.36 |
| a(G) | 0.049 | 0.21 |
| λ̄ (natural connectivity) | 0.24 | **−0.60** |

- In CSN-EG (protein-inclusive networks), no measure achieves a strong correlation with survival; all |r| ≤ 0.34.
- In CSN-GO (gene-only networks), p_c^r (r = −0.56) and λ̄ (r = −0.60) are strongly negatively correlated with 5-year survival — more robust signaling network structure associates with worse patient outcomes.
- The authors favour λ̄ over p_c^r because p_c^r assumes uniform random attack probability, which is biologically unrealistic; targeted-attack or hub-centric perturbations better model therapeutic interventions.
- Natural connectivity (λ̄) matched degree-entropy H (r = −0.62, replicating Breitkreutz et al. 2012) and outperformed modularity Q (r = −0.21) from Takemoto & Kihara 2013.

### Why CSN-EG fails and CSN-GO succeeds

The inclusion of renewable/replaceable proteins and small molecules in CSN-EG adds nodes that dilute the signal and inflate apparent robustness in ways not reflective of targetable network structure. Proteins can be regenerated from intact genes, so disrupting them is not necessarily lethal to the CSN; CSN-GO captures the more durable gene-level backbone.

### Node importance characterisation

Top-3 natural-connectivity-impactful nodes across all 14 CSN-GOs consistently have degree, closeness centrality, and betweenness centrality substantially above their network means (e.g., for prostate cancer top node: degree 12 vs. mean 2.29; betweenness 850.67 vs. mean 156.29; for AML top node: betweenness 545.00 vs. mean 98.00). This confirms that λ̄-identified nodes are also topologically central by conventional measures.

## Limitations

- 14 cancer types is a very small N for correlation analysis; the Pearson correlations are not corrected for multiple comparisons across six measures and two network types.
- KEGG cancer pathway networks are curated literature-derived topologies, not tumour-specific networks inferred from patient omics data; they reflect community consensus signaling models, not actual per-tumour pathway activity.
- CSN-GO and CSN-EG are undirected and unweighted; directed and edge-weighted representations could substantially change robustness estimates.
- The SEER 5-year survival data are population-level and pre-date the immunotherapy era; survival probabilities for many cancers (melanoma, NSCLC, AML) have shifted substantially since the data used here.
- The "top 3 impactful nodes" are identified but not named or biologically annotated in the paper — the connection to specific druggable targets is asserted but not demonstrated.
- The analysis is cross-sectional across cancer types; it cannot distinguish whether pathway robustness causes poor outcomes or is a correlated feature of other tumour biology.
