## Key Findings

### Circadian rhythms of cardiovascular non-hematopoietic cells

- **Endothelial cells (ECs):** VEGFA/VEGFR2 and VEGFR1 oscillate antiphase (VEGFA and KDR peak at dawn; FLT1 at night). CLOCK drives ICAM-1 transcription directly via E-box binding; serum-shock induces oscillatory ICAM-1 in cultured ECs. BMAL1 cKO in ECs (Cdh5-lineage) abrogates time-of-day differences in ICAM-1 and VCAM-1 in liver and lung and abolishes rhythmic homing of CD45+ leukocytes to tissues.
- **Smooth muscle cells (SMCs):** SMC-specific Bmal1 deletion (*SM22α-Cre*) eliminates blood pressure rhythm in constant darkness. In vivo, vascular SMC-specific deletion aggravates atherosclerosis (≈2-fold) and enhances SMC migration and apoptosis.
- **Cardiomyocytes:** Daily rhythms of heart electrical activity are controlled by circadian inputs. *Bmal1*-deficient human iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes show ≈2-fold reduced contractility, calcium dysregulation, and disarrayed myofilaments. Cardiomyocyte death is under circadian control, relevant to MI severity.

### Circadian rhythms of immune cells

- Blood leukocyte counts peak at the behavioral rest phase (night in humans; midday in nocturnal rodents). Oscillation amplitudes: neutrophils 1.5–2-fold, lymphocytes ≈1.5–2-fold, eosinophils ≈1.5–2-fold, monocytes ≈1.3–2-fold (human data); 2–7-fold in mice.
- **Macrophages:** Phagocytic activity peaks at ZT12 in young mice; Bmal1-deficient macrophages have enhanced phagocytic capacity. BMAL1 suppresses IL-1β via NRF2; REV-ERBα binds the NLRP3 promoter to inhibit inflammasome activity.
- **Monocytes:** Both inflammatory (CD115+Ly6Chigh) and non-inflammatory (CD115+Ly6Clow) subsets oscillate in blood, peaking at behavioral rest. Myeloid-specific Bmal1 deletion disrupts Ly6Chigh oscillations and affects systemic metabolism.
- **Neutrophils:** Peak at midday in mice (ZT5 for young; ZT13 for aged). Rhythmic neutrophil aging is regulated by BMAL1. Aged neutrophils peak in human evening blood (≈2-fold increase around 7 PM).
- **Dendritic cells (DCs):** CRY1/2 deletion in bone marrow DCs enhances maturation markers (CD86, MHCII) and proinflammatory cytokines. CD80 is under direct BMAL1 control; BMAL1 governs circadian T-cell responses in melanoma via CD80.
- **T and B cells:** T-cell receptor mRNAs and T-cell activation positive regulators peak during the day; negative regulators at night. Vaccination with OVA-loaded DCs at ZT6 generates significantly more IFN-γ–producing effector T cells vs. ZT18 (1.5–5-fold). B cell TLR9 expression oscillates (peak ZT19), linking circadian variation to humoral response amplitude.
- **HSPCs:** Noradrenaline peaks in bone marrow at night, driving G2/M and S phase cell cycle entry and HSPC proliferation and migration.

### Steady-state leukocyte–vascular circadian interactions

- Leukocyte adhesion to arteries peaks in the morning (≈1.5-fold) and to veins at night (≈4-fold) in mice.
- Lineage-specific Bmal1 ablation in ECs abolishes time-of-day ICAM-1/VCAM-1 expression; ablation in B cells (Cd19-Cre), myeloid cells (Lyz2-Cre), or T cells (Cd4-Cre) each disrupts subset-specific rhythmic homing to lymph nodes and spleen.
- In lymphatics, DC migration to skin afferent lymphatics peaks at ZT7 (≈3-fold). This requires CCL21, LYVE-1, CD99, and JAM-A on lymphatic ECs plus CCR7 on DCs; BMAL1 in lymphatic ECs or conventional DCs drives CCL21 and CCR7 expression.
- BMAL1 in ECs of draining lymph nodes controls ICAM-1 in high endothelial venules; TNF in the LN upregulates BMAL1-controlled ICAM-1, coupling inflammation to rhythmic lymphocyte infiltration (Figure 1B). Rhythmic lymphocyte egress from LNs is governed by S1pr1 via circadian oscillations peaking ZT5–7.

### Atherosclerosis

- Night shift workers have increased carotid intima-media thickness and 1.5–2× higher likelihood of carotid plaques vs. non-shift workers. Mouse models with weekly 6-hour light-phase advances or delays, or 12-hour shifts for 15 weeks, develop increased atherosclerotic lesion size and severe fibrous-cap lesions (up to 2-fold) vs. regular LD mice.
- Monocytes and neutrophils adhere to carotid artery in oscillatory fashion in early-stage Apoe−/− and Ldlr−/− mice, peaking at ZT1 (trough ZT13, 1.5–3-fold). CCL2 levels are higher on arteries at ZT1 (≈1.6-fold); CCR2/CCR5 expression on monocytes is higher at ZT1 (≈1.4-fold).
- Myeloid Bmal1 deletion has opposing effects depending on background: in Apoe−/− mice it worsens atherosclerosis and macrophage content; in Ldlr−/− mice it reduces lesion burden ≈2-fold and shifts macrophage polarization toward CD206+ anti-inflammatory. Mechanistic resolution likely requires inducible models to avoid developmental confounds.
- Clock dominant-negative (ClockΔ19) combined with Apoe−/− enhances atherosclerosis (1.6–4-fold) and impairs cholesterol efflux via ABCA1/ABCG1 in macrophages through USF2-dependent regulation.
- Rora−/− mice develop severe atherosclerosis (6–7.5-fold) with plaque rupture and inflammatory macrophage infiltration; RORα/γ inhibition reduces lesions ≈2-fold and promotes Treg/Th2 while suppressing Th17.
- **Chronotherapy proof-of-concept:** CCR2 antagonist RS102895 administered to atherosclerotic Apoe−/− mice 8 hours before peak monocyte adhesion (ZT1) significantly suppressed ≈2-fold atherosclerotic lesion formation while leaving venular immune surveillance intact. Daily intraperitoneal SR9009 (REV-ERBα agonist) reduced atherosclerotic plaque size by 23%.

### Myocardial Infarction

- MI, sudden cardiac death, and stroke each occur most frequently in the morning in humans, indicating a circadian pattern.
- In mice, MI induced at the sleep–wake transition (ZT13) is more severe than at wake–sleep transition (ZT5). MI size is 78% larger at ZT10–12 vs. ZT0–2 in phase-shifted mice. RCAN1 mediates the time-of-day susceptibility; calcineurin inhibitor FK506 decreases infarct size at ZT10–12.
- Neutrophil infiltration to the murine heart peaks at ZT13 (nadir ZT5, ≈2.5-fold). MI at ZT13 leads to higher Ly6Chigh monocyte and macrophage infiltration in the heart (1.6–2-fold), suppressible by CCR2 inhibitor RS504393.
- Shift work increases MI incidence and cardiac infarct size (adjusted HR 1.92 in a clinical cohort of 186 patients). Shift-work mouse models exacerbate ZT12 MI reperfusion damage and suppress NR1D1 expression; cardiomyocyte-specific Nr1d1 cKO worsens MI; AAV-Nr1d1 or REV-ERBα agonist SR9009 alleviates MI damage in mice and sheep by reducing neutrophil infiltration (Figure 2D).
- SCN lesioning paradoxically attenuates MI damage (≈2-fold cardiac fibrosis reduction) by promoting anti-inflammatory CD206+ macrophage infiltration through an IGF2-mediated mechanism; anti-IGF2R antibody suppresses this protection.
- Per2 deficiency exacerbates ischemia; intense light (13,000 lux) stabilizes Per2 and is cardioprotective via HIF1A and endothelial metabolism in an EC-Per2-dependent manner.
- **Chronotherapy:** CCR2 inhibitor RS504393 and CXCR2 inhibitor SB225002 protect against severe MI at ZT13 by reducing infiltration of Ly6C+ monocytes and CXCR2+ neutrophils, respectively.

### Conclusions and perspectives

- Circadian rhythms are active regulators of vascular immunopathology; misalignment consistently worsens atherosclerosis and infarction.
- Different clock gene manipulations (target cell, constitutive vs. inducible, agonist vs. antagonist) can generate opposing outcomes — inducible models are preferred to avoid developmental confounds.
- No clinical chronotherapies for vascular disease are yet approved, but CCL2–CCR2 axis, CXCL2–CXCR2 axis, REV-ERBα, and time-restricted feeding are highlighted as actionable targets.
- Prospective controlled trials with time-of-day treatment allocation are needed; several clinical trials are ongoing (NCT00725127, NCT05932472 testing aspirin timing for cardiovascular outcomes).

## Limitations

- Heavy reliance on murine models; translation to humans is complicated by the nocturnal vs. diurnal divide (mice active at night, humans active in daytime — ZT interpretations reverse).
- Constitutive vs. inducible clock-gene knockout strategies give contradictory results in atherosclerosis (e.g., myeloid Bmal1 in different lipid backgrounds), and authors acknowledge this confound explicitly. Inducible models are preferred going forward.
- Mechanistic studies are largely single-cell-type or single-gene; the mutual interaction between EC clock and leukocyte clock (both required for rhythmic homing) is difficult to disentangle.
- Most chronotherapy proof-of-concept is preclinical; no approved vascular chronotherapy agents yet. Clinical trial results for aspirin timing are pending.
- Review does not cover sex-specific or reproductive-stage-specific modulation of circadian–vascular immune interactions, despite sex hormones modulating both circadian amplitude and immune phenotype — a gap relevant to this project.
