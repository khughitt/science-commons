## One-Sentence Summary

OPEN (Objective Prioritization for Enhanced Novelty) is a gradient-boosting machine learning framework that prioritizes causal disease genes at GWA loci using >40,000 unbiased genomic features — deliberately excluding literature-derived annotations such as GO terms and PPI networks — achieving AUROC 0.75–0.96 across 36 cardiac and non-cardiac traits, and experimentally validating three novel CVD candidates (*FLNC*, *USP13*, *SVIL*) in zebrafish.

## Key Findings

1. **High performance across cardiac complex traits.** Cross-validation AUROC values range from 0.75 to 0.90 for cardiac phenotypes including blood pressure, cardiac conduction, coronary artery disease, HDL, LDL, heart rate, PR interval, QRS duration, QT interval, and triglycerides (Figure 2D, left). Precision at 20% recall ranges from 2% to 6% for lipid traits — far above the chance baseline of ~0.1% (P = 6.6×10^-5 for plasma triglycerides).

2. **Successful prioritization without prior information.** Omitting prior experimental upweighting reduces AUROC only modestly: for triglycerides, from 0.85 to 0.80; for cardiac conduction, from 0.84 to 0.78. This demonstrates that unbiased genomic features are sufficient for high performance; prior biological knowledge is an asset but not a requirement.

3. **Strong cardiomyopathy performance (Mendelian).** AUROC 0.96 for HCM, AUROC 0.88 for DCM. At 20% recall, precision is 42% for HCM and 19% for DCM, indicating strong enrichment of true causal genes at the top of the ranking (Figure 3A,B).

4. **OPEN generalizes to 26 non-cardiac traits.** AUROC 0.75–0.93 across autoimmune, metabolic, cancer, neurologic, pulmonary, and renal phenotypes (Figure 2D, right). For type 2 diabetes and glucose phenotypes, 27 of 35 likely positives are prioritized (P < 0.0001, AUROC 0.83). Performance is weaker for heterogeneous traits such as weight phenotypes (AUROC ~0.65), where genetic architecture ambiguity degrades signal.

5. **LVD loci are enriched for cardiomyopathy genes.** Applying DCM OPEN scores to 478 genes at 11 nominally significant LVD loci (P < 5×10^-5), OPEN identifies a top-ranked gene known to harbor Mendelian CMP mutations at 10 of 11 multi-genic loci. Cardiomyopathy genes are significantly enriched among LVD loci top-ranked genes (P = 8×10^-5, odds ratio 7.65). Four top-ranked genes (*PLN*, *ACTN2*, *TNNT2*, *TTN*) were already known DCM genes; two (*GBE1*, *APOBEC2*) affect heart contractility in animal models; the remaining five (*CASQ2*, *MYL2*, *TMEM43*, *RYR2*, *USP13*, *SVIL*, *FLNC*) include the three novel candidates subsequently validated.

6. **Zebrafish experimental validation of three novel candidates.** *USP13* (rank 105 genome-wide under unbiased features; deubiquitinase): morpholino knockdown causes dose-dependent decrease in cardiac output (both decreased ventricular stroke volume and heart rate; Figure 5A). *FLNC* (rank 2 genome-wide; filamin C, actin-crosslinking protein): splice-blocking morpholino causes cardiac-specific defects including atrial distension, backflow on contraction, increased atrial cardiomyocyte size (hypertrophy), and decreased ventricular conduction velocity by optical mapping (Figure 5B). *SVIL* (rank 298; supervillin, actin/myosin binding): morpholino knockdown causes obvious pericardial edema and decreased atrial conduction velocity (Figure 5C).

7. **Novel FLNC splice-site mutation identified in a DCM patient.** Capture-based sequencing of exons from 34 established and 13 OPEN-candidate DCM genes in 60 idiopathic DCM patients (median coverage >700-fold) identified one patient with severe DCM (left ventricular ejection fraction 25%, incessant ventricular tachycardia) harboring a novel splice acceptor mutation in *FLNC* (c.3791-1 G>C), predicted to exclude a 53 amino acid exon and create a frameshift. No established DCM genes harbored pathogenic mutations in this patient; >7,500 control samples carried no comparably damaging FLNC mutations. The authors note this is the first FLNC mutation implicated in cardiomyopathy (previously linked only to myofibrillar myopathy of skeletal muscle).

## Limitations

- **CVD-centric scope.** The primary development, validation, and experimental follow-up are all cardiovascular. The non-cardiac results (Figure 2D right panel) show high AUROC but no experimental validation, and the method has not been systematically benchmarked for diseases with a very small causal gene set or diseases where the causal mechanism is non-coding.
- **"Likely positive" gold standard is imperfect.** True positives for complex-trait GWA phenotypes do not exist; the paper uses genes with prior annotation (GO, MPD, OMIM) at implicated loci as "likely positives" for AUROC calculation. This set is itself biased toward well-characterized genes, meaning AUROC may underestimate performance on truly novel genes and overestimate it for the false-positive scenario where common annotation is shared without causality.
- **Precision is low in absolute terms.** Precision at 20% recall is only 2–6% for complex traits. In practice this means many predicted candidates will not be causal; experimental validation of multiple candidates per locus is still required.
- **eQTL information not incorporated.** The paper notes that expression quantitative trait loci (eQTL) data at sufficient scale across human tissues were not available at time of publication (2014). This is now a large gap relative to GTEx v8 and other resources; the current OPEN performance floor may be substantially improvable.
- **Biased features for training examples.** Although OPEN's predictive features are unbiased, the positive training examples themselves are partly derived from literature and annotation (GO, MPD, OMIM upweighting). The paper acknowledges this and shows OPEN can run without prior upweighting — at modest performance cost — but the standard configuration retains some annotation dependence.
- **60-patient DCM cohort is small.** The targeted sequencing cohort has power to find only strongly penetrant variants; the single FLNC finding reflects the statistical limits of a pilot-scale patient series.
