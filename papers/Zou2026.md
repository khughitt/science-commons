## Key Findings

**CNV landscape:** 53.8% of CNVs were singletons; 99.3% had frequency < 0.1%. Rare/singleton CNVs were enriched for loss-of-function effects and dosage-sensitive genes. Duplications had a larger genomic footprint (median 50 kb, 41 genes) vs. deletions (median 26 kb, 22 genes).

**pQTL validation (protein dosage effects):**
- 142 rare + 175 common significant CNV–protein associations.
- Cis-1kb deletions → reduced protein levels in 98% of cases; duplications → increased levels in 91% — confirming gene dosage as the primary mechanism.
- Novel trans-pQTLs revealed protein–protein interactions: MSR1 copy loss increased C1QTNF5 and LGALS3BP (proteins linked to diabetes and metabolic disorders); MSR1 copy gain reduced their levels. No small MSR1 variant produced comparable effects.

**Binary phenotype PheWAS:**
- 189 variant-level and 196 gene-cluster-level significant associations.
- Hotspots at 16p11.2–16p13.3, 17p12, 21p11.2.
- Known associations confirmed (CMT1A from PMP22 duplication; thalassemia; obesity from chr. 16 deletions).
- New associations: B3GALNT2 heterozygous deletion → neuro-ophthalmo-psychiatric phenotype (a novel heterozygous association for a gene known recessive in congenital muscular dystrophy).
- New protective: SLC2A9 enhancer deletion → reduced gout risk; gene-cluster deletion (CD160/PDZK1/RNF115) → reduced gout risk; PDZK1 duplication → increased gout risk.
- Novel: MSH2 gene-level collapsing (no individual CNV significant) → strong colorectal cancer association (P = 5.18 × 10⁻¹⁹, OR = 192).

**Quantitative PheWAS:**
- 617 gene-cluster-level significant associations across 287 traits.
- ZNF451 deletion (chr. 6) → increased leukocyte telomere length (P = 1.38 × 10⁻¹¹, β = 0.49).

**CNV + PTV collapsing:**
- 2,274 binary + 2,965 quantitative significant associations — substantially more than CNV-only.
- Integration helped disentangle causal genes within multi-gene CNVs (e.g., HBB vs. HBD in thalassemia).
- Identified novel trans-pQTL: IFI35 deletion → reduced NMI protein levels (innate antiviral NMYC–IFI35 complex).
- TMPRSS5: chr. 17 duplication is a trans-pQTL for elevated TMPRSS5 plasma levels and is associated with neurological disease (CMT1A context); TMPRSS5 proposed as a candidate biomarker for CMT1A.

**Multi-ancestry:** 12 binary and 175 quantitative significant associations not detected in NFE alone; ancestry-specific findings include sickle-cell disorder association (AFR) and α-thalassemia (EAS).

**Pleiotropic CNVs:** 16p11.2 deletion associated with neurological diseases, obesity, COPD, acute renal failure, anemia, and cellulitis — exemplifying how single multi-gene CNVs can simultaneously affect multiple disease domains.

## Limitations

- **Autosomal CNVs only.** X and Y chromosome CNVs, which contribute to disease, were excluded.
- **Multi-gene CNVs complicate causal gene identification.** Passenger genes can confound gene-cluster associations; CNV+PTV integration mitigates but does not eliminate this.
- **CNV size threshold (>10 kb) misses smaller CNVs.** Smaller structural variants and indels are not captured, potentially leaving relevant variation undetected.
- **Non-coding regulatory elements incompletely covered.** Collapsing restricted to protein-coding, lncRNA, and miRNA genes; broader regulatory element dosage changes are not addressed.
- **UKB is predominantly NFE.** Although multi-ancestry analyses are included, the power differential between NFE and non-European groups limits ancestry-specific discoveries.
- **No cross-disease comparative analysis.** This study is not a disease-similarity study; it tests CNV-phenotype associations but does not directly compute a disease-disease similarity matrix. Conversion to a disease similarity axis (as the pan-disease project would need) requires additional processing.
- **Literature circularity not fully avoided in drug target framing.** The drug-target and biomarker discovery narrative still relies on literature-annotated pathways and mechanistic priors.
