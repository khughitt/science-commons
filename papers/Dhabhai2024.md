## Key Findings

- **X-linked TSG single-hit vulnerability:** XCI renders the single active X allele in females equivalent to the hemizygous male state; one somatic hit suffices for inactivation, bypassing Knudson's two-hit requirement. ~40% of cancer samples show LOH at X-linked loci. Extensive X-LOH correlates with higher tumor grade and lymph node metastasis.
- **Roster and chromosomal distribution:** 23 coding X-linked TSGs catalogued (mostly q-arm); 15 additional noncoding X-linked TSGs also identified. FOXP3, KDM6A, and BTK are highlighted as the best-characterised. KDM6A (Xp11.2) escapes XCI yet is frequently mutated in bladder, medulloblastoma, and renal carcinoma; 19 X-linked TSGs implicated specifically in breast cancer.
- **XCI as functional LOH and evolutionary context:** ~15% of X-linked genes escape XCI; escapees cluster at Xp22 and Xq26-28. LINE-1 retrotransposons propagate the inactivation signal. Mammalian X chromosomes show a decreased TSG:non-cancer-gene ratio compared to non-mammalian species, consistent with negative selection driving TSGs off the X. X-linked TSGs show higher nonsynonymous somatic mutation rates than autosomal TSGs.
- **Sex chromosome-autosome crosstalk:** X-linked TSGs interact spatially and genetically with autosomal loci; loss/gain of entire sex chromosomes (XO, XXY) profoundly affects autosome epigenetic state. TSG-OCG protein interaction network (50 TSGs, 50 OCGs, 106 nodes, 303 edges) shows 84.8% of edges connect TSGs to OCGs, with TSG proteins having higher betweenness and shorter path distances than target or essential proteins.
- **ncRNA regulation:** X-linked TSG mRNAs are miRNA targets; miRNAs suppress TSG translation or trigger degradation. lncRNAs (including XIST) act as master regulators of XCI and can suppress hematologic cancers. Meiotic sex chromosome inactivation (MSCI) affects most X-linked coding genes; some X-linked miRNAs escape MSCI.
- **Immunomodulation and sex bias:** X chromosome harbours immune-related genes; X-linked TSG dysregulation contributes to gender-based cancer incidence differences. FOXP3 modulates PD-L1 and immune infiltration in HER2+ breast cancer.
- **Therapeutic angles:** FOXP3 reactivation via anisomycin (c-Jun/ATF2 pathway) reduces mammary tumor growth in mice. Oligonucleotide therapies targeting TSG mRNA degradation or PTEN pathway AKT inhibition are discussed. CRISPR/Cas9 is presented as a tool to silence OCGs or restore TSG function. p53-targeting compounds APR-246 and PRIMA-1 are cited as clinical examples.

## Limitations

- Narrative review with no systematic search protocol; citation selection reflects author emphasis on X-linked biology and may underrepresent autosomal TSG literature.
- The 23-gene X-linked TSG catalogue is sourced from TSGene (a specific database snapshot); the criteria for TSG designation vary across databases used.
- Therapeutic claims (FOXP3 reactivation, p53 rescue compounds) are mostly preclinical or early-phase; generalisability is limited.
- The review conflates well-established biology (RB, TP53, KRAS) with more speculative X-linked claims without clearly stratifying evidence quality.
- Sex-bias cancer incidence claims rely on population statistics without controlling for confounders such as exposure, screening, or tumour-type composition differences between sexes.
