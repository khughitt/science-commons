## Key Findings

### Wheel-running activity (replication and validation)
- Peak daily wheel revolutions occurred at proestrus/estrus (ratio paired t-test
  p < 0.0001), consistent with prior literature on estrogen-driven activity peaks.
- Infradian (4–5-day) cycling of wheel-running activity was abolished by ovariectomy
  (p = 0.03, Pearson's chi-squared).
- In constant darkness, infradian wheel cycles were maintained in intact but not OVX
  mice (p < 0.001).

### Eating behavior rhythm amplitude
- In 12L:12D: eating behavior rhythm amplitude peaked every 4–5 days in gonadally-
  intact mice, most often at proestrus or estrus (ratio paired t-test p = 0.008).
- Ovariectomy disrupted the 4–5-day infradian cycling of eating rhythm amplitude
  (p = 0.02, Pearson's chi-squared).
- In constant darkness (circadian conditions): same result — eating rhythm amplitude
  peaked every 4–5 days in intact mice (p = 0.008), amplitude peaks co-occurred with
  days of greatest wheel revolutions (a marker of proestrus), and ovariectomy reduced
  4–5-day amplitude cycles (p = 0.037).
- Total food consumption did **not** vary significantly across estrous cycle stages
  (p = 0.23, one-way ANOVA with repeated measures) — the effect is on *when* eating
  occurs within the day, not *how much* is eaten.

### Eating behavior rhythm phase
- No systematic relationship between eating rhythm phase and estrous cycle stage in
  either 12L:12D or DD (ratio paired t-test p = 0.68 in LD, p = 0.83 in DD).
- Ovariectomy did not affect the proportion of peak-phase occurrences at 4–5-day
  intervals (p = 0.71 in LD, p = 0.56 in DD).
- The estrous cycle controls *how consolidated* eating is within the circadian night
  window, but not *when* the peak of eating begins.

### Proposed mechanism (authors' interpretation)
- Estrogens bind estrogen response elements (ERE) on core circadian clock genes
  (Per1, Per2, and others) in brain regions controlling eating, thereby modulating
  clock gene expression amplitude.
- Authors propose that high eating rhythm amplitude near proestrus/estrus consolidates
  eating into a narrower nocturnal window, freeing time for mate-seeking behavior —
  an adaptive functional hypothesis.
- Authors cannot rule out a secondary contribution of running-wheel-driven energy
  demand on the same nights.

## Limitations

- **Mouse model only:** The estrous cycle is 4–5 days; the human menstrual cycle is
  ~28 days. Whether the same amplitude-modulation mechanism operates in humans is
  untested.
- **Running-wheel confound:** Wheel running was used to confirm estrous cyclicity and
  co-varies with eating rhythm amplitude on proestrus nights. Authors acknowledge the
  amplitude peak could partly reflect increased energy demand from running rather than
  direct estrogenic regulation of eating consolidation. A no-wheel experiment is a
  stated future direction.
- **Estrogen specificity:** Progesterone's role cannot be excluded with the OVX design
  alone (OVX removes both estrogen and progesterone). The prior exogenous-estradiol
  result (Omotola et al., 2019) is the primary evidence pointing at estrogen rather
  than progesterone.
- **Mechanism unexplored:** The neural circuit controlling eating rhythm is unknown;
  the ERE-mediated clock gene expression explanation is plausible but not tested
  directly in this study.
- **Standard chow, young mice:** All mice were 12 weeks old on standard chow (low
  fat). Generalization to diet-induced obesity or aged animals is not established
  (prior exogenous-estradiol work used high-fat diet).
- **Small n:** n = 9 per group for the primary within-subject analyses, though the
  within-person (within-mouse) design provides reasonable power for amplitude effects.
