## Key Findings

### Data-derived findings (D)

1. **(D) ecDNA oncogenes rank in the top 1% of expressed genes across cancer genomes.** Allele-specific RNA-seq analysis revealed massively elevated transcription from ecDNA-encoded copies of EGFR, MYC, CDK4, and MDM2, placing these among the most highly expressed genes in the respective cancer transcriptomes. (Fig. 2)

2. **(D) ecDNA displays significantly increased ATAC-seq peak density compared to chromosomal DNA, even after copy-number normalization.** Analysis of 8,068 circular versus 6,247 linear amplified genes from 77 TCGA samples confirmed that circular amplicons carry significantly higher ATAC-seq signal than linear amplicons matched for copy number. (Fig. 3b–c)

3. **(D) ecDNA exhibits a significant deficit in long chromatin fragments (>1,200 bp) compared to chromosomal amplicons.** MNase-seq and ATAC-seq fragment size distributions show that ecDNA is depleted of the long fragments that characterize compacted, higher-order chromatin fiber, while retaining nucleosomal periodicity (i.e., nucleosomes are present but less compacted into 30-nm fiber-like structures). (Fig. 3a; Extended Data Fig. 5b–c)

4. **(D) ATAC-see microscopy directly visualizes ecDNA as foci of accessible chromatin at single-cell resolution.** In COLO320DM cells, ATAC-see signal co-localizes with MYC FISH signal (ecDNA marker) in both interphase and metaphase cells, confirming that the open chromatin state is a property of individual ecDNA molecules, not a diffuse nuclear effect. (Fig. 3d–e)

5. **(D) ecDNA retains organized domain structure: CTCF and cohesin bind ecDNA, and HiChIP reveals active chromatin long-range contacts within ecDNA circles.** CTCF ChIP-seq and SMC3 ChIP-seq show binding on ecDNA segments, consistent with topologically associating domain (TAD) organization. HiChIP anchored at H3K27ac shows massively increased corner reads on ecDNA interaction maps and notably increased distal interactions in active chromatin — supporting a compact but topologically organized ecDNA structure with enhanced intra-molecular enhancer–promoter looping. (Fig. 4b–c; Extended Data Fig. 10a–b)

6. **(D) Circular ecDNA amplicons range in size from 168 kb to 5 Mb (median ~1.26 Mb) and achieve far higher copy numbers than linear amplifications in matched samples.** AmpliconArchitect classification and BioNano optical mapping confirm the circular topology. [UNVERIFIED: exact median copy numbers for ecDNA vs. linear amplicons are not stated in the accessible summary; the qualitative direction is confirmed.]

### Author interpretations (L)

1. **(L) The lack of higher-order compaction on ecDNA explains, mechanistically, why ecDNA-amplified oncogenes are expressed far above levels achievable by chromosomal amplification.** The authors interpret the absence of 30-nm fiber-like organization as directly enabling transcription-factor and RNA polymerase access across the entire ecDNA circle.

2. **(L) The circular topology itself — rather than sequence composition or copy number alone — confers the accessible chromatin state.** The authors argue that the circular, centromere-free structure liberates ecDNA from the heterochromatic compaction imposed on chromosomal loci, particularly pericentric and telomeric domains that constrain linear amplicons.

3. **(L) Enhanced intra-ecDNA enhancer–promoter looping (shown by HiChIP) contributes to transcriptional amplification beyond what copy number alone would predict.** Active enhancers captured within ecDNA circles are interpreted as repeatedly contacting the resident oncogene promoter, providing a structural basis for the "ultrahigh" expression phenotype.

4. **(L) ecDNA's open chromatin and extreme expression phenotype give tumors carrying ecDNA a fitness advantage over tumors with only chromosomal amplification of the same gene.** This interpretation extends from the chromatin data to an evolutionary claim: ecDNA-bearing cells are proposed to drive oncogene expression to levels unreachable by chromosomal amplification, enabling faster proliferation and adaptation.

5. **(L) The combination of high copy number (enabled by non-Mendelian random segregation) and high per-copy expression (enabled by open chromatin) makes ecDNA a uniquely potent oncogenic amplification mechanism.** This dual-mechanism interpretation synthesizes the Decarvalho 2018 (segregation) and present (chromatin) findings into a unified mechanistic model.

## Limitations

- The paper studies only three cell lines and TCGA samples; cell-line-specific chromatin remodeling (e.g., GBM39 being a GBM EGFR-amplified line) may not generalize across all ecDNA-containing tumor types.
- The circular topology is correlated with open chromatin but is not proven to be the *causal* driver in an experimental intervention (e.g., linearizing ecDNA and measuring accessibility). The mechanism remains an interpretation.
- No direct comparison of *matched* ecDNA vs. chromosomal amplification of the same locus in the same cell is shown; TCGA comparisons conflate locus identity and amplicon type.
- Enhancer–promoter looping on ecDNA (HiChIP) is shown to be increased but not directly connected to specific enhancer–oncogene pairs responsible for the top-1% expression; the mechanism linking looping to expression levels is inferred.
- The paper does not address whether the open chromatin state on ecDNA is heritable across cell divisions — relevant to whether the accessibility advantage is maintained under selective pressure or can erode.
- TCGA ATAC-seq data availability limits the analysis to cancer types with profiled samples; ecDNA-enriched tumor types with limited ATAC-seq data (e.g., certain sarcoma subtypes) are underrepresented.
