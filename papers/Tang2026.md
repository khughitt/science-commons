## Key Findings

### mCA landscape and detection

- 43,617 autosomal mCAs detected in 35,033 of 484,081 individuals; mCA burden rises from 0.04 per individual at age 40–44 to 0.14 at age 65–69.
- WGS enabled detection of large mCAs at low clone fractions (<1%) and small mCAs (≤1 Mb) that are invisible to SNP-array; 624 CN-LOH mutations detected vs. 111 from SNP-array on the same cohort.

### Hotspots and fragile sites

- Short mCAs cluster at **53 genomic hotspots**, 46 not previously reported; several overlap chromosomal fragile sites (e.g., *MACROD2*, *RBFOX1*), implicating replication-stress-driven deletions independent of clonal selection.
- Fragile-site hotspots tend to be less age-enriched and less focal than CH-driver hotspots, consistent with an earlier developmental origin rather than somatic selection.
- Most interstitial deletion breakpoints show no microhomology (0 bp most common), indicating non-homologous end-joining (NHEJ) as the dominant mechanism; a minority show 1–8 bp microhomology (microhomology-mediated repair).

### CLL-associated 13q14 deletions — a high-prevalence precursor

- 13q14 deletions detected in **2,906 individuals** — a 5-fold increase over SNP-array — rising from 0.1% at age 40 to **1.1% by age 70**.
- Clone-fraction-stratified Kaplan-Meier curves: 10-year CLL-free survival ranged from 92% (clone fraction <0.05) to 72–66% (clone fraction >0.1); deletion length also influenced fitness, with shorter deletions reaching higher cell fractions more often.
- 13q CN-LOH frequently co-occurs with 13q deletion; of 149 high-cell-fraction (>5%) 13q CN-LOH carriers, 109 had evidence of a 13q deletion made biallelic by CN-LOH — yet monoallelic and biallelic 13q14 loss carry similar CLL prognosis, consistent with epidemiological data.
- mCA GWAS on 13q14 identified 18 significant loci (vs. 12 from CLL outcome GWAS), including novel loci such as *TERC*, *PRF1*, *ETS1*, suggesting mCA detection is a more powerful intermediate phenotype for CLL genetic risk than CLL diagnosis itself.
- Hi-C data from naïve B cells shows physical proximity of the most common left and right breakpoints, suggesting chromatin looping drives recurrent deletion of this 1-Mb segment.

### CN-LOH clonal expansions — 38 target genes

- Rare protein-coding variants in **38 genes** associated with CN-LOH clonal expansions in cis (burden P < 1.2 × 10⁻⁵, FDR <0.01); gene-ontology enrichment: DNA double-strand break response (P = 1.0 × 10⁻⁷), apoptosis (P = 5.1 × 10⁻⁶), cytokine signalling (P = 1.3 × 10⁻⁶), protein ubiquitination (P = 6.3 × 10⁻⁴).
- For most genes, CN-LOH duplicated a proliferation-increasing allele (second hit); for four genes (*CFLAR*, *IL2RB*, *ERG*, and others), CN-LOH removed a fitness-reducing allele (revertant mosaicism / natural gene therapy).
- *TM2D3* LoF alleles showed the highest penetrance: >50% of UKB carriers with *TM2D3* LoF had detectable 15q CN-LOH; penetrance increased from 30% at age 40–44 to 69% at age 65–69. *TM2D1* similarly strong. Proposed mechanism: dysregulation of Notch signalling via frameshift mutations in *NOTCH1*'s final exon.
- Despite high penetrance of rare coding variants, clonal fractions of most CN-LOH expansions attributable to these variants were modest (<5%), suggesting coding-variant effects are often early events subsequently amplified by other mechanisms.
- Common variants contribute modestly: heritability of CN-LOH direction attributable to common variants spanning each arm was ~0.08 (s.e.m. = 0.03), well below the contribution of rare coding variants. Only one genome-wide significant common-variant association found (*DLK1* locus).

### Biological cost / non-malignant phenotypes

- CN-LOH mutations on chromosome arms with proliferation-increasing polygenic scores for blood cell counts (WBC PGS) tended to be amplified; this effect strengthened with variant effect size — providing direct evidence that CN-LOH expansions modulate haematopoietic output even short of malignancy.

## Limitations

- **Cross-sectional design:** UKB data do not permit longitudinal tracking of individual clones; natural history inferences rest on age-stratified cross-sectional snapshots and retrospective linkage to cancer outcomes, not direct clone evolution measurement.
- **European ancestry dominance:** UKB is predominantly European-ancestry; generalizability of hotspot frequencies, gene associations, and prevalence estimates to other ancestries is unknown.
- **Sex chromosomes excluded:** mCAs on X and Y have distinct biology and were not analysed here; mosaic loss of Y (mLOY) is among the most common CH events in males.
- **Short mCA sensitivity floor:** Despite WGS gains, detection of very short mCAs (<100 kb) or very low clone fractions (<2%) remains limited by sequencing depth; some clones likely missed.
- **No clone cell-type resolution:** WGS on bulk blood does not resolve which haematopoietic lineage carries a given mCA; differentiation between myeloid and lymphoid clones requires orthogonal approaches (e.g., scDNA-seq).
- **CN-LOH causal inference:** The rare-variant burden associations are consistent with a second-hit model but cannot fully exclude LD with ungenotyped variants or epigenetic effects.
- **Progression mechanisms unexplained:** For most mCA carriers, the factors determining whether a 13q14 del clone progresses to overt CLL remain uncharacterised; the paper identifies risk correlates (clone fraction, deletion length) but not mechanistic gatekeepers.
