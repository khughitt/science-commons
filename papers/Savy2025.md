## Key Findings

### Early origins and preclinical evolution
- Driver clones can emerge decades before diagnosis: chromosome 3p loss in ccRCC may arise in childhood or adolescence; a breast cancer founding translocation der(1;16) was traced to puberty.
- Colibactin mutational signature (from *E. coli* colibactin exposure in the first decade of life) elevates *APC* driver probabilities and associates with earlier-onset CRC, showing mutagen exposure can prime the cancer evolution trajectory long before clinical detection.
- NOTCH1-mutant subclones dominate the esophageal epithelium by midlife but have a negative fitness effect in frank esophageal cancer — illustrating that clone fitness is context-dependent.

### Evolution of metastases
- No metastasis-specific driver mutations have been identified across tumor types; the same drivers that increase fitness in the primary tumor appear sufficient for metastatic potential.
- Metastatic seeding patterns are cancer-type-specific: late monoclonal seeding in NSCLC, breast, and prostate; late polyclonal in melanoma; early/stellate in CRC and esophageal adenocarcinoma; very early monoclonal in some CRC cases.
- Whole-genome duplication (WGD) frequently precedes seeding; it facilitates CIN, buffers deleterious mutations, and may increase fitness through copy-number driver evolution, but its specific role remains unclear.
- Cellular plasticity (e.g., EMP1+ undifferentiated CRC cells transitioning to ISC-like states) and cancer-associated fibroblasts contribute directly to metastatic outgrowth — microenvironmental (niche-constructing) factors are mechanistically required, not merely correlative.

### Drug resistance evolution — Darwinian and plastic routes
- Resistance evolves via two intertwined routes: (1) classical Darwinian selection of pre-existing genetic resistance clones and (2) phenotypic plasticity — drug-tolerant persister states that are largely nongenetic and often reversible.
- In melanoma treated with BRAF/MEK inhibitors: when pre-existing genetic resistance subclones are absent, cells transit through a transient neural crest stem cell (NCSC) state via nongenetic reprogramming; subsequent genetic resistance then emerges within persister cells.
- Breast cancer cells treated with endocrine therapy stochastically enter a persister state with a distinct, heritable epigenome — a nongenetic, heritable adaptation consistent with EES inclusive inheritance.
- BRCA-deficient cells on PARP inhibitors acquired resistance through sequential dedifferentiation → stemness → EMT → metabolic reprogramming, largely reversible, with cellular memory on re-exposure; gradual dose escalation (not upfront high dose) promoted progression through this continuum.
- In NSCLC, blocking the third-generation EGFR inhibitor resistance pathway with a bispecific EGFR+MET antibody reduced MET amplification ~3-fold and EGFR C797S/L718X/G724X mutations ~8-fold, validating evolutionary forecasting as a clinical strategy.
- No clear resistance mechanism was identified in two-thirds of NSCLC patients at acquired ICI resistance, implicating nongenetic mechanisms broadly.
- APOBEC-driven mutagenesis in EGFR-targeted NSCLC induces a persister state and raises structural variant rates; APOBEC deletion delays emergence of bona fide resistance clones — mutagenic processes shape the evolutionary supply of resistance variants.

### Immune escape and immunotherapy resistance (immunoediting as selection)
- In MMRd CRC, clonal MHC-I antigen presentation defects (biallelic B2M inactivation, IFNγ pathway disruption) occur in 26% of tumors, with 32% having subclonal mutations — parallel evolution of immune evasion in multiple subclones.
- Subclonal immune evasion (truncal genetic changes → sparse T cell infiltrates; subclonal → high CD8+ infiltrates) demonstrates ongoing Darwinian selection by the adaptive immune system.
- B2M LOH paradoxically associates with better ICI responses in MMRd CRC, mediated by γδT and NK cells — illustrating EES niche construction: the immune environment itself shapes the selective landscape.
- Melanoma lineage plasticity confers resistance to signaling pathway inhibitors AND activates cell-intrinsic IFNγ signaling and T cell exclusion — a single plastic transition generates multi-resistance.
- Only ~1/3 of acquired ICI resistance in NSCLC involved new genetic mutations; chronic IFNγ stimulation activated a cell-intrinsic IFNγ program that persisted after IFNγ withdrawal (heritable epigenetic state, consistent with inclusive inheritance).

### Moving beyond Darwin: EES mechanisms explicitly mapped to cancer

| EES Mechanism | Cancer Manifestation | Cancer Evidence Cited |
|---|---|---|
| **Phenotypic plasticity** | Drug-tolerant persister states; metastatic state transitions; NCSC reprogramming | Melanoma BRAF/MEK resistance [52]; SCLC platinum resistance [48]; CRC EMP1+ metastasis [30] |
| **Plasticity-led evolution (genetic accommodation / Baldwin effect)** | Phenotype-first survival → subsequent mutation fixation; gradual dose escalation resistance continuum | BRCA-deficient PARP inhibitor resistance [55,56]; Figure 3 |
| **Inclusive / epigenetic inheritance** | Heritable epigenome changes in persister cells; heritable IFNγ program in ICI resistance | Breast cancer endocrine therapy [54]; NSCLC ICI resistance [78] |
| **Niche construction** | Cancer-associated fibroblasts remodeling microenvironment to confer EGFR antibody resistance via HGF/FGF; immune TME shaping selection | CRC EGFR antibody resistance [40]; immune evasion TME [73–75] |
| **Developmental bias** | Plastic phenotypes accessible to cancer cells are constrained by normal developmental/homeostatic regulatory programs | Aggressive metastatic cancers sharing a 'basal cell state' [30]; NCSC state in melanoma [52] |

**Note on developmental bias:** The paper argues that the plastic phenotypes cancer cells can access are constrained by the cell's regulatory program heritage — i.e., accessible attractors are developmentally biased. This is implicit rather than explicitly labeled "developmental bias" in the text.

**Note on non-genetic inheritance vs. epigenetic inheritance:** The paper uses "epigenetic alterations" as the inclusive inheritance mechanism; it does not address transgenerational non-genetic inheritance beyond the cell-lineage level.

## Limitations

- Review paper: no new data; synthesis is selective and interpretive.
- The EES framework is proposed but not empirically tested in cancer; causal evidence for genetic accommodation specifically is largely inferred from pattern (reversibility, gradual dose response) rather than direct mechanistic demonstration.
- The paper does not clearly distinguish developmental bias (constrained phenotype accessibility) from niche construction (altered selective environment) — both are invoked under "plasticity" broadly.
- Non-genetic inheritance mechanisms beyond epigenetic cell-lineage inheritance (e.g., cytoplasmic inheritance, metabolic memory beyond the cell cycle) are not discussed.
- The review draws predominantly from solid tumors with extensive single-cell or multi-region sequencing data; hematologic malignancies and the role of systemic immune context are underrepresented.
- Quantitative claims about resistance timing and clone fractions are derived from specific cohort studies and may not generalize across treatment lines or tumor purity.
