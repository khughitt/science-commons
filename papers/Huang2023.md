## Key Findings

### Clock–immune interface

- The BMAL1:CLOCK heterodimer drives rhythmic expression of CCL2, TNF, IL-6, IL-12, and CXCL5 — key cytokines and chemokines that set the tone of innate immunity across the 24-hour cycle.
- Immune cells (B/T lymphocytes, monocytes, neutrophils, macrophages, NK cells) exhibit circadian oscillations in trafficking, migration from blood to tissue, and functional activity; peaks occur during the host rest phase.
- Glucocorticoids regulate chemokine receptor CXCR4 expression rhythmically, while IL-7R expression on T cells shows daily variation dependent on glucocorticoid activation.
- REV-ERBα mediates circadian regulation of inflammatory cytokines via selective regulation of inflammatory gene expression; BMAL1 ablation in macrophages amplifies cytokine storm and abolishes sepsis-phase protection (miR-155–BMAL1 axis in LPS-induced sepsis in mice).
- BMAL1 regulates NRF2 (antioxidant/anti-inflammatory) in immune cells, linking clock disruption to oxidative inflammatory risk.

### Bacterial infections

- Susceptibility to Gram-negative endotoxin and *S. Typhimurium* infection shows circadian gating: higher microbial loads and mortality during the rest phase in mice.
- Neutrophil phagocytosis and antimicrobial activity peak in the second dark phase, dependent on nocturnal melatonin surge.
- B-lymphocyte humoral immunity shows night-phase peaks in rat lymph nodes; BMAL1 knockout impairs B-lymphocyte maturation.
- Circadian disruption of intestinal microbiota (dysbiosis) through clock-gene silencing promotes pathogen invasion and metabolic dysfunction.

### Viral infections

- **HBV:** PER1 and PER3 genes downregulated in ~90% of cases; low PER mRNA in HCC tumour vs. peritumoral tissue → increased CLOCK-BMAL1 activity may underlie HCC association.
- **HCV:** PER2 overexpression reduces HCV RNA by ~27%; REV-ERBα dose-dependently inhibits HCV entry and replication.
- **HSV:** BMAL1 and CLOCK regulate chromatin remodelling at HSV loci; BMAL1 expression at time of infection determines replication outcome.
- **HIV:** REV-ERB silencing increases HIV-1 promoter activity; BMAL1 gene associates with HIV RNA levels; HIV Tat protein alters circadian rhythmicity.
- **Influenza:** infection leads to circadian disruption with deleterious effects on BMAL1 knockout mice; BMAL1 loss increases parainfluenza-3 and RSV replication and severity.
- **SARS-CoV-2:** BMAL1 silencing or REV-ERB agonist treatment reduces ACE2 expression and inhibits SARS-CoV-2 entry and replication in lung epithelial cells; circadian disruption correlates with acute-COVID-19 complication severity.

### Parasitic infections

- *Trypanosoma brucei*, *T. cruzi*, and *Plasmodium* spp. dramatically downregulate clock gene expression in the host; pro-inflammatory cytokines dampen rhythm amplitude in vitro.
- *T. brucei* possesses its own transcription/translation oscillator (independent of host cues), facilitating brain access and sleep-cycle disruption (African sleeping sickness = circadian disorder).
- *P. falciparum* requires an intrinsic oscillator for the intraerythrocytic cycle, regulated by transcription.
- *S. Mansoni*: host and parasite match circadian clocks; parasite metabolically active / interacting with immune system during host sleep phase, enabling long host survival.
- Infection timing determines susceptibility: *Trichuris muris* — active-phase infection shows delayed resistance vs. rest-phase.
- Microbiota rhythms disrupted when host clock genes silenced; gut-microbiome interaction with circadian clocks is bidirectional.

### Fungal infections

- Limited human data; most evidence from plant-fungal models (*Neurospora*, *Botrytis cinerea*, *Cercospora Kikuchii*, *Arabidopsis-Botrytis*).
- Fungal pathogens possess intrinsic circadian oscillators (FRQ proteins in *Neurospora*; BcFRQ1 in *B. cinerea*); oscillator disruption impairs virulence.
- *Aspergillus fumigatus* clearance shows a time-of-day effect in mice (2018), but mechanism unclear. Human fungal circadian data largely absent.
- WC-1/WC-2 protein expression varies across fungal species; detailed roles in human fungal diseases unknown.

### Therapeutic directions

- **REV-ERB agonists** (synthetic): bind REV-ERB receptor, prevent RNA replication and infectious particle release; shown to suppress HIV replication/transcription and inhibit HCV entry.
- **ROR inverse agonists**: compete with REV-ERB agonists at DNA response elements; anti-viral potential in vitro.
- **Melatonin / melatonin analogues**: can resynchronise circadian disruption; immune-enhancing effect stronger at night; melatonin accelerates therapy in septic patients; suppresses *T. cruzi*; increases RBC invasion risk for *P. Chabaudi* (agonist vs. antagonist strategies differ by pathogen).
- **Hormonal replacement** (adrenal insufficiency): modified-release hydrocortisone (DREAM trial) reduced infection susceptibility and improved innate immunity vs. standard regimen — linking glucocorticoid timing rhythm to infection outcomes.
- **Chronopharmacology of antibiotics**: circadian disruption alters pathogen sensitivity periods; acyclovir dosage for HSV-2 optimal at ZT18 in mice. Antibiotic timing in bacterial infections not yet systematically explored.

## Limitations

- Narrative review with no formal systematic protocol, no PRISMA flow, no risk-of-bias assessment; susceptible to selective citation.
- Mechanistic claims are predominantly from rodent (mouse/rat) or in vitro cell-line models; human translational evidence is sparse and mostly observational.
- Parasitic and especially fungal sections rely heavily on plant-pathogen models (*Neurospora*, *Botrytis*, *Arabidopsis*); extrapolation to human fungal disease is speculative.
- The review does not address sex differences in circadian-immune interactions, despite documented sex-hormone modulation of clock gene expression and immune function.
- Therapeutic claims (REV-ERB agonists, melatonin, ROR inverse agonists) are largely preclinical; clinical trial evidence is limited to the DREAM trial for modified-release hydrocortisone.
- Inter-individual variation in circadian phase (chronotype, age, shift work) is not discussed as a source of heterogeneity in infection outcomes.
- The paper was published January 2023 and therefore predates the bulk of post-acute-COVID-19 / long-COVID circadian literature.
