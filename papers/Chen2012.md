## Key Findings

**Acute lung injury.** The composite index I peaks at 8 h, declared the pre-disease state, consistent with phenotypic clinical observations. The identified DNB contains 220 genes (1167 links) enriched in oxidative stress response (GO:0006979, p = 7.6×10⁻¹⁰), inflammatory regulation, lipid biosynthesis, and response to wounding. KEGG pathways include p53 signaling, glutathione metabolism, and acute myeloid leukemia signaling; 43 of 262 DNB genes (hypergeometric test p < 7.89×10⁻³⁶) were independently validated as phosgene-lung-injury targets. DNB members include antioxidant genes (SRXN1, HMOX1, PGD), heat shock proteins (HSPA1A, HSPA1B), and apoptosis regulators (PMAIP1, GADD45G).

**HBV-induced liver cancer.** Composite index I peaks at the pre-disease time point. KEGG enrichment includes pathways in cancer (p = 1.54×10⁻⁶), MAPK signaling (p = 4.42×10⁻⁶), p53 signaling (p = 6.57×10⁻⁶), and hepatitis C (p = 4.71×10⁻⁵). Key DNB members: B2M and HLA-DMA (antigen presentation), STAT6 (antibody isotype switching), CDH1 (HBV X protein target). 17 of 56 DNB genes (p < 8.51×10⁻⁴) validated with known HBV roles.

**Simulation.** All three DNB criteria met exactly at the theoretical bifurcation point for z1, z2 in the 5-node network, confirming the mathematical derivation.

**Key structural observation.** The DNB satisfies all three criteria simultaneously only in the pre-disease state; in the normal state there is no significant concentration of variance/correlation in any subnetwork, and in the disease state the DNB members return to normal-like behavior (the new stable attractor). This transience is a characteristic signature of the saddle bifurcation structure.

## Limitations

- **Small-sample constraint.** Each time point uses only 2–5 samples, which limits statistical estimation of PCCs. The method works because each sample has many measurements (thousands of gene expressions), but sample-size-dependent variance inflation is not formally quantified.
- **Assumes the dominant eigenvalue scenario.** The derivation presupposes that exactly one eigenvalue of the Jacobian approaches 1 at the bifurcation. Bifurcations with multiple simultaneously critical eigenvalues (e.g., Hopf or degenerate pitchfork) are not analyzed, though the authors note these are handled in Supplementary Information A2.
- **Individual variation in DNB composition.** Different individuals may have different dominant groups even for the same disease. The paper acknowledges this but does not provide a method to aggregate DNBs across a cohort or test population-level significance.
- **Tissue-based data only.** All three applications use tissue samples (lung, liver, lymphoma). Applicability to blood or plasma biomarker settings is explicitly deferred to future work.
- **Does not identify drivers.** The method finds the responsive DNB (molecules that first reflect the transition) but not the causal parameters P driving the system. The DNB members may or may not overlap with the causal drivers.
- **No sensitivity-specificity analysis.** The paper reports only that I peaks at the pre-disease period; it does not provide a receiver operating characteristic curve or formal false-positive rate under permutation.
- **Linear approximation.** The derivation relies on linearization near the fixed point. For strongly nonlinear systems or large perturbations, the three criteria may not hold quantitatively even if the bifurcation is approached.
- **Code not released with original paper.** The 2012 publication describes the algorithm in supplementary text but provides no executable software. (Later extensions and R/MATLAB packages have since been developed by the group.)
