## Key Findings

- Across 265 drugs, the study identifies 8,454 unique drug-specific genes, with an average of 534 per drug.
- Drug-specific gene profiles capture functional similarities among epigenetic modulators and classical chemotherapies even when structural similarity is low.
- Chaetocin ranks as a broad candidate sensitizer, predicted among top candidates for roughly 120 primary drugs.
- Chaetocin downregulates a substantial fraction of resistance-associated genes and upregulates many sensitivity-associated genes for vorinostat and BMS-345541.
- Combination treatment reduces viability more than single agents in the tested resistant cell lines, with apoptotic death and G2/M arrest.
- A negative validation with doxorubicin in the lung cancer validation line supports the specificity of the prediction framework.

## Limitations

The manuscript is an article-in-press version and may undergo final editing.
The validation uses two resistant cell-line contexts, so the breadth of the computational prediction remains untested in patient-derived models or in vivo systems.
Drug-specific genes are inferred from cell-line pharmacogenomics and may not transfer cleanly to tumors with microenvironmental, immune, spatial, or pharmacokinetic constraints.
