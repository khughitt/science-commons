## Key Findings

1. **Wound healing mechanism.** Wound healing requires two sequential phases: (i) prostaglandins (PGs) promote PSC proliferation via localized inflammation and increased membrane permeability; (ii) chemo-surveillance — operating through DIs (differentiation inducers, including arachidonic acid and metabolites) and DHIs (differentiation helper inducers, including pregnenolone) — induces terminal differentiation (TD) of PSCs to complete repair. Abnormal MEs (ternary complexes of MAT-MT-SAHH) are constitutively active in PSCs due to telomerase association, blocking TD. DIs eliminate telomerase from MEs; DHIs potentiate DI activity.

2. **Cancer as failed wound healing.** TNF (produced by the wound immunological response) is responsible for cachexia symptoms, which cause excessive urinary excretion of low-molecular-weight metabolites (DIs and DHIs), collapsing chemo-surveillance. Without functional chemo-surveillance, TD of PSCs is blocked, and PSCs continue to proliferate. A single epigenetic event — silencing TET-1 enzyme — is sufficient to convert PSCs to CSCs. Because PSCs already harbor abnormally active MEs and express telomerase, they are "well within reach" of this conversion. 3. **MDS as paradigm case.** Myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS) is presented as the canonical example of wound-induced cancer evolution. MDS begins with immune dysregulation, TNF-driven apoptosis of bone marrow stem cells, compensatory PSC proliferation (evidenced by elevated telomerase in peripheral blood and marrow), and progressive chromosomal abnormalities leading to AML. The authors cite prior observations that mutations affecting methylation enzymes were frequent in MDS progression. [UNVERIFIED: specific mutation frequencies cited from third-party literature]

4. **CSC biology and therapy resistance.** CSCs share key properties with PSCs: ATP-binding drug pumps, upregulated anti-apoptotic programs, residence in acidic/hypoxic microenvironments, dormancy unless recruited by wound signals. These features explain why cytotoxic therapies targeting dividing cells fail to eradicate CSCs and why complete responders to destructive therapies still ultimately relapse.

5. **Post-surgical wound as recurrence risk.** Cytotoxic therapies (chemotherapy, radiation) create additional wounds, aggravating the wound environment that supports CSC maintenance, and further damaging chemo-surveillance. This creates a mechanistic explanation for post-treatment recurrence: therapy opens new wound states that recruit dormant CSCs and impair the chemo-surveillance needed to enforce their TD.

6. **Therapeutic strategy.** The paper argues that wound-healing metabolites (particularly phenylacetylglutamine and related DI/DHI compounds) are the most appropriate cancer therapy: they (i) eliminate cachexia symptoms to restore chemo-surveillance, (ii) destabilize abnormal MEs in CSCs to overcome differentiation blockade, and (iii) eliminate oncogenes and tumor suppressor gene abnormalities by returning cells to cell-cycle exit (TD). CDA-2 (purified wound-healing metabolites from human urine) is cited as superior to Vidaza and Decitabine in MDS on cytological/hematological endpoints, and without their carcinogenic side effects. ## Limitations

- This is an opinion article with no new primary data. All mechanistic claims derive from the authors' prior published work (largely from CDA Therapeutics) and from literature citations that are not independently evaluated.
- The "chemo-surveillance" framework is an idiosyncratic theoretical system developed within the authors' own research program; it is not a mainstream mechanistic consensus.
- The TET-1 silencing claim ("a single hit to silence TET-1 enzyme can convert PSCs to become CSCs") is stated without citation of direct experimental evidence for this specific conversion step.
- The CDA-2 clinical superiority claim vs. Vidaza/Decitabine is drawn from the authors' own non-randomized studies and has not been validated in independent phase III trials.
- The paper conflates PSCs (progenitor stem cells, normal adult stem cells) with cancer stem cells without clearly distinguishing the evidence base for each claim.
- The MacCarthy-Morrough and Martin citation for "hallmarks of wound healing = hallmarks of cancer" is referenced without detailed engagement with the original argument, making it difficult to assess the strength of this evidence for the framework.
- No quantitative predictions are made that would distinguish this framework from competing models of cancer initiation and progression.
