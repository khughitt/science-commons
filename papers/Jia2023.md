## Key Findings

- **A 20-dimensional continuous "disease space."** A word2vec embedding trained on
  chronologically ordered diagnosis histories places 547 broadly-defined diseases as points
  in a 20-dimensional metric space, where proximity reflects co-occurrence in patient
  histories rather than anatomical or ICD grouping.
- **The space recovers etiologically coherent neighborhoods.** Asthma's nearest neighbors are
  allergic rhinitis, emphysema/COPD, chronic upper respiratory infection, chronic sinusitis,
  food allergy, alveolar disease and pneumonia — all assigned to the same constellation
  (constellation 3). Vector algebra also yields plausible disease analogies, e.g.
  (chondrocalcinosis) + (connective tissue infection) ≈ (septic arthritis).
- **It contradicts ICD grouping in interpretable ways.** ICD-9 groups migraine with eye
  inflammation ("diseases of the CNS and sensory organs," codes 320–389). In the learned
  space, cosine similarity migraine–IBS = **0.48** versus migraine–eye inflammation = **0.08**,
  supporting prior data-driven claims that migraine sits closer to immune-system disease.
  Dimension 8 ("ophthalmological (−) to CNS (+)") drives the separation.
- **116 genome-wide significant associations at 108 unique loci**, found by treating each
  patient's position in the disease space (a weighted mean of their diagnosed diseases'
  coordinates) as 20 quantitative traits and running a GWAS in 306,629 unrelated
  white-British UK Biobank participants. **40 of the 108 loci had never been reported in any
  prior GWAS.** Pleiotropy is pervasive: HLA-B markers associate with dimensions 1, 3, 4, 7,
  12 and 16; immune-related loci (BACH2, STAT6, EMSY, SMAD3) load on dimension 8.
- **Replication is directionally consistent but only partially significant.** In BioBank Japan
  (n = 166,612, East Asian), 100 of the 116 associations were testable and 87/100 matched the
  discovery effect direction, but **only 41 reached significance at 5% FDR**. In BioVU
  (n = 16,545), 87/116 matched direction and **only 15 reached 5% FDR**.
- **Heritability of the disease-space axes is low.** No single dimension exceeds h² = 0.2;
  joint co-heritability across all 20 dimensions is 0.187. Family-based (MarketScan) and
  GWAS-based (BBJ) genetic-correlation estimates agree only weakly (r = 0.224, 95% CI
  0.085–0.355).
- **Ten "disease constellations"** (k-SVD clusters of the 547 embedded diseases) can be
  predicted from a *universal* polygenic score built from the same 116 SNPs for every outcome.
  On balanced held-out sets (chance = 50%), **prediction accuracy ranges 52.3%–64.4% (GLM) and
  53.2%–64.5% (GBM)**; Nagelkerke R² ranges 0.016–0.160 (GLM). Only one constellation reaches
  the mid-60s; eight of ten fall between 52% and 58%.
- **Constellations are easier to predict than single diseases.** The same universal PRS beat
  disease-specific models for 9 of the 10 comparisons (all but allergic rhinitis). For single
  diseases, the disease-space model raised Nagelkerke R² over published disease-specific
  baselines (asthma 0.025 → 0.06; depression 0.011 → 0.07; hypertension 0.035 → 0.14), but
  **accuracy stayed under 63% for every disease and was as low as 54% for asthma**; only 12 of
  30 diseases exceeded 60% accuracy.

## Methods

- **Design.** Build a continuous disease space from the temporal context of diagnosis codes,
  project individual patients into it, then treat each coordinate as a quantitative trait for
  GWAS, replication, and polygenic prediction.
- **Embedding corpus.** Merative MarketScan US insurance claims (the database covers >151
  million unique individuals enrolled 2003–2013; the *analytic* sample per Table 1 is
  **122,740,623**, median age 35, 46.5% male). ICD-9/ICD-10 codes were mapped to **547**
  broadly-defined, non-overlapping disease groups.
- **Embedding.** word2vec (Gensim), with diseases as "words" and a patient's chronological
  diagnosis sequence as the "sentence"; context window = 8; **20 dimensions**; PCA rotation so
  dimension 1 = PC1 … dimension 20 = PC20. Dimensions were labeled by the disease-category pair
  they most separate (Wilcoxon rank-sum over all 21×20 combinations = 4,200 tests, BH-FDR).
- **Embedding validation.** (i) Dimensionality sensitivity: distances in the 20-d space
  correlate with a 50-d space (Pearson r = 0.74 Euclidean / 0.57 cosine), and 50-d with 300-d
  (r = 0.62 / 0.59). (ii) **Physician review** of the resulting nosology — the authors state
  this human judgment, not an optimization criterion, is the benchmark. (iii) Ten constellations
  derived by **k-SVD**; hierarchical clustering reproduced them one-to-one (hypergeometric
  P = 6.5e-4 to 2.5e-34). (iv) 140 UK Biobank phenotypic measures regressed on the 20 dimensions
  as external anchoring.
- **Patient coordinates.** Each individual is placed at the count-weighted mean of the embedding
  vectors of the diseases in their record, giving a 20-vector per person.
- **GWAS (discovery).** UK Biobank, **306,629 unrelated white-British** participants; ~96 million
  genotyped + imputed variants testable; MAF > 0.01, HWE P > 1e-6; per-dimension linear
  regression, covariates = sex, age, first 10 genetic PCs; significance P < 5e-8 with Bonferroni
  correction across dimensions. Novelty assessed against the GWAS Catalog (17 May 2022) within
  ±5 kb and r² > 0.1.
- **Replication.** BioBank Japan (**166,612**, East Asian) and BioVU/Vanderbilt (**16,545**,
  European descent). One-sided P-values for direction-matched replication, BH-FDR 5%.
- **Heritability.** Family-based h² from **128,989 MarketScan nuclear families** (481,657
  individuals); GWAS-based h² and genetic correlations from BBJ.
- **Polygenic prediction.** A *universal* PRS per dimension from the 116 discovered SNPs, applied
  unchanged to every outcome. Three models: GLM on disease-specific GWAS-Catalog SNPs, GLM on the
  universal PRS, and gradient boosting on the universal PRS. Covariates: age, sex, 40 genetic PCs.
  **Balanced sampling — 5,000/5,000 for training and 2,500/2,500 for testing, repeated 100× — so
  the chance baseline for "prediction accuracy" is 50%.** Outcomes: 30 diseases (5 most common +
  25 with ≥15,000 UKB cases) and the 10 constellations. Fit assessed by Nagelkerke R², plus
  accuracy, PPV, NPV.
- **Code/data.** Code Ocean capsule (doi:10.24433/CO.0096653.v1). MarketScan requires a purchased
  license; UKB/BBJ/BioVU require their respective access processes.

## Limitations

**Stated by the authors:**

- **The 20-dimension choice was pragmatic, not optimized.** The authors say explicitly that "in a
  perfect world" dimensionality would be a model-selection problem with "unambiguous optimization
  criteria," but that embedding algorithms "are not fully suited for such rigorous model
  selection" — so the benchmark was **physicians' diagnostic judgment**.
- **A Euclidean metric was used where a hyperbolic one might be more appropriate**, since
  Euclidean geometry does not naturally represent disease hierarchy.
- **No transformation maximizing orthogonal genetic loading** of the dimensions was applied; the
  authors considered it but state that how to define/implement it "is yet unclear."
- **The core assumption may fail:** "the hypothesis of chronological co-occurrence of diseases
  being biologically linked may not always hold in daily clinical practice."
- **Histories are partial, not cradle-to-grave**, so longer-term associations "may appear lost."
- **MarketScan is claims data biased toward affluent, privately insured Americans** (the authors'
  own wording), so care-seeking and insurance status shape what is observed.
- **Cross-ancestry confounding in replication.** Discovery is white-British; BBJ replication is
  East Asian, and the authors acknowledge that differing allele frequencies and LD "could possibly
  confound the genetic association tests."
- **BioVU is small** (16,545) relative to UKB and BBJ.
- **Low heritability ceiling.** No dimension exceeds h² = 0.2; joint co-heritability is 0.187.

**Fair inferences from the design (not stated as limitations by the authors):**

- **The disease space itself was never externally replicated.** It is estimated from MarketScan
  alone; BBJ and BioVU replicate only the *genetic associations*, not the embedding. Whether the
  547-disease geometry reproduces in another health system, coding regime, or country is untested
  here. **This is the single most important caveat for anyone reusing the space as an axis.**
- **Replication yield is weak in absolute terms.** Direction concordance is high (87/100 in BBJ,
  87/116 in BioVU), but only **41** and **15** associations respectively survive 5% FDR — reported
  without discussion.
- **The discovery GWAS is restricted to unrelated white-British participants**, so the loci and
  polygenic scores carry the usual European-ancestry portability caveat.
- **Predictive performance is modest despite optimistic framing.** The abstract says the
  associations "robustly predict various morbidities," but against a 50% chance baseline,
  constellation accuracy is 52–64% and single-disease accuracy never exceeds 63% (54% for asthma).
  The R² gains are relative to weak published PRS baselines (0.011–0.035).
- **The 547 categories are coarse**, aggregating many ICD codes, so within-category heterogeneity
  (subtypes, severity, endotypes) is invisible to the space.
- **The embedding cannot separate biology from care pathways or coding artifacts.** Diagnostic
  co-occurrence reflects referral patterns, billing practice, and surveillance as well as shared
  etiology, and the method offers no way to distinguish causal ordering from shared clinical
  context.
