## Key Findings

### Molecular clock architecture
The core loop is a CLOCK–BMAL1 heterodimer that drives transcription of *Per1/2/3* and *Cry1/2*; PER–CRY complexes feed back to repress CLOCK–BMAL1, generating the ~24-hour oscillation. Auxiliary loops through REV-ERBα/β and RORα reinforce the core. Post-translational modifications (phosphorylation via CK1δ/ε, ubiquitin–proteasome degradation) fine-tune the period. Mutations in CK1δ and its target PER2 underlie familial advanced sleep phase syndrome. More than half of protein-coding genes show circadian oscillation in one or more tissues.

### Pacemaker and peripheral clocks
The SCN (~20,000 neurons) is necessary and sufficient for behavioral rhythms; it entrains peripheral-tissue clocks via neural signals, glucocorticoids, body temperature, and feeding cues. Feeding can synchronize peripheral clocks (liver, kidney) independently of the SCN, creating misalignment when food is consumed at the wrong circadian phase. bHLH–PAS transcription factors bridge the molecular clock to metabolic inputs including NAD⁺/NADH redox state and HIF-1α oxygen sensing.

### Circadian disruption and disease
- **Sleep disorders:** Misalignment between intrinsic clock and the light–dark cycle causes circadian rhythm sleep disorders. Extreme chronotypes have heritable variants in clock genes. Shift-work sleep disorder and social jet lag are common modern variants.
- **Psychiatric and neurodegenerative disease:** Mood disorders (seasonal affective disorder, bipolar disorder) are associated with reduced-amplitude or phase-shifted rhythms; therapeutic efficacy of agomelatine is attributed partly to its melatonin-agonist/5-HT₂c-antagonist dual action. Clock disruption precedes pathognomonic symptoms in neurodegenerative diseases (Huntington, Parkinson, Alzheimer); astrocyte/microglia clock control and glymphatic system clearance of neurotoxic proteins are proposed mechanisms. Clock genes regulate dopamine synthesis/degradation enzymes (tyrosine hydroxylase, MAO-A), a potential schizophrenia link.
- **Cancer:** Shift-work epidemiology (Nurses' Health Study) implicates circadian disruption in increased breast and colorectal cancer risk. At the molecular level, PER proteins interact with CRY to regulate cell-cycle checkpoints and p53, and CLOCK–BMAL1 coregulates MYC-driven glycolytic genes and HIF-1α in hypoxia-dependent tumours. CRY1/CRY2 modulate androgen-receptor signaling (prostate cancer relevance). DNA excision repair has a circadian nadir in the morning in mice.
- **Infection, inflammation, and cardiovascular disease:** Innate immune responses (cytokine release, bone marrow egress) show day–night variation. REV-ERBα represses proinflammatory cytokines in macrophages. NF-κB activity is circadianly gated through PER2. Morning temporal clustering of myocardial infarction and aortic rupture is attributed to morning peaks in platelet activation, catecholamines, and PAI-1; rhythmic endothelial nitric oxide and thromboxane production also contribute. Non-dipping nocturnal blood pressure is an independent cardiovascular risk marker.
- **Metabolic disease:** The "dawn phenomenon" (elevated morning glucose) reflects circadian-clock regulation of hepatic gluconeogenesis, growth hormone, and cortisol. Pancreatic beta-cell and alpha-cell clocks govern insulin and glucagon secretion rhythms. Melatonin receptor variants associate with glucose levels. Misalignment of feeding with clock cycles impairs glucose tolerance and contributes to obesity; time-restricted feeding can reverse diet-induced metabolic disease and reproduces benefits of caloric restriction.

### Diagnosing clock disruption
Standard assessment is plasma melatonin dim-light onset, which requires serial evening samples. Single-sample blood-transcriptome methods (e.g., TimeSignature) can estimate circadian phase computationally, opening the door to routine clinical phenotyping of circadian state.

### Therapeutic targeting
- **Behavioral/environmental:** Light therapy, melatonin agonists, timed feeding, sleep hygiene.
- **Chronopharmacology:** Oxaliplatin side-effect reduction with evening dosing in colorectal cancer; HMG-CoA reductase (statin target) peaks at night → evening statin administration; diurnal variation in receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitor metabolism suggests timing-optimized chemotherapy.
- **Direct clock modulation (mostly preclinical):** CK1δ/ε inhibitors, CRY stabilizers, NADPH oxidase inhibitor nocturnin, ROR agonists, heme modulators (REV-ERB ligands), glucocorticoid receptor signaling modulation.

## Limitations

- Narrative review (no systematic search, no meta-analysis); selection of evidence is editorial.
- Most mechanistic evidence is from rodent models; circadian-disease links in humans are largely epidemiological (shift work, daylight saving time) rather than from clinical trials. The paper acknowledges this gap under "Future Challenges."
- Therapeutic section is mostly preclinical; clinical chronotherapy trials are sparse and mostly oncological.
- Does not discuss sex differences in circadian clocks, the interaction between circadian and menstrual/infradian rhythms, or reproductive-stage effects on clock entrainment — gaps directly relevant to this project's reproductive-cycles theme.
- The circadian phase estimation from blood transcriptomics (TimeSignature) was not yet in widespread clinical use at time of writing; diagnostic utility remains largely unvalidated at population scale.
