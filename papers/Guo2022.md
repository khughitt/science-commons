## Key Findings

### 1. Ten Hallmarks of Aging at Three Levels

**Molecular hallmarks:**
- *Genomic instability* — DNA double-strand break accumulation, mtDNA mutation, nuclear architecture loss (lamin B1/A), cytoplasmic DNA → cGAS-STING pathway → SASP and inflammation
- *Telomere dysfunction* — progressive shortening → Hayflick limit → p53/p21 and p16INK4a-pRb checkpoint activation → cell cycle arrest
- *Epigenetic alterations* — DNA methylation drift (Horvath, Hannum, PhenoAge, DunedinPoAm clocks), histone modification changes, chromatin remodeling, circRNA accumulation
- *Loss of proteostasis* — UPR/UPS/autophagy-lysosome impairment; ribosomal "translation pauses" increase with age, causing quality-control overload and de novo peptide aggregation
- *Compromise of autophagy* (10th hallmark) — ATG5/ATG-7/BECN1 expression decline with age; impaired mitophagy → damaged mitochondria accumulate; transition metal (iron, copper) accumulation in senescent cells
- *Mitochondrial dysfunction* — ROS production, NAD+/NADH ratio decline, OXPHOS defects, MiDAS (mitochondrial dysfunction-associated senescence), impaired quality control (biogenesis vs. mitophagy balance)

**Cellular hallmarks:**
- *Cellular senescence* — replicative and stress-induced; p53/p21 and p16INK4a/Rb pathways; SASP (IL-6, IL-8, CCL2, MMP13, VEGF, GDF-15, TGF-β) drives tissue inflammation and paracrine senescence spread
- *Stem cell exhaustion* — HSC aging (DNA damage, epigenetic drift, symmetric/asymmetric division imbalance, cell size increase); neural stem cell decline
- *Altered intercellular communication* — SASP as paracrine driver; extracellular vesicle (EV)-mediated tRNA/protein/lipid transfer; juxtacrine NOTCH-JAG1 senescence induction; tunneling nanotube mitochondrial transfer

**Systemic alterations:**
- *Deregulated nutrient sensing* — insulin/IGF-1, mTOR, AMPK pathway dysregulation; gut-brain axis (GBA) dysbiosis → systemic inflammation → neurodegeneration; centenarian microbiota composition differs from general population

### 2. Disease-Specific Pathomechanisms

The review covers 13 conditions. Common drivers appear across multiple disease categories:

| Disease | Primary Hallmarks Implicated |
|---|---|
| Alzheimer's disease | DNA damage, proteostasis (Aβ/tau aggregation), mitochondrial dysfunction, SASP (senescent astrocytes), autophagy impairment |
| Parkinson's disease | α-synuclein aggregation/spread, PINK1/Parkin mitophagy failure, neuroinflammation (NLRP3/IL-1β), ~20 genetic loci |
| Heart failure | Senescent cardiomyocyte SASP → cardiac fibrosis; ROS-mediated cardiomyocyte senescence; oxidative stress → CCR2+ monocyte recruitment |
| Type 2 diabetes | β-cell senescence (SASP: CCL4, IL-6); adipose cell senescence → insulin resistance; gut microbiota → glucose tolerance impairment |
| NAFLD/NASH | Mitochondrial FAO impairment, ER stress/UPR, lipophagy decline, hepatocyte senescence (p16/p21/p53), gut dysbiosis → portal LPS |
| Osteoarthritis | Chondrocyte senescence → MMP13/ADAMTS-5 → cartilage loss; SASP recruits macrophages → synovitis; telomere attrition at lesion sites |
| Osteoporosis | Osteoblast–osteoclast imbalance; genomic instability → osteocyte senescence; SASP → osteoclast survival, osteoblast inhibition |
| COPD | Oxidative stress, alveolar epithelial cell senescence, chronic airway inflammation, mitochondrial dysfunction |
| AMD | RPE senescence, mitochondrial dysfunction, VEGF-driven neovascularization, mtDNA/nuclear DNA damage |
| Atherosclerosis | Endothelial senescence, foam cell formation, SASP-driven plaque inflammation |
| Presbycusis | Mitochondrial complex IV deficiency, ROS damage to cochlear hair cells, mtDNA deletion/mutation, inflammaging |
| Cancer | *Dual role of senescence*: tumor-suppressive (OIS, p53/p21 arrest, immune surveillance via CCL2/IL-15/CXCL1) vs. tumor-promoting (SASP → immunosuppressive microenvironment, MDSC recruitment, ECM remodeling, VEGF). Whether a given senescent-cell population is protective or pro-tumorigenic depends on trigger type, persistence, immune context, and TP53 status. |

### 3. Interventions and Treatments

**Lifestyle / non-pharmacological:**
- Caloric restriction (CR): best-evidenced non-pharmacological intervention; reduces senescent cell burden, reverses abnormal cell–cell communication patterns (Ma et al.)
- Exercise: activates AMPK/PGC-1α, preserves mitochondrial function; reduces AD/cognitive decline risk
- Mediterranean diet, omega-3 supplementation, ketogenic diet: cognitive protection
- Fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT): mixed clinical results; partially effective for NAFLD and metabolic endpoints

**Pharmacological (selected):**
- *Senolytics* (dasatinib, quercetin, fisetin, navitoclax/ABT-263): selectively eliminate senescent cells via BCL-2 inhibition or PI3K-AKT suppression; preclinical evidence strong, clinical trials ongoing
- *mTOR inhibitors* (rapamycin): extend lifespan in multiple model organisms; reduces OXPHOS defects
- *Metformin*: extends healthspan; reduces neurodegenerative, cardiovascular, and metabolic disease incidence; mechanism partly AMPK-mediated; side effects at high doses remain a concern
- *NAD+ precursors* (NR, NMN): extend healthspan in preclinical models; paradoxical results in healthy overweight humans for insulin/metabolic endpoints; mechanism: DNA repair, mitophagy enhancement
- *SGLT2 inhibitors* (dapagliflozin): improve HFpEF symptoms; trial NCT03030235
- *IL-1β inhibitors* (canakinumab): reduces OA symptoms and joint-replacement rates; repurposed from CANTOS CVD trial
- *Anti-Aβ immunotherapy* (aducanumab): divergent trial results (EMERGE positive at high dose, ENGAGE negative); regulatory approval contested
- *Stem cell / cell therapy*: MSC therapy for COPD (clinical trials); hESC-derived RPE transplantation for AMD (improved visual acuity in early trial)
- *Senescence-inducing therapy + senolysis* (two-step strategy for cancer): CDK4/6 inhibitors (palbociclib, abemaciclib), telomerase inhibitors, epigenetic modulators followed by senolytic clearance

### 4. Cross-Disease Common Pathway Summary (authors' synthesis)

The review explicitly concludes: mitochondrial dysfunction, defective autophagy, loss of proteostasis, cellular senescence/SASP, and altered intercellular communication are the **shared core drivers** of aging-related diseases. These are present across all 13 disease categories; other hallmarks (genomic instability, epigenetic alteration, telomere attrition, nutrient-sensing dysregulation, gut microbiota dysbiosis) also appear, but with more disease-specific patterns.

## Limitations

1. **No primary data.** All mechanistic claims are literature syntheses from heterogeneous study designs (mostly rodent/cell, some human epidemiology). No original experiments, cohort analyses, or meta-analyses.
2. **Narrative (not systematic) review.** No stated inclusion/exclusion criteria, no PRISMA flow, no search-strategy transparency. Susceptible to selection and confirmation bias in coverage.
3. **Model-organism bias.** Mechanism sections are weighted toward C. elegans, Drosophila, and mouse models. Human translational gaps are acknowledged in the conclusion but not quantified in the body.
4. **Intervention optimism.** The interventions section presents cell and animal study results alongside clinical trial outcomes without consistently distinguishing evidence strength. Senolytics, stem cell therapies, and NAD+ precursors are described with insufficient emphasis on current clinical translation gaps.
5. **Static snapshot (2022).** Some mechanistic claims (e.g., aducanumab efficacy, FMT for NAFLD) have been substantially revised by subsequent clinical data. Senolytic clinical evidence has advanced since 2022.
6. **Contradiction handling.** The review notes contradictions (e.g., NAD+ precursor paradox in metabolic health; aducanumab trial divergence; IGF-1 as both pro-aging and pro-muscle) without systematically adjudicating them.
7. **Cancer section is underdeveloped.** Given the hallmark-overlap between cancer and aging, the cancer section is shorter than the disease-specific detail for AD or NAFLD and does not engage with the deeper cancer-as-aging-endpoint debate.
