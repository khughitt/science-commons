## Key Findings

### Mechanisms treated as inadequately captured by classical Darwinian framing

The paper explicitly flags the following as requiring conceptual expansion beyond the core Darwinian triad (heritable variation + selection + fitness consequence):

1. **Macroevolution / punctuated equilibrium.** Chromothripsis, whole-genome doubling (WGD), breakage-fusion-bridge (BFB) cycles, and chromoplexy generate large-effect, single-event genomic changes that violate Darwin's "natura non facit saltum." These are interleaved with periods of near-stasis, producing a punctuated rather than gradual alteration accumulation curve. Observed pan-cancer (CIN in NSCLC, WGD in ccRCC, chromoplexy in prostate cancer, "big bang" in colorectal cancer).

2. **Neutral evolution.** A non-trivial fraction of intratumour heterogeneity (ITH) arises by random genetic drift, not selection. Approximately one-third of TCGA tumours lack evidence of subclonal positive selection (Williams et al., 2016). Neutral dynamics are most prominent at low population sizes and under weak purifying selection; they can mimic branched evolution patterns, making selection inference difficult without VAF modelling.

3. **Discordant (non-Mendelian) inheritance — extrachromosomal DNA (ecDNA).** ecDNA lacks centromeres and is therefore randomly segregated during mitosis, producing daughter-cell-to-daughter-cell oncogene copy number variability decoupled from chromosome inheritance. This creates clonal diversity in genomically stable backgrounds (Decarvalho et al., 2018) and enables rapid adaptation to microenvironmental or therapeutic selective pressure, both of which are non-Darwinian in the strict sense (inheritance is non-heritable in the Mendelian sense; variation is generated by segregation, not mutation).

4. **Cell plasticity.** Tumour cells can reversibly switch phenotypic states through dynamic epigenetic and transcriptomic changes in the absence of genetic mutation. Drug-tolerant persister (DTP) cells, EMT, and integrated-stress-response (ISR) activation are canonical examples. Resistance phenotypes arising this way are heritable at the population level only while the selective pressure persists; removal of the drug can restore the sensitive state. Importantly, MAPK inhibition in melanoma drives transcriptional reprogramming within hours of treatment initiation — a time scale incompatible with clonal selection. This is the mechanism most thoroughly developed as "beyond Darwin" in the paper.

5. **Tumour microenvironment (TME) as evolutionary niche constructor.** The TME (aged soma, immune milieu, metabolite landscape) shapes cancer initiation and evolution independently of cell-intrinsic genetic changes. Age-dependent physiological decline of somatic tissue creates a permissive environment for malignant transformation — akin to ecological niche construction — and fibroblastic stroma remodelling can promote ERK reactivation through FAK signalling, conferring drug tolerance independent of tumour cell genotype.

6. **Age-dependent carcinogenesis via soma ageing.** Cancer incidence rises steeply after age 50 not only due to mutation accumulation but also because a weakening immune system and physiological tissue decline lower the barrier to malignant transformation (DeGregori/Laconi model). This is a non-cell-intrinsic, non-genetic driver that Darwin's individual-organism framework does not address.

### Clinical applications derived from extended-synthesis framing

- **Adaptive therapy** (Fig 3B): exploit fitness differences between drug-sensitive and drug-resistant subpopulations; treat to a tumour-size threshold, not to eradication; maintains a drug-sensitive "competitor" population that suppresses resistant clones. Early clinical results in prostate cancer (NCT02415621-equivalent) promising but not yet practice-changing.
- **Collateral sensitivity** (Fig 3C): resistance to one drug induces susceptibility to a second. ABL inhibitor combinations in CML (Zhao et al., 2016); gefitinib-resistant NSCLC shows TNFα sensitivity.
- **Sucker's gambit** (Fig 3D): first-line therapy forces tumour into a highly drug-sensitive state (phenotypic adaptation), then second-line eliminates the adapted clone.
- **Preventing adaptation** (Fig 3E): combined therapies that simultaneously eradicate drug-tolerant cells at treatment initiation (ISR inhibition in melanoma; BRAF+FAK co-inhibition).
- **MRD targeting**: ctDNA and CTCs allow detection of latent residual disease years before radiographic relapse; treating MRD may eliminate resistant subpopulations while tumour burden and ITH are lowest.
- **Early detection via evolutionary timing**: molecular clocks and WGD/truncal-event dating indicate driver events precede clinical presentation by years to decades; this extends the window for interception.

## Limitations

- Being a review, it does not resolve the quantitative prevalence of each mechanism across cancer types — the claim that ~40% of therapy-resistant tumours lack a genetic driver is cited from multiple sources but not meta-analysed.
- The neutral evolution evidence is equivocal: Williams et al. (2016) findings likely suffer from low statistical resolution (acknowledged in the paper), so the true prevalence of neutral ITH is uncertain.
- Cell plasticity section leans heavily on melanoma and NSCLC examples; generalisability to haematological malignancies or slower-growing solid tumours is not discussed.
- The TME section conflates niche construction (active remodelling by tumour cells) with niche permissiveness (passive age-related tissue decline), which have different evolutionary implications.
- No formal quantitative modelling of when plasticity vs. selection is expected to dominate (q003 remains unresolved by this paper alone).
- Clinical translation section is optimistic; adaptive therapy clinical results are cited as "promising" but remain restricted to prostate cancer, and the fitness-cost assumption underlying collateral sensitivity does not always hold (ffrench-Constant & Bass, 2017, cited).
