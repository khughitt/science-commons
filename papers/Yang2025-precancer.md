## Key Findings

### Pathway taxonomy

The review organizes precancer-relevant signaling into six mechanistic domains and ten-plus pathways:

| Domain | Pathways |
|---|---|
| Aging and microenvironment | TGF-β, NF-κB, JAK/STAT |
| Mutational drivers / cell identity | p53, Wnt, PI3K, MAPK/KRAS, EGFR |
| Immune dysregulation / inflammation | NF-κB, JAK/STAT |
| Nutrient adaptation / metabolic | JAK/STAT, HIF-1α |
| Angiogenesis | Hedgehog, HIF-1α |
| Senescence-associated | NF-κB (SASP), p53 |

Key pathway-specific findings for the evolutionary framing:

**TGF-β:** Context-dependent tumor suppressor → promoter switch. Loss of canonical SMAD signaling (TβRII/SMAD2-4) is an early event in precancers, validated by mouse model experiments. Non-canonical TGF-β (TAK1→NF-κB, Nrf2 axis) drives EMT. Stromal TGF-β signals (via fibroblast TβRII) can spontaneously initiate squamous carcinoma and intraepithelial neoplasia in mouse models — a foundational "stroma-first" observation (Bhowmick et al., Science 2004).

**p53:** TP53 mutations and biallelic inactivation are pivotal initiating events in precancers. Mutant p53 acquires gain-of-function oncogenic properties. TP53 biallelic inactivation frequency increases markedly during early precancer evolution; context-specific p53 variants (e.g., p53^S53,54^ double mutant in PanIN) show context-dependent tumor-suppressor capacity, underscoring that p53-targeted therapies must exploit mutation-specific vulnerabilities.

**Wnt:** Wnt acts as a "main regulator of premalignant clonal evolution" via genetic, epigenetic, and epithelial differentiation mechanisms. Wnt-KRAS co-activation initiates biliary precancer; Wnt-driven genomic instability underlies colorectal adenoma-carcinoma sequence. Epigenetic (AXIN2/MCC promoter methylation) and mutation-independent Wnt activation are prominent in precursors.

**PI3K/mTOR:** The most commonly mutated pathway in cancers and precancers. PIK3CA/PIK3CB mutations + PTEN loss converge on PIP3 accumulation. Short-term alpelisib (PI3Kα inhibitor) prophylaxis slows early lesion expansion and prevents cancer development (preclinical), though metabolic toxicity (hyperglycemia-induced insulin feedback) hinders clinical translation. DNMT1 inhibition + PI3K/CDK inhibitors show synergy in oral cancer models.

**MAPK/KRAS:** KRAS mutations occur in ~25% of all human malignancies; present in precursor lesions across multiple tissue types. ERK signaling promotes stemness by overriding replication stress responses. MEK/ERK inhibition reinstates oncogene-induced senescence via MDM2/p21 axis reactivation (preclinical, multiple organ systems). Direct KRAS inhibition remains challenging; downstream ERK1/2 inhibition disrupts mutant KRAS-driven premalignant progression.

**EGFR:** One of the most frequently mutated oncogenes in preneoplastic lesions across oral cavity, lung, cervical, and GI tract. Macrophage EGFR signaling amplifies innate immune activation; myeloid-specific EGFR ablation reduces colitis-associated tumorigenesis and protects against high-grade dysplasia (dual role). Cetuximab mediates histopathological regression in aerodigestive tract dysplasia (clinical evidence).

**NF-κB:** Molecular moderator balancing inflammation and tumorigenesis. Linked to SASP, inflammaging, and pathogen (TLR, H. pylori) activation. Context-dependent: NF-κB inhibition attenuates precancerous phenotypes in some models but complete pathway ablation accelerates pancreatic cancer progression (mouse). Needs context-stratified targeting to preserve tumor-suppressive senescence.

**JAK/STAT:** Master regulator of inflammatory carcinogenesis; pivotal conduit from chronic inflammation to premalignant progression. STAT3 induces COX-2 (H. pylori context); high pSTAT3 (Tyr705) correlates with intestinal metaplasia; STAT3 overactivation + loss of a protein inhibitor of activated STAT3 enables high-grade serous ovarian carcinogenesis.

**Hippo:** Oncogenic YAP/TAZ role predominantly microenvironmentally driven in precancers, not mutation-driven (human cancers rarely exhibit Hippo gene mutations). Context-dependent (tumor suppressive in host-pathogen interactions; pro-growth coopted by chronic inflammatory milieus). YAP/TAZ activation synergizes with other intrinsic signals (e.g., NRG1) to disrupt airway epithelial polarity.

**Hedgehog:** Drives angiogenic and tissue-remodeling programs; upregulated in precancerous oral lesions. Key to the HIF-1α angiogenesis axis in microenvironmental hypoxia-linked transformation. Cyclic nucleotide signaling modulates Hedgehog-dependent epithelial homeostasis.

### Soil degeneration hypothesis

The review proposes a novel synthesis framing: malignant evolution of precancers is driven by simultaneous "seed" malignant transformation (intrinsic cellular pathway dysregulation) AND "soil degeneration" (extrinsic, progressive microenvironmental degradation). Key soil-degeneration hallmarks are: aging-associated dyshomeostasis, microbiome interactions, metabolic dysregulation, genomic/epigenetic reprogramming, stromal-immune remodeling, and angiogenic switching. Crucially, the PME can independently induce spontaneous epithelial carcinogenesis — TGF-β receptor II (TβRII) ablation in murine fibroblasts spontaneously initiated forestomach squamous carcinoma and prostatic intraepithelial neoplasia, positioning the stroma as a potential *initiator*, not just an enabler.

This framing updates the "seed and soil" (Paget) and field cancerization concepts for the precancer stage by proposing that soil degeneration is not static but actively degenerating under intrinsic and extrinsic stressors.

### Macroenvironmental drivers (evolutionary timing relevant)

- **Aging:** >50% of oncogenic mutations emerge before onset of neoplasia; age-associated genomic instability is a major driver of accumulated mutations in precancers. C>T substitutions at NpCpG trinucleotides show strong age-dependent correlations across malignancies (attributable to age-associated 5-methylcytosine deamination).
- **Microbiome:** HPV orchestrates cervical carcinogenesis through E6/E7 oncogene integration — the first confirmed microbial driver of precancer at the genetic level. H. pylori accelerates gastric premalignancy via CD44 activation and mucosal metaplasia. Candida promotes OLK via CXL3 secretion and IL-17A/IL-17RA macrophage pathway.
- **Obesity:** Metabolic syndromic precancer amplifier across multiple organ sites; promotes protumorigenic PME via chronic inflammation, hyperinsulinemia, growth factor axis activation, and mitochondrial dysfunction.
- **Metabolism:** Warburg effect (aerobic glycolysis) serves as a metabolic linchpin in premalignant transformation; KRAS-driven mitochondrial ROS generation triggers acinar cell dedifferentiation into PanIN progenitors.

### Chromosomal instability and clonal evolution in precursors

A key claim for evolutionary timing: chromosomal instability can emerge before many driver mutations, as evidenced by premalignant lung and Barrett's esophagus data. Copy number variations (CNVs) accumulate early in precursors, particularly at loci harboring oncogenes and tumor suppressors. Multistep carcinogenesis is hierarchically organized: initial driver mutations dysregulate cell cycle and establish the premalignant niche; subsequent hits (apoptosis evasion, differentiation blockade, metabolic-immune reprogramming) orchestrate the transition to invasive malignancy. Multiple stages can coexist within a single lesion — direct evidence of subclonal diversity in precancerous tissue (oral precancer data).

### Clinical-trial stage vs. preclinical (Table 2 summary)

**Vaccines / immune (clinical):**
- HPV-16/18 AS04-adjuvanted vaccine (Phase 3 completed): High efficacy for CIN2+ lesions; long-term absolute reductions in high-grade lesions
- VGX-3100 (Phase 2b completed): Efficacy against CIN2/3 with HPV16/18
- Nivolumab (Phase 2 active, OPVL): Potential clinical activity in high-risk OLK
- Pembrolizumab (Phase 2 recruiting, high-risk IPNs and liver cirrhosis)
- Baricitinib JAK1/2 inhibitor (Phase 2 recruiting, liver cirrhosis, OLP)

**Small-molecule inhibitors (clinical):**
- PRI-724 (CBP/β-catenin inhibitor): Phase 1/2 for HCV/HBV-induced liver cirrhosis; Phase 3 completed
- Geftinib / Erlotinib (EGFR inhibitors): Phase 2 for DCIS and liver cirrhosis
- AVX001 (tubulin polymerization / Src kinase inhibitor): Phase 1/2 for actinic keratosis (AK)

**Repurposed drugs (clinical):**
- Metformin (Phase 2/3 for OLK, CIN 2/3, dysplasia): Blocks STAT3-mediated signaling, PI3K/mTOR, NF-κB pathways; well-documented preclinical + early clinical evidence
- Celecoxib (Phase 2 active, OLK/erythroplakia): COX-2 inhibitor
- Imiquimod (TLR7 agonist, Phase 3 for HSIL): Completed; reduces progression to cancer (NCT02135419)
- Tamoxifen (Phase 3 active, ADH/DCIS/LCIS): ERs modulator; prevents breast lesion recurrence; 10-year follow-up data available
- Thalidomide (Phase 4 completed, erosive OLP): Well-tolerated
- Artesunate (Phase 2 active, AK): Inhibitor of STAT3 and PKCα/PKCδ

**Preclinical (selected):**
- Senolytics (dasatinib + quercetin, navitoclax): Promising preclinical, underexplored in clinical precancer management
- CAR-T against uPAR+ senescent cells: Preclinical
- Alpelisib (short-term PI3Kα inhibition): Preclinical (breast cancer prevention)
- DNMT1 inhibition + PI3K/CDK inhibitors: Oral cancer preclinical
- ATRA (all-trans retinoic acid): Clinical evidence in gastric precancers (suppression of malignant transformation via exosomal LncHOXA10/pyruvate carboxylase axis)

### Evolutionary timing of pathway dependence in precursors vs. cancers

The paper makes the following implicit and explicit claims about evolutionary timing:

1. **CIN/CNV precede most driver mutations in some precancer types** (lung, Barrett's esophagus). Chromosomal instability is a priming event, not a consequence of advanced evolution.
2. **Epigenetic driver events (DNA methylation, histone modifications) are among the earliest detectable molecular changes** in multiple precancer types — preceding morphological atypia in some settings (HCV-infected liver, PanIN from Kras models). TET1-mediated 5hmC enrichment at ZEB1 and VIM promoters in cervical precancers represents an early, staged event.
3. **Pathway dependence shifts from microenvironmental/stromal signals to cell-intrinsic genetic drivers as precancer progresses.** In early/premalignant stages, PME signals (TGF-β from stroma, inflammatory cytokines, microbial factors) dominate. As driver mutations accumulate (p53, KRAS, PIK3CA), cell-autonomous pathway dependence emerges. The paper does NOT quantify this transition with data but asserts it conceptually.
4. **Premalignant clonal evolution is governed primarily by Wnt as a master regulator** linking genetic, epigenetic, and cell differentiation mechanisms — an "early pathway" claim specific to clonal dominance.
5. **Hierarchical organization of mutational events:** Initial driver mutations (cell cycle dysregulation, establishing the premalignant niche) → subsequent hits (apoptosis evasion, differentiation blockade, metabolic-immune reprogramming) → invasive malignancy.

## Limitations

- The review covers epithelial precancers comprehensively but hematologic precancers (CHIP, MGUS, SMM) are mentioned only briefly (as unique models lacking morphological boundaries). The evolutionary mechanisms documented here are heavily epithelial-biased and do not translate directly to clonal hematopoiesis contexts.
- The "evolutionary timing" of pathway dependence is asserted conceptually but not supported by systematic quantitative data (e.g., mutation timing analyses, phylogenetic reconstruction of precancer clonal trees). The paper relies on carcinogenesis model studies and genomic correlations, not evolutionary trajectory data.
- The "soil degeneration hypothesis" is a compelling framework but is not formally tested in the paper — it is proposed as a synthesis of existing observations rather than a new empirical result. Key prediction (that soil degeneration precedes and drives epithelial transformation rather than being co-incident) requires temporal data (prospective biopsies of pre-lesional stroma) not yet available.
- The clinical trial table (Table 2) is comprehensive but heterogeneous in evidence quality: multiple entries have "no results posted" or "unknown status," limiting the ability to assess which pathway interventions have actually demonstrated efficacy at the precancer stage.
- The review is primarily focused on oral, gastric, esophageal, cervical, and colorectal precancers with strong histopathological traditions. Lung, pancreatic, and liver precancers receive less systematic coverage of pathway data.
- No engagement with evolutionary biology frameworks (Darwinian selection, neutral evolution, bet-hedging, evolvability) or Extended Evolutionary Synthesis. The review treats pathway dysregulation as a linear mechanistic pathway rather than as fitness-landscape navigation.
- Potential institutional/publication bias: strongly weighted toward Chinese institutional data (Sichuan University, West China Hospital) in both the clinical sections and mouse model citations.
