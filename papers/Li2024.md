## Key Findings

The review synthesizes the following conclusions:

1. **Cross-sectional TP is declining.** The widespread adoption of NGS has made regional bulk and single-cell designs preferable even for between-patient comparisons; oncogenetic tree approaches built on CGH are largely historical.

2. **Bulk + single-cell complementarity.** Bulk sequencing TP offers affordability and multi-region breadth but suffers from averaging artifacts and subclone underresolution. Single-cell TP provides cellular-resolution clonal architecture but is costly and computationally demanding. Their combination (e.g., PhISCS) is proposed as an emerging complementary strategy.

3. **Lineage tracing captures evolutionary dynamics unavailable to snapshot methods.** CRISPR-Cas9-based lineage tracing (Yang et al. 2022 KP-tracer / Cassiopeia-Hybrid) revealed that rare subclones with unique transcriptional programs — following a period of heightened plasticity — drive metastasis. This is direct empirical support for plasticity-driven clonal dynamics.

4. **Multi-omic integration is the field's growth edge.** Integrating transcriptomic, epigenomic, and spatial data into phylogenetic trees moves beyond genetic determinism to capture non-genetic adaptive forces. The paper argues that the full spectrum of evolutionary forces (including epigenetic plasticity) only becomes visible through this integration.

5. **Key evolutionary insights accessible via phylogenetics:**
   - Timing of metastatic lineage divergence (often predating clinical diagnosis — Zhao et al. 2016).
   - Temporal ordering of key mutational milestones (genome doubling, driver mutations) along the tree trunk.
   - Identification of subclone-specific mutations and phenotypic features driving therapy resistance.
   - Tumor classification by mutation/expression profiles without geometric clustering assumptions.
   - Detection of monoclonal vs. polyclonal metastasis seeding patterns.

6. **Therapy resistance as an evolutionary readout.** Phylogenetic analysis of lung adenocarcinoma demonstrated that cisplatin therapy induced a localized mutation burst, leading to EGFR T790M resistance — illustrating how phylogenetics can timestamp and cause-attribute resistance emergence.

7. **Spatial transcriptomics** is flagged as an emerging modality for the spatial dimension of intra-tumor heterogeneity, and machine learning methods for phylogeny topology inference are noted as a growing area.

## Limitations

- Narrative review with no systematic search protocol described; coverage of tools reflects the Yu lab's ggtree-centric ecosystem, potentially under-representing alternatives.
- The paper advocates for multi-omic integration but the practical barriers (format heterogeneity, visualization complexity, limited shared standards) are acknowledged as largely unsolved.
- Single-cell methods section does not deeply address emerging long-read sequencing approaches (PacBio, Oxford Nanopore) beyond mentioning their necessity for recombinase-based lineage tracing.
- Machine learning for phylogeny topology inference is mentioned but not critically assessed.
- The review does not address the statistical challenges of distinguishing neutral from adaptive evolution within reconstructed trees (a central challenge for the project's research question).
- No discussion of copy-number-only phylogenetics (relevant for ecDNA, CIN studies) or ecDNA-specific phylogenetic considerations.
- Spatial transcriptomics is flagged as an emerging area but not reviewed substantively.
