## Key Findings

1. **Two top-level molecular disease classes: infectious vs non-infectious.** At k = 2, the 3526 diseases split into C1 (416 diseases, 305/416 = 73% infectious) and C2 (3110 diseases, >99% non-infectious). Crucially, 111/416 diseases in C1 are not clinically classified as infectious — examples include constipation, colorectal cancer, muscle dystrophy, pneumonia, and bronchitis — revealing clinically non-obvious diseases with molecular signatures resembling infectious diseases.

2. **Top MOA proteins are shared across vast numbers of diseases.** The top 50 MOA proteins each appear in 350–1500+ of the 3526 diseases. The top proteins (AR, NR3C2, KCNA10, TSPAN13, NR4A3) are associated with 1392–1531 diseases. The top 100 MOA proteins collectively cover ~80% of all diseases. This explains the observed high density of disease comorbidity.

3. **Nine non-infectious subclasses (level 2) are mostly clinically coherent.** Five of nine C2 subclasses are dominated (>70%) by a single ICD10-CM clinical classification: C2-1 by digestive (98/110), C2-2 by metabolic (317/411), C2-4 purely by neoplasms (155/155), C2-6 by eye diseases (429/438), C2-8 by neoplasms (75/103). C2-9 (1405 diseases) is the most diverse and warrants level-3 subdivision.

4. **Molecular clustering reveals clinically unexpected disease groupings.** Class C2-7 (n = 130, "diverse") groups cervical cancer, prostate cancer, liver cancer, Alzheimer's disease, coronary artery disease, Crohn's disease, opiate dependence, asthma, night blindness, and central sleep apnea — diseases spanning nervous system, circulatory, digestive, respiratory, and cancer categories. The shared molecular thread is the canonical retinoid cycle in rods (twilight vision) pathway, mediated by shared proteins LRAT, RDH11, RBP4. These proteins link retinoic acid signalling across apparently unrelated diseases.

5. **Class-level pathway enrichment is highly reproducible and literature-supported.** 17 of 21 classes tested have their top frequent pathways present in >90% of member diseases. For the infectious disease class C1, all top 20 GO functions and top 20 Reactome pathways are present in >95% of member diseases; C2 is more diverse (~1/3 coverage for top functions). Literature search of the top 10 pathways for 7 classes found ≥77% correctness. For C2-9's class-level pathway analysis, Glutathione conjugation is the top pathway (q < 0.1, 253/1405 diseases), with PLK1 regulation, SCF-BTRC complex, and beta-catenin deactivation among the top 4.

6. **ChatGPT and Valsci validation of disease-pair relationships.** For 26 clusters with top-10 ranked non-redundant disease pairs: 20% of within-cluster pairs have ChatGPT-confirmed literature evidence vs 4.2% for random cross-cluster pairs (enrichment factor 4.8). At the top-500 ranked pairs the fraction drops to 13%, but the cluster-vs-random enrichment factor remains 2.5. Valsci analysis of 1700 pairs confirmed χ² = 12, p = 0.0003, enrichment factor 1.9 for highly-supported associations (score 4–5).

7. **Infectious class C1 is the molecular root of the disease dendrogram.** In the Euclidean distance dendrogram over the 26 class centroid vectors, C1 is most distant from all non-infectious classes. A small circulatory subclass C2-9-13 (varicose veins, anterolateral myocardial infarction, anteroseptal myocardial infarction) is the closest non-infectious class to C1, sharing 92 of 95 significant pathways with C1 (immune system, innate immune system, cytokine signalling, JAK-STAT).

8. **Sankey diagram reveals systematic misalignment of clinical and molecular categories.** Neoplasms and eye diseases map cleanly to single molecular classes; most other clinical categories scatter across multiple molecular classes. Infectious diseases map predominantly to C1 but also to digestive, eye, metabolic, and circulatory subclasses of C2 — demonstrating that molecular re-classification does not recapitulate ICD10 structure.

9. **PICMOA is extensible to rare diseases.** The 2000 MOA basis genes overlap 508 of 6095 OMIM genes (p = 3.88 × 10⁻¹¹). Extension to include all 6095 OMIM genes is noted as future work to cover rare Mendelian diseases.

## Limitations

- **MOA proteins are predicted, not measured.** LeMeDISCO derives MOA proteins from predicted drug-comorbidity associations, not from direct transcriptomic, proteomic, or genetic evidence. The molecular signal in PICMOA is therefore itself partly literature-derived (drugs and their known targets are documented in the literature). This means PICMOA is not a fully independent molecular axis from the project's perspective — it sits between the project's gene-axis (literature-derived gene mentions) and a true expression- or genetics-derived molecular axis.

- **Tissue-of-origin specificity is claimed but not fully validated.** The paper states only proteins expressed in the disease tissue are included, but the tissue-expression criterion is applied at the level of predicted comorbidities, not direct tissue proteomics. Cross-tissue contamination is possible.

- **k-means requires specifying a distance metric (Euclidean).** Disease feature vectors of different magnitudes may be dominated by high-frequency hub proteins, which could partially reproduce publication-volume bias effects at the protein level (analogous to this project's publication-gravity confound on the gene axis).

- **Validation is primarily literature-based.** ChatGPT and Valsci confirmations both draw on published literature; they are not independent of the publication record. The ~20% literature confirmation rate for within-cluster pairs (vs 4.2% random) is a real enrichment but leaves 80% unconfirmed — the paper acknowledges this reflects the limits of current published evidence, not necessarily wrong predictions.

- **The approach is currently population-averaged, not personalized.** MOA proteins represent average disease profiles; individual patient genetic variation (SNPs, exome data) is not incorporated. This limits immediate clinical translation.

- **3526 diseases is a large but incomplete disease universe.** The HDO set excludes many rare diseases and phenotypes well-represented in OMIM (only 508 of 6095 OMIM genes overlap the MOA basis). Disease coverage biases the molecular taxonomy toward common diseases.

- **No direct comparison to other molecular disease taxonomies.** The paper does not compare PICMOA cluster assignments to TCGA pan-cancer molecular subtypes, HPO-based phenotypic clusters, or PubTator-derived gene-axis clusters — making external ARI comparison an open contribution.
