## Key Findings

- Evolutionary medicine can guide biomedical innovation by treating disease vulnerability and disease resistance as evolved traits.
- Comparative mapping across species may reveal natural animal models of resistance to cancer and other high-impact diseases.
- Cancer therapy should account for the evolutionary consequences of treatment exposure, especially selection for resistant subclones.
- Adaptive therapy and extinction therapy are presented as general evolutionary strategies because both depend on changing treatment in response to tumor dynamics.
- The paper explicitly contrasts fixed maximum-tolerated-dose treatment with strategies that target resistance evolution.
- The antimicrobial and phage-therapy sections reinforce a project-relevant design principle: therapeutic resistance is often inevitable, but its direction and collateral trade-offs can sometimes be steered.

## Limitations

This is not a systematic review and does not provide a new cancer dataset, clinical trial, or mathematical model.
The cancer section is intentionally selective, so project decisions should rely on the more focused papers for evidence strength, parameterization, and trial-readiness claims.
