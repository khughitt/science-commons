## Key Findings

**BMAL1 loss in OA:**
- BMAL1, CLOCK, RORα, and REV-ERBα proteins are detectable in mouse FHC explants and in cryopreserved apparently intact human cartilage.
- IL-1β treatment (10–50 ng·mL⁻¹) dose-dependently reduces Bmal1 mRNA (Fig. 1H) and BMAL1 protein (Fig. 1I–L), while increasing catabolic markers MMP13 and ADAMTS5 and decreasing anabolic markers COL2A1 and Aggrecan.

**NOB and SR8278 — protective; SR1078 — mixed:**
- In IL-1β-treated mouse FHC explants, NOB (32 µM) and SR8278 (10 µM) significantly increased cellular density and BMAL1 expression, reduced catabolic markers (MMP13, ADAMTS5), and reduced collagen denaturation (F-CHP signal). SR1078 (10 µM) upregulated BMAL1 and suppressed catabolism but also suppressed anabolism (Col2a1 not restored), reducing its net benefit.
- In IL-1β-treated human cartilage explants: NOB and SR8278 improved cellular density, reduced ADAMTS5, and restored COLII; SR8278 effect was confirmed by western blot (Fig. 6).
- In DMM mice: NOB and SR8278 significantly reduced OARSI scores vs. vehicle-treated DMM mice (Fig. 5B); SR1078 trended towards improvement but did not reach significance. NOB and SR8278 increased BMAL1-positive chondrocytes (Fig. 5D) and reduced F-CHP fluorescence vs. DMM vehicle.

**BMAL1-dependence confirmed:**
- In C28/I2 cells with Si-BMAL1#3 knockdown, protective effects of NOB and SR8278 on COL2A1 expression and MMP13 suppression were abolished (Fig. 7F,G), demonstrating BMAL1-dependent action.
- SR1078's inhibitory effect on anabolism persisted under BMAL1 knockdown (Fig. 7H), indicating that SR1078's anabolism suppression is clock-independent and explains why it diverges from NOB and SR8278.

**BMAL1 oscillation rescue:**
- In IL-1β-challenged C28/I2 cells at ZT intervals, BMAL1 transcripts oscillate with a cosinusoidal pattern (CircaCompare P < 0.05). IL-1β decreased oscillation amplitude; NOB and SR8278 significantly rescued amplitude (P < 0.05 for amplitude difference); mesor differences were not significant (Fig. 7I,J).

## Limitations

- All animal experiments use male mice only; sex differences in chondrocyte clock function and OA are unaddressed.
- Central clock contributions are uncontrolled: systemic drug delivery alters the suprachiasmatic nucleus as well as peripheral cartilage clocks; the paper acknowledges that coordinated central-peripheral effects may contribute to the in vivo results.
- Circadian activity measurements were not taken in DMM mice, so the degree to which OA pain-associated activity disruption feeds back on clock dampening is unknown.
- SR1078's clock-independent anabolism suppression was not mechanistically resolved; the paper recommends against using ROR agonists as sole surrogates for BMAL1-dependent clock modulation.
- Human sample size for cartilage explants is small (N = 3 patients); replicated findings are in a standard chondrocyte cell line (C28/I2).
- Study lacks pharmacokinetic data for NOB and SR8278 in joint tissue specifically; systemic versus local drug concentrations at the cartilage are unknown.
- The contribution of tissue-specific versus isoform-specific ROR and REV-ERB actions (e.g., RORα vs. RORγ in cartilage) is not dissected.
- Whether TGF-β and NFAT signalling downstream of BMAL1 loss are the operative effectors remains a stated open question.
