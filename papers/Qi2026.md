## Key Findings

**NP-hardness:** Both Backbone Trees from Reads problems are NP-hard even for m=2 samples (Theorem 1, proved by reduction from Perfect Phylogeny Mixture Deconvolution). Finding a maximum-likelihood full tree is a special case, confirming hardness throughout.

**Near-optimal backbone recovery (simulations, n=8):** Median approximation ratio = 1 (optimal) across 20 instances; maximum ratio 7 (14 Sapling trees vs. 2 optimal, at ℓ=5). Perfect recall of backbone trees relative to ground-truth projections (data not shown in paper). For τ-constrained mode, median approximation ratio = 1; minimum 0.25 (|S|=1 vs. optimal |S*|=4, for τ=1).

**fastppm speedup:** Using `fastppm` instead of `cvxopt` achieves several orders of magnitude speedup (Fig. 3a–c of journal version), making Sapling practical for n=100 mutations on a laptop (16 GB RAM, Apple M1 Pro).

**Full-tree quality (beam search, n=20 simulations):** Sapling with w=1 outperforms Orchard with w=100 in log-likelihood for n ∈ {50,100} while running in a fraction of the time (median 27.91 s for Sapling w=1 vs. 641.12 s for Orchard w=100). Sapling outperforms fastBE (w=100) in likelihood for all mutation regimes (fastBE uses L1 approximation). In tree-structure distance metrics (PC, AD, CASet, DISC): Sapling better matches ground-truth than Orchard and is comparable to fastBE across beam widths and mutation counts (Table 2). Example: n=20, w=50 → Sapling median PC=0.54 vs. Orchard 0.69 vs. fastBE 0.54.

**Real data (TRACERx NSCLC, n=100 patients):** For ρ=0.4, 26 patients have ≥2 plausible trees; for ρ=0.9, 14 patients. With τ=1, a single backbone tree covers a median 75% (ρ=0.4) or 81% (ρ=0.9) of mutation clusters per patient. For patient CRUK0013 (n=9 clusters), the τ=1, ρ=0.4 backbone spans 7/9 clusters and is a proper subtree of the MCT consensus tree. For CRUK0037 (n=10 clusters), τ=2 covers one additional cluster over τ=1 for both ρ values.

## Limitations

- **ISA assumption:** The infinite sites assumption (each mutation gained once, never lost) is explicitly acknowledged as imperfect, particularly due to copy-number loss. Mutation clustering mitigates this but does not eliminate it. Extension to non-ISA trees is listed as a future direction.
- **τ=1 underperformance:** For the single-backbone-tree regime (τ=1), Sapling's approximation ratio can be as low as 0.25, and the paper notes a decrease in performance relative to higher τ. The authors plan a specialized algorithm for this important single-tree case.
- **Scalability of FastBackboneEnumeration:** Running time scales exponentially with n and τ for the full enumeration mode (Fig. 3a–c). For large tumors (n=100) with high τ, enumeration becomes impractical. The beam-search mode (BackboneBeamEnumeration) trades completeness for scalability.
- **Single-sample or single-region tumors:** Sapling requires multi-sample (or multi-region or multi-timepoint) bulk sequencing data (m≥2 for NP-hardness result; practically, more samples improve frequency estimation). Single-region tumors produce highly ambiguous F-hat, degrading backbone quality.
- **Bulk sequencing only:** Designed for bulk (regional or temporal) DNA sequencing. The backbone tree formalism does not directly apply to single-cell data, where ISA can be applied at the cell level with higher resolution but different statistical challenges.
- **No explicit copy-number modeling:** Tumor phylogeny pipelines feed mutation clusters to Sapling, but copy-number inference and cluster assignment are handled upstream. Errors in upstream clustering propagate into backbone trees.
- **Comparison scope:** The journal version adds fastBE as a comparator but does not include more recent single-cell-aware methods or deep-learning-based phylogenetic reconstructors. The competitive landscape is evolving.
