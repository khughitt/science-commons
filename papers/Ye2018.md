## Key Findings

**1. Pervasive clock-gene transcriptional dysregulation in cancer.**
90.2% of the 51-gene set is differentially expressed in at least one of 14 cancer types with paired normals. Among core clock genes, CRY1/2 and PER1/2/3 show the most significant downregulation; ARNTL2 is upregulated in 9 cancer types. Cancer-type–specific expression patterns occur: PER2 is upregulated in kidney renal clear cell carcinoma (KIRC; FC = 1.8) while downregulated in most other types. RORB downregulation is notably widespread (10 cancer types). Promoter DNA methylation negatively correlates with expression across most clock genes, suggesting epigenetic silencing contributes to dysregulation.

**2. Circadian rhythm disruption and reprogramming in cancer.**
The number of genes genome-wide that correlate with clock genes is significantly reduced in tumor versus normal tissue across all cancer types examined, indicating global disruption of the clock's co-expression network in tumors. CRY2 loses correlation with ARNTL2 in normal kidney tissue (R = −0.7; FDR = 0.002) but not in KIRP tumors, illustrating cancer-specific rewiring. In an MCF7 breast cancer cell line versus MCF10A non-tumorigenic epithelial line, 478 genes show JTK_CYCLE/meta2d circadian oscillation patterns in MCF10A but not MCF7 (including PER1, ARNTL, NR1D2, PARP1, GSK3B, CSE1L). Additionally, 552 genes oscillate in MCF7 but not MCF10A, enriched in TNF and WNT signaling pathways.

**3. Clock-gene expression links to oncogenic pathways.**
Across the 10 RPPA-derived signaling pathways (apoptosis, cell cycle, DNA damage response, EMT, hormone a/b, PI3K/AKT, RAS/MAPK, RTK, TSC/mTOR), clock genes are broadly associated with pathway activation or inhibition patterns. CRY2 is associated with inhibition of cell cycle in 15 cancer types and DNA damage response in 5 cancer types; RORA and RORB are associated with EMT activation, while RORC is associated with EMT inhibition. Paralog combinatorial analysis shows that high co-expression of RORA and RORC together activates 90 pathways and inhibits 103, more than any single paralog, and patients with low combined RORA/RORC expression have significantly worse survival (Figure S2C).

**4. Genetic and epigenetic alterations in clock genes.**
Mutation frequencies are generally low for core clock genes (< 5 mutations in most samples), but EP300 (p300) and CREBBP (CBP) — coactivators of BMAL1-CLOCK — show relatively high mutation frequencies. Despite low mutation rates, mutations in core clock genes are associated with patient survival: breast cancer (BRCA) patients with core clock mutations have worse overall survival (log-rank p = 0.05); PER1/2/3 mutations are associated with markedly worse survival (log-rank p = 5.9 × 10⁻⁵ in BRCA).

**5. Clock-gene expression predicts patient survival, tumor stage, and subtype.**
31 clock genes are associated with overall survival in at least one cancer type. CRY2 low expression is associated with worse survival in LIHC (p = 1.9 × 10⁻⁴), KIRP (p = 4.5 × 10⁻⁴), and KIRC (p = 1.4 × 10⁻⁵). ARNTL2 overexpression is associated with worse survival in LGG (p = 1.7 × 10⁻⁴) and LUAD (p = 0.0022) — indicating ARNTL2 can function as an oncogene in these contexts. 23 clock genes are differentially expressed across tumor stages (e.g., CRY2 decreases in stage IV KIRP). 43 clock genes show subtype-specific expression; CRY2 is differentially expressed across KIRC subtypes (FDR = 3.9 × 10⁻³⁶). In triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), ARNTL2 (FDR = 1.9 × 10⁻⁷) and NPAS2 (FDR = 7.9 × 10⁻¹⁸) are upregulated, while CRY2 (FDR = 1.1 × 10⁻⁴⁶), PER2 (FDR = 2.4 × 10⁻¹⁹), NR1D1 (FDR = 4 × 10⁻⁹), and RORB (FDR = 3.8 × 10⁻²⁰) are downregulated.

**6. Clock genes interact with clinically actionable genes (CAGs).**
145 of 154 CAGs (94.2%) have significant correlations with at least one clock gene in more than five cancer types. CRY2 alone is significantly and positively correlated with 31 CAGs enriched in PI3K/AKT, RAS, and FOXO pathways, and negatively correlated with 15 CAGs. ARNTL2 is correlated with 16 of 19 (84.2%) immunotherapy target genes. Co-expression, protein–protein interaction (BioGRID/HPRD), and ChIP-seq evidence converge to identify specific interactions: e.g., CREBBP is both a clock co-activator and a C646-targeted CAG; EZH2 interacts with CRY2 to regulate clock gene expression. CRY2 knockdown in MEF cells decreases AKT3, EPHA3, PIK3R1, PDGFRA, PDGFRB expression and increases AURKA, BRCA1, CDK4, ETV6, JAK3 expression, confirming functional links.

**7. Clock-gene expression correlates with anti-cancer drug sensitivity.**
In 1,080 cancer cell lines (GDSC), 2,059 significant correlation pairs are identified between drug sensitivity and clock-gene expression. Sensitivity to 82 anti-cancer drugs correlates with at least 10 clock genes. Known chronotherapy drugs including 5-fluorouracil (negatively correlated with CRY2, PER2, PER1, RORB, and ten other clock genes), methotrexate, and docetaxel are confirmed. Novel candidate: phenformin (biguanide targeting metabolism) shows strong negative correlation with CRY2 (Z = −4.5; p = 8 × 10⁻⁸), PER2 (Z = −5.99; p = 2.3 × 10⁻⁹), and CSNK1D (Z = 6.04; p = 1.7 × 10⁻⁹). Drugs whose targeted genes correlate highly with clock genes include HDAC inhibitors (histone acetylation, 70% of drugs tested), WNT inhibitors (50%), cell-cycle inhibitors (45.5%), PI3K inhibitors (45%), and DNA replication drugs (35.7%). GDSC and CTRP results are consistent across 53 drugs found in both databases.

## Limitations

- **No within-patient time-of-day sampling:** All TCGA samples are single timepoint biopsies. Clock-gene expression in a snapshot does not directly measure phase or rhythm amplitude; "high vs. low expression" conflates phase and amplitude with stable up/down regulation.
- **Tumor purity correction is imperfect:** Pearson correlations were corrected using TIMER tumor purity estimates, but residual immune and stromal cell contributions may confound apparent circadian disruption.
- **Cell-line pharmacogenomics may not translate:** Drug-sensitivity correlations in cancer cell lines (GDSC/CTRP) are not always predictive of in vivo patient outcomes. The paper itself notes that "drug response in cancer cell lines is not always consistent with patient survival."
- **Mutation rates are low and survival associations are borderline:** Core clock gene mutation frequencies are generally < 5% across samples; the core clock mutation survival association in BRCA is at p = 0.05 (uncorrected).
- **Causality unresolved:** The direction of causality between clock disruption and oncogenic pathway activation is not established — dysregulation could be downstream of tumor biology rather than upstream.
- **No in vivo chronotherapy validation:** The pharmacogenomic correlations and drug-timing implications are inferred from cross-sectional expression data; no experimental validation of timing-optimized drug delivery is performed in this study.
- **Paralog interaction analysis is correlational:** Combined RORA+RORC pathway associations are derived from expression correlations in patient samples; specific synergistic mechanisms are inferred rather than tested.
- **Time-series data are limited to two cell lines:** The MCF7/MCF10A oscillation comparison (GSE76370) and MEF CRY2 knockdown (GSE89018) are informative but represent a narrow slice of cancer cell biology.
