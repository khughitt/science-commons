## Key Findings

1. **Scale of rare-variant landscape.** Among 287,917 UKB participants, 96% of genes had at least one heterozygous PTV carrier; 42% had at least one compound heterozygous or homozygous/hemizygous PTV carrier; only 20% had at least one homozygous/hemizygous PTV carrier. Only 884 genes (4.7%) had PTVs with MAF > 0.5%, illustrating the predominance of rare functional variation.

2. **Variant-level ExWAS identifies 5,193 binary and 41,754 quantitative associations.** Effect sizes for rare variants were substantially higher than for common variants (Wilcoxon P = 1.1 × 10⁻³⁷; Fig. 1c). 83% of significant rare variants arose from MAF < 0.5%. Three significant variant types spanned PTV, missense, and dominant/recessive models.

3. **Collapsing analyses: 1,703 gene-phenotype associations for binary traits, 767 for quantitative traits.** Median OR = 12.4 for binary traits; median absolute beta = 0.35 for quantitative traits. 83% of binary and 58% of quantitative collapsing associations were not detectable via single-variant ExWAS — demonstrating the additive power of collapsing under high allelic heterogeneity.

4. **Strong enrichment for FDA-approved drug targets.** Binary collapsing OR for drug targets = 7.38 (95% CI 3.71–13.59, P = 1.46 × 10⁻⁷); quantitative OR = 3.71 (95% CI 2.28–5.76, P = 4.5 × 10⁻⁸). OMIM OR = 5.95 (95% CI 4.90–7.23). 88.6% of significant collapsing associations for binary phenotypes were supported by ClinVar pathogenic/likely pathogenic annotations.

5. **Missense tolerance ratio (MTR) improves signal-to-noise.** 15% (133/878) of binary significant relationships and 55% of quantitative associations emerged from MTR-informed missense models. For phenotypes significant in both MTR and non-MTR versions, effect sizes were significantly higher in MTR-informed models (Mann-Whitney P = 0.006).

6. **Notable large-effect biology-specific findings.** FLG (filaggrin) PTVs in haematological/immune phenotypes (dermatitis P = 5.1 × 10⁻⁹⁵; asthma P = 3.1 × 10⁻³²; melanoma P = 4.7 × 10⁻¹³; basal cell carcinoma P = 9.9 × 10⁻³⁰) — carriers showed increased skin cancer risk attributed to UV sensitivity from impaired skin barrier. APOB and PCSK9 (protective PTVs with LDL reduction). PKD1/PKD2 (polycystic kidney). HBB (thalassaemia). APC (colorectal). JAK2, ANK1, SPTB (haematological). TET2, ASXL1, SRSF2, SF3B1 (haematological/clonal).

7. **Somatic variant detection as a byproduct.** Seven genes significantly associated with haematological malignancies carried variants that appeared to be somatic (rather than germline), including IGLL5 — supporting the potential of blood-based sequencing to incidentally detect somatic clonal events.

8. **Pan-ancestry analysis adds 26 unique gene-phenotype signals and improves 59 quantitative trait associations.** Most prominent: PTVs in HBB associated with thalassaemia in South Asian ancestry (P = 2.7 × 10⁻⁴⁶; OR = 176.4, 95% CI 84.1–369.7). 20 European-significant associations lost significance in the combined pan-ancestry model. For quantitative traits, pan-ancestry added 59 associations not seen in Europeans, including rare OCA2 variants and younger age of wearing glasses (β = −0.45, P = 4.7 × 10⁻¹⁰) and ASGR1 variants associated with reduced LDL cholesterol.

9. **Expected false positives at threshold.** Under P ≤ 2 × 10⁻⁹ across 46,947 total significant collapsing associations, the expected number of false positives is 18 — an extremely low false discovery rate at this scale.

## Limitations

- **European-ancestry dominance.** 95.7% of the sequenced cohort is of European ancestry. Non-European ancestries have reduced power (n = 1,475–5,714 per group) and higher MAF thresholds for detectable associations, creating a health-equity gap in the rare-variant map.
- **Fisher's exact test framework.** Cannot adjust for covariates; relies on sex-matched case-control construction and ancestry pruning. Regression-based methods (SAIGE, REGENIE) are complementary and sometimes more powerful, though Phred score correlation between Fisher's and REGENIE 2.0.2 is r = 0.99 for common signals and r = 0.94 overall.
- **Binary phenotype definitions are ICD-10-chapter-mapped.** Chapter mapping is most useful for diagnostic rather than procedural codes; surgical procedure codes can contaminate chapter-level phenotypes with procedure-selection effects rather than disease biology.
- **No longitudinal follow-up in this study.** Survival, disease progression, or age-of-onset differences by genotype are not modelled.
- **Somatic variant confound.** The pipeline is tuned for germline variants; seven haematological signals driven by apparently somatic variants were detected incidentally and flagged, but the systematic scope of somatic contamination is unquantified.
- **Summary statistics only (public release).** Full individual-level genotype-phenotype data require UK Biobank data access agreement; the public AZPheWAS portal provides summary statistics only, limiting integration into custom pipelines without UKB access.
- **ICD-10 to MeSH crosswalk not provided.** Direct comparison with PubTator's MeSH-keyed disease similarity matrices requires an additional mapping step not supplied by the paper.
