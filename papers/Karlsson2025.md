## Key Findings

- Pediatric cancer relapse often reflects treatment-filtered clonal evolution, including additional mutations at relapse, loss of diagnostic variants, and relapse from minor or suppressed subclones.
- Rebiopsy at relapse and molecular comparison to the primary tumor are framed as essential whenever treatment options remain.
- ctDNA and liquid biopsy are promising for relapse monitoring and evolutionary tracking but still have sensitivity, compartment-shedding, and resolution limits.
- The paper proposes an oncology OODA loop: observe, orient, decide, and act using repeated clinical, imaging, and molecular measurements.
- It groups evolution-based therapies into adaptive therapy, extinction therapy/secondary strikes, and reflexive control/evolutionary steering.
- The authors stress inherent prediction limits: resistance variants may be too rare to detect at baseline, relapses may seed from single cells, and advanced metastatic disease may have too many routes to resistance.

## Limitations

The review is pediatric-cancer focused, so its clinical priorities do not automatically generalize to adult metastatic cancers.
It is also primarily conceptual and evidence-synthetic rather than a formal analysis of monitoring performance.
The OODA model is useful, but it still depends on unresolved measurement constraints.
