## Key Findings

- The review frames lethal cancer as convergent evolution toward metastasis plus resistance, despite patient- and tumor-specific heterogeneity.
- It classifies four major eco-evolutionary paths to therapy resistance: phenotypic heterogeneity, evolutionary triage, epigenetic plasticity, and proliferative dormancy.
- It argues that treating all resistance as a targetable mutation is insufficient because dormant or plastic cells may evade antiproliferative therapies without requiring a new fixed mutation.
- It maps practical strategies onto ecological and evolutionary principles: double-bind therapy, adaptive therapy, early removal of oligometastatic "invasive species," targeting dormant disseminated cells, destroying supportive tumor ecosystems, shifting selective pressure, and triggering immunogenic cell death.
- The adaptive therapy section emphasizes maintaining sensitive competitors, lowering cumulative exposure, and using continuous biomarker or imaging monitoring plus patient-specific models.
- The review predicts a clinical need for evolutionary tumor boards that complement molecular tumor boards.

## Limitations

This is a narrative review and perspective, so its taxonomy should be treated as an organizing framework rather than direct empirical evidence for any one intervention.
Several examples come from specific cancers or clinical contexts and need disease-specific validation before being generalized.
The ecological analogies are useful for hypothesis generation but should not substitute for mechanistic or clinical evidence.
