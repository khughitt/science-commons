## Key Findings

### Molecular backdrop
The mammalian circadian clock consists of interlocking TTFLs. The positive arm (CLOCK:BMAL1/NPAS2 heterodimer) activates PER and CRY transcription; accumulated PER:CRY:CK1δ/ε complex represses CLOCK:BMAL1, completing the ~24 h cycle. An auxiliary REV-ERB/ROR loop (NR1D1/2 and RORA/RORB) regulates BMAL1 expression. A third DBP-linked loop interlocks via D-box elements. CK1δ and CK1ε regulate PER nuclear localisation and degradation via phosphorylation.

### Sleep disorders
- **FASP (familial advanced sleep phase):** rs121908635 in PER2 (Ser662Gly) disrupts a CK1-priming phosphorylation site, destabilising PER2 and shortening period to ~23.3 h. rs104894561 in CK1δ (Thr44Ala) reduces CK1δ enzymatic activity, recapitulating FASP in humans. rs201220841 in CRY2 (Ala260Thr) prevents FAD binding and promotes CRY2–FBXL3 ubiquitin-mediated degradation.
- **DSPD (delayed sleep phase disorder):** rs184039278 in CRY1 (intronic, causes exon 11 skipping producing CRY1Δ11) increases CRY1 affinity for CLOCK:BMAL1, lengthening period; frequency ~0.6% in the human population. PER3 rs150812083 and rs139315125 (Pro415Ala and His417Arg) associate with FASP and seasonal affective disorder (SAD).

### Psychiatric conditions
- **Major depressive disorder (MDD) / SAD / bipolar disorder:** BMAL1 rs2279287 (5′-UTR) associates with SAD in Indian family study. CLOCK rs1801260 (3′-UTR) enhances CLOCK expression under glucocorticoid signalling, is protective against MDD in males only, and associates with sleep duration, quality, ADHD symptoms, and sleep disturbances linked to mood. CRY2 rs10838524 and rs10838527 (3′-UTR) associate with seasonal depression and dysthymia across large Finnish and Swedish cohorts (n up to 5910). PER3 VNTR rs57875989 (4-repeat vs. 5-repeat allele of an 18-aa motif) strongly associates with depression and bipolar disorder.
- **ADHD:** CLOCK rs11931061, rs11932595, and rs3817444 associate with inattention and ADHD symptoms. CRY1 Δ11 and Δ6 variants implicate DSPD/ADHD overlap via shared BMAL1:CLOCK affinity.
- **Addiction:** CK1ε rs1534891 associates with bipolar disorder and drug addiction; polymorphisms alter CK1ε regulation of the DARPP-32/dopamine signalling cascade.

### Cancer susceptibility
- **Breast cancer:** CLOCK rs11943456 and rs3749474 associate with increased and decreased risk, respectively, across a 96,756-subject meta-analysis, stratified by shiftwork and menopausal status. BMAL1 rs2290035 and rs969485 confer increased risk when combined with night-shift work. CRY1 rs1056560 (3′-UTR, near miR-300/miR-381 binding sites) is protective in premenopausal women via elevated CRY1 mRNA.
- **Prostate cancer:** CRY1 rs7297614 (5′-UTR) associates with increased risk in the Icelandic population. CK1ε rs1534891 and rs1997644 associate with prostate cancer, potentially via altered testosterone/dihydrotestosterone ratios.
- **Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC):** CRY1 rs3809236 (transcription factor binding region) associates with improved survival and decreased recurrence. PER3 rs2640908 (exonic splicer enhancer) associates with increased HCC survival and lower prostate cancer risk.
- **Non-Hodgkin lymphoma:** CRY2 rs1401417, rs11038689, and rs7123390 associate with increased risk through immune/hematologic pathway dysregulation; CRY2 knockdown in osteosarcoma decreases TP53 and increases c-MYC and CCND1, enhancing proliferation. CRY2 Asp325His and Ser510Leu missense mutations identified in TCGA promote c-MYC expression and reduce CLOCK:BMAL1 transcriptional activity.
- **Lung cancer:** BMAL1 rs2290035 and rs3816360 associate with elevated lung cancer risk in a Northeast Chinese cohort.

### Metabolic disorders
- CLOCK rs4580704 (intronic) reduces type 2 diabetes risk and lowers body weight, hyperglycaemia, inflammation, and dyslipidaemia across multiple European cohorts.
- BMAL1 rs11022775 and rs7950226 (promoter functional haplotypes) alter expression and are linked to hypertension and type 2 diabetes aetiology.
- CRY1 and CRY2 SNPs implicated in regulation of liver triglycerides (rs8192440 for CRY1, rs11605924 for CRY2) and glucose tolerance.
- PER2 rs35333999 associates with both evening chronotype and myocardial infarction, and led to increased body temperature and melatonin levels indicating a shifted circadian period.
- CRY2 rs10838524 associates with risk of elevated fasting glycaemia and reduced liver fat content in large German cohort (n = 2500).

### Cardiovascular and autoimmune
- PER2 rs10462023 is a risk factor for depression, schizophrenia, and ischaemic stroke.
- PER2 rs2304674 associates with rheumatoid arthritis (RA) via bidirectional relationship between inflammation and circadian gene expression; rs11894491 associates with systemic lupus erythematosus.
- CLOCK SNPs associate with cardiovascular risk, obesity, and dyslipidaemia (rs10002541, rs1801260, rs3749474).

### Infertility
- CLOCK rs11932595 and rs6811520 associate with male infertility in European cohorts. rs3817444 also associates with male infertility in Chinese and Japanese populations, with elevated testosterone and FSH suggesting a hormonal mechanism.

### Methodological limitations (Concluding Remarks)
- Most variants are identified by association, not confirmed by functional mechanism in animal models.
- Intronic GWAS loci do not necessarily act on the nearest gene; variant-to-gene mapping is required.
- SNPs act as predisposition indicators, not diagnostic causes; environmental factors (shiftwork, light exposure, lifestyle) modulate penetrance substantially.
- Population stratification remains a major confounder; large diverse biobanks (e.g., UK Biobank) are needed for allele frequency validation.
- The review is not exhaustive: hundreds to thousands of SNPs exist for each gene in NCBI SNP Database; only those with clinical evidence are included.

## Limitations

- All associations are observational unless Mendelian (FASP/DSPD examples); causal direction is rarely established.
- Heavy sampling of East Asian and European populations; African, South Asian, and admixed population data are sparse.
- Many entries in Tables 1–3 list OR without confidence intervals or small case/control n, reducing reliability.
- Post-translational clock mechanisms (phosphorylation, ubiquitination) are emphasised; epigenetic and non-coding RNA clock layers are not reviewed.
- The review does not assess polygenic scores or epistasis; treating each SNP in isolation ignores combinatorial clock-gene effects.
- No sex-disaggregated analysis is provided for most SNPs despite known sex-dimorphic clock biology.
- Cancer associations span very heterogeneous phenotypes (stage, receptor status, shiftwork exposure) that are not always distinguished.
