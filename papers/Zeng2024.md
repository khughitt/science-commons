## One-Sentence Summary

A phenome-wide comparison of 287,012 All of Us participants against US GBD estimates and 502,477 UK Biobank participants finds that most diseases are more prevalent in All of Us than in either reference, yet PheWAS effect-size correlations across the 9 tested diseases are moderate-to-strong (r = 0.40–0.61, except cystic fibrosis r = 0.24), supporting cross-cohort generalizability of disease-association findings.

## Key Findings

1. **Most diseases are more prevalent in All of Us than in the US general population.** Of 314 GBD-curated diseases, 254 (80.9%) were more common in All of Us (prevalence ratio PR > 1.1, P < 2×10⁻⁵), spanning pregnancy-related disorders, neoplasms, congenital, digestive, and circulatory conditions. The smallest PR was for G6PD deficiency; the largest PRs were for thalassemia, appendicitis, and acute hepatitis C (PR > 100 in some states).

2. **Most diseases are also more prevalent in All of Us than in UK Biobank.** Of 2,515 shared phecodes, 2,152 (85.6%) were more prevalent in All of Us (PR > 1.1, P < 2×10⁻⁵) spanning 17 disease categories. A total of 144 phecodes were more prevalent in UK Biobank (PR < 0.91, P < 2×10⁻⁵), including colorectal cancer, breast cancer, esophageal cancer, cardiac cancer, Alzheimer's disease/dementias, and transplantations.

3. **Rare diseases show dramatically amplified enrichment in All of Us.** The mean rare-disease PR (All of Us vs US national) was 19.97 — far exceeding the PR for common diseases. States without healthcare provider organizations (HPOs) had particularly high rare-disease PRs (all states with PR > 100 are non-HPO states), reflecting an "EHR data gap": residents with rare diseases in non-HPO states travel to HPO sites for care, so only their EHR data are captured.

4. **PheWAS effect sizes are largely reproducible between cohorts.** Pearson correlation coefficients of PheWAS betas between All of Us and UK Biobank were: ischemic heart disease 0.61, lung cancer 0.50, COPD 0.60, Alzheimer's disease/other dementias 0.57, colorectal cancer 0.40, low back pain 0.53, lupus 0.47, multiple sclerosis 0.46, and cystic fibrosis 0.24. The notably low cystic fibrosis correlation (r = 0.24 vs median ~0.50) was attributed to different CFTR variant frequencies and survivorship selection in UK Biobank (minimum enrollment age 40 excludes severe pediatric presentations).

5. **Known clinical associations are replicated in both cohorts.** All tested known correlates were replicated with significant odds ratios in both All of Us and UK Biobank (Table 3). Effect sizes were broadly comparable, e.g., hypertension→ischemic heart disease: OR 6.60 (AoU) vs 6.25 (UKB); depression→dementia: OR 4.05 (AoU) vs 5.10 (UKB); tobacco use→COPD: OR 7.64 (AoU) vs 9.48 (UKB).

6. **Demographic profile differences explain much of the prevalence gap.** All of Us participants are more likely to be Black/African American (19.9% vs 1.6%), Hispanic/Latino (18.8% vs ~0%), younger (mean 51.7 vs 56.5 years), with higher BMI (30.0 vs 27.3) and shorter EHR record length (11.6 vs 22.3 years) than UK Biobank participants. The higher disease burden reflects intentional recruitment of underrepresented populations and enrollment through healthcare provider organizations (enriching for disease-seeking individuals).

7. **Race/ethnicity drives some high-PR conditions.** Thalassemia enrichment was primarily driven by individuals identifying as Asian, Black/African American, and Hispanic/Latino; appendicitis enrichment was driven by individuals identifying as Hispanic/Latino.

## Limitations

- **EHR-based phenotyping limitations.** Billing-code-based phenotyping (ICD → phecodes) misses diseases requiring genetic, laboratory, or imaging confirmation (e.g., G6PD deficiency, male infertility). Dental and neonatal/pediatric conditions are absent from most All of Us EHRs. OMOP harmonization reduces but does not eliminate cross-site coding heterogeneity.
- **All of Us is not a representative sample.** Participants are enrolled at healthcare provider organizations with intentional enrichment for historically underrepresented groups — the cohort is explicitly not population-representative. Disease prevalence estimates from All of Us should not be used as US incidence or prevalence estimates.
- **Age-at-enrollment selection (UK Biobank).** UK Biobank enrolls only ages 40–69, creating survivorship bias for early-onset or severe conditions (e.g., cystic fibrosis, childhood cancers). This explains the cystic fibrosis PheWAS outlier (r = 0.24).
- **All of Us data currently limited to HPO-contributing states.** Participants in states without HPOs lack EHR data; the apparent rare-disease enrichment in non-HPO states is a capture artifact.
- **Short follow-up in All of Us.** EHR records have a mean length of only 11.6 years (vs 22.3 in UKB) and last follow-up was July 2022 — incident disease ascertainment is limited relative to UK Biobank's longer record horizon.
- **No causal inference.** Cross-sectional design; PheWAS associations are correlational.
