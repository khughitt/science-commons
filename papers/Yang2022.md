## Key Findings

### Subclonal dynamics and fitness

- Most KP tumors contained one or two expanding subclones; expanding cells had significantly higher CNV burden (Mann-Whitney U, p < 0.0001) and cell-cycle scores, and significantly higher single-cell fitness scores (p < 0.0001) versus non-expanding cells.
- Fitness-score predictors (tumor age, tree depth, cell count, unique lineage states) individually explained only 9–28% of fitness variance (R² 0.09–0.28); an additive linear model with all covariates explained R² = 0.52, indicating substantial unexplained variance from intrinsic subclone properties.
- CNV profiles of expansions from the same tumor were often distinct (Figure S2K), confirming independent origins.

### Fitness-associated transcriptional programs (FitnessSignature)

- Fifteen transcriptional Leiden clusters span an AT2-like → Gastric-like / Endoderm-like / Lung Mixed → Mesenchymal trajectory.
- Three non-overlapping fitness gene modules: Module 1 = Gastric/Endoderm (Tff1, Hnf4a, Gkn2); Module 2 = Early EMT (Hmga2, Inhba, Gap43); Module 3 = Classical Mesenchymal/pro-metastatic (Vim, Twist1, Cdh2, Runx2, Cd109, Foxg1).
- FitnessSignature highest in Mesenchymal clusters; lowest in AT2-like cluster. Mesenchymal fitness gene expression significantly associated with poor prognosis in TCGA LUAD (log-rank p = 5e-4).
- Fitness-module usage was tumor-specific: individual expansions preferentially employed one module, though some showed module coexpression.

### Plasticity — EffectivePlasticity dynamics

- Tumor cells showed widely varying scEffectivePlasticity scores; AT2-like and Mesenchymal states exhibited the lowest plasticity (most stable), while High-Plasticity, Gastric-like, and Endoderm-like states had elevated EffectivePlasticity.
- **Critical temporal pattern:** EffectivePlasticity was maximized at intermediate FitnessSignature scores and minimized at both low and high extremes. This supports a model in which: (1) loss of AT2-like state triggers transient, high-rate parallel transitions to multiple novel states; (2) high-fitness states are subsequently stabilized and clonally swept; (3) terminal Mesenchymal states are the most stable endpoint.
- The High-Plasticity cell state (Leiden cluster 5, previously described by Marjanovic et al. 2020) had the highest mean EffectivePlasticity across the tumor.

### Stereotyped evolutionary paths — Fate Clusters

- EvoCoupling analysis resolved three Fate Clusters (FC1, FC2, FC3) grouping tumors by their predominant cell-state transition patterns. FC1 and FC2 represent two parallel, non-overlapping trajectories both departing from the AT2 state:
  - **FC1 (Gastric/Endoderm route):** AT2 → Early Gastric → Gastric-like → (via EMT) → Mesenchymal; late marker: Gkn2.
  - **FC2 (Lung Mixed/AT1 route):** AT2 → AT1-like / Lung Mixed → Gastric-like / High-Plasticity → EMT → Mesenchymal; late markers: Sox2, Scgb1a1 (airway progenitor); Cd24a, Itgb4 (tumor-propagating cells).
  - **FC3:** dominated by high-fitness EMT/Mesenchymal states from the outset; tumors in FC3 may have entered EMT from either FC1 or FC2 trajectories.
- Principal components of EvoCoupling explained ~32% of observed variance; FC1/FC2 couplings were the strongest discriminators between tumors.

### Tumor suppressor perturbation (Lkb1, Apc) — altered paths

- **KPL (Lkb1 KO):** progression stabilized rapidly to a Pre-EMT state (Leiden cluster 9, uniquely stabilized); reduced EffectivePlasticity in Pre-EMT relative to KP; differentially expressed genes include Sox17, Stmn2, Krt14 (up) and Gata6, Tnfaip2 (down). Unique trajectory: fast progression + Pre-EMT stability → EMT → Mesenchymal.
- **KPA (Apc KO):** progression via novel Apc-specific states (Wnt-active: Axin2+, Notum+, Nkd1+ clusters; Apc early, Apc mesenchymal); unique trajectory: AT2 → Apc-early → Early EMT/Mesenchymal. Wnt upregulation consistent with Apc's negative regulatory role.
- Both perturbations increased tumor burden but did not significantly change the number or relative size of subclonal expansions; fitness gene modules were largely genotype-specific, suggesting altered evolutionary landscape rather than accelerated general progression.

### Metastatic origins — spatial-phylogenetic analysis

- Metastases consistently originated from expanding subclones within the primary tumor, across KP, KPL, and KPA genotypes (phylogenetic distance of metastases to expanding subclones was significantly shorter than to non-expanding regions, p < 0.0001, one-sided Mann-Whitney U).
- Multiregion analysis of one primary tumor (3724_NT_T1) with 4 metastases (3 liver, 1 soft tissue) showed that: individual metastases originated from distinct spatial locations within the primary; liver metastases were significantly more similar (transcriptionally) to their originating expansion than soft tissue metastasis was to its origin (p < 0.0001), suggesting transcriptional drift or state change during colonization.
- Evidence of multisubclonal seeding from one primary tumor to the lymph node, and cross-seeding from one metastasis to another primary tumor, was observed (Figures S7A–S7C).
- Conclusion: tumor progression at the primary site (expansion, fitness gain) is a prerequisite for metastatic competence; the data argue against early systemic dissemination as the predominant metastatic route in this KP model.

## Limitations

1. **No direct temporal sampling.** All tumors are harvested at a single time point (5–6 months post-induction). Transient states and intermediate phylogenetic branches are inferred from the tree, not observed longitudinally. The direction of transitions is probabilistic (EvoCoupling), not directly observed.
2. **Fitness-inference assumption.** Single-cell fitness scores assume that observed tree topology reflects Darwinian selection (the Williams et al. 2018 neutral null model). High CNV burden or genomic instability that generates tree asymmetry could inflate fitness scores for non-selective reasons; the paper partially addresses this by showing CNV concordance, but cannot fully exclude it.
3. **GEMM vs. human tumors.** KP tumors have lower somatic mutation burden and faster developmental timescale than human LUAD; fitness gene modules and plasticity dynamics may not quantitatively translate. The TCGA cross-validation (FitnessSignature survival association) provides partial human support but is correlational.
4. **EffectivePlasticity measures frequency, not directionality.** The statistic captures how often cells change state within a subclone but cannot determine whether transitions are reversible (true plasticity) or irreversible (progressive differentiation). State re-entry to the AT2 state is not demonstrated.
5. **Tumor suppressor scope.** Only Lkb1 and Apc were tested; other common LUAD TSGs (STK11-distinct, KEAP1, RB1, CDKN2A) may create yet further distinct evolutionary trajectories not captured by the three fate clusters.
6. **Metastasis sample size.** Detailed spatial-phylogenetic analysis is available for only one primary-tumor family (3724_NT_T1) at full resolution; five additional families provide supporting data but with less resolution on transcriptional drift post-dissemination.
7. **Immunocompetent?** The paper does not explicitly address immune context in the KP-Tracer mice; the mESC chimera approach could introduce partial immune tolerance to mESC-derived antigens. Immune selection pressure on expanding subclones is not modeled.
