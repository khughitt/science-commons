## Key Findings

1. **A straight-line LFIM does not imply neutral competition.** 2D spatial Moran simulations with as many as 25% non-neutral mutations produce a straight-line LFIM once the tissue is colonised predominantly by large non-neutral clones — the surviving small neutral clones then dominate the tail of the distribution, which retains an exponential shape. The signal of non-neutrality disappears from the LFIM precisely when clonal takeover is most advanced.

2. **Biopsy sampling further masks non-neutrality.** Isolated punch biopsies (as used in the eyelid study) truncate the upper tail of clone sizes, reducing the AUC of LFIM as a neutral/non-neutral classifier from 0.94 (full data, 1% non-neutral) to 0.68 (biopsy + sequencing). When 25% of mutations are non-neutral, biopsy sampling reduces the AUC to 0.52 — indistinguishable from random guessing.

3. **Human oesophagus shows non-neutral signatures when measured appropriately.** Re-analysis using gridded biopsies (which capture larger clones) produces LFIMs with systematically higher curvature (lower R²) than isolated-punch sampling of the same data would yield. Older individuals show greater LFIM deviation, consistent with age-related clonal takeover by positively selected mutants.

4. **Clone size correlates with dN/dS in most but not all genes.** For four of five strongly selected genes in the oesophagus (TP53, NOTCH1, NOTCH3, FAT1), median VAF scales with dN/dS ratio, consistent with stronger selection driving larger clones. NOTCH2 is an exception: high dN/dS but synonymous-level clone sizes — possibly reflecting initial expansion followed by context-dependent growth constraint by neighbouring clones.

5. **Combining LFIM with dN/dS substantially improves classification.** Neither metric alone is definitive; orthogonal approaches together provide a high-confidence inference of non-neutral competition, and inconsistency between methods can itself be informative (e.g. NOTCH2).

6. **Simulations confirm non-neutrality prevalence of eyelid tissue.** By the end of simulations (≈58 years), almost all tissue in the 25%-non-neutral scenario is colonised by non-neutral clones, meaning the straight-line LFIM observed in the original eyelid study is fully consistent with widespread positive selection hidden by clonal saturation.

## Limitations

- **No longitudinal data.** The oesophageal and eyelid datasets are cross-sectional snapshots of different individuals; clonal dynamics over time are inferred from simulations, not tracked prospectively. Age-stratified patterns are suggestive but indirect.
- **Single-progenitor model assumed.** The stochastic model assumes one class of proliferating progenitor; tissues with hierarchical stem-cell structure or transit-amplifying cell pools may behave differently.
- **Fitness drawn from a simple normal distribution.** Selection coefficients are sampled N~N(0.1, 0.1) in non-neutral simulations; real fitness landscapes are gene-specific and mutation-specific, with epistatic dependencies not captured.
- **dN/dS limitations acknowledged.** The method is sensitive to the neutral mutation model used and can be misleading for genes where gain- and loss-of-function mutations have opposite fitness effects (e.g. NOTCH2). The authors note this explicitly but do not resolve it.
- **Detection floor censors small clones.** The minimum VAF detectable at 1000× depth means that very recent or very small clones are systematically missed; the estimated clone size distributions are right-censored at the lower end.
- **Restricted gene panel.** Both datasets used targeted sequencing of ~74 genes; driver mutations in unsequenced genes are invisible, meaning total non-neutral clone burden is underestimated.
- **Scope is epidermis and oesophagus.** Generalisability to glandular epithelia, haematopoietic tissue, or other pre-cancer contexts (e.g. Barrett's, MGUS, MDS) requires additional validation.
