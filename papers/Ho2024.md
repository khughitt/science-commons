## Key Findings

### 1. Alternating clonal dominance (ACD) at the primary tumour

Contrary to most theoretical models predicting linear fitness gain or loss, primary-tumour clonal dynamics were highly non-linear. Across 12 discrete trajectory clusters, many barcodes showed oscillating fitness: initial gains followed by losses, dormancy followed by bursts, sigmoidal progression reversed mid-study. ACD was documented across all quartiles of final clonal abundance and was confirmed in KPC immunocompetent models, ruling out an immunodeficiency artefact. The 934 barcodes common to all primary tumours were stable (Pearson R = 0.990–0.998 through week 10; R = 0.714 at week 14), confirming oCRT homogeneity while demonstrating progressive divergence.

**Operational definition of clonal dominance:** Relative representation (%) of a barcode at a given time point; "dominance" means a lineage transiently holds the largest share of tumour mass. ACD = the repeated exchange of which lineage occupies that top position over time.

### 2. Most disseminated clones undergo abortive colonisation

Large fractions of primary tumour clones could be detected in peripheral tissues (liver: ~47% of the 438 subclones, lung: comparable proportions) but without forming macroscopic metastatic nodes. In PATC69, 70% of clones that seeded the liver were abortive (150/213); in the lung, 48% (98/206) were abortive. Abortive colonisation was defined by transient expansion followed by counter-selection, consistent with the "seed and soil" model rather than universal fitness.

### 3. Fitness in the primary tumour predicts metastatic dissemination — but is not sufficient

Subclonal abundance at all early time points (weeks 4, 6, 10) positively correlated with eventual dissemination at week 14. The top-fitness clones in the primary tumour were enriched among disseminating clones. However, multiple exceptions existed: some high-abundance primary-tumour clones were never found in the periphery; some low-abundance clones disseminated. This indicates that cell-intrinsic factors beyond primary-tumour representation contribute to metastatic competence.

### 4. Clonal dominance at metastatic sites: largely monoclonal, established pre-seeding

LCM of liver metastatic lesions: 10 of 12 visible lesions were **monoclonal** — each sustained by a single barcode lineage that also dominated a specific region of the primary tumour. 2 of 12 were oligoclonal (each dominated by one lineage; 2 were polyclonal from a cluster of spatially proximate primary-tumour cells). The same 3 dominant clones sustained 8 of 10 monoclonal lesions.

**Critical finding for q031:** Seeding is polyclonal (many clones reach the liver), but metastatic colony formation is monoclonal. Clonal dominance at the met is therefore not a result of post-seeding competition among multiple co-arriving clones; it reflects which specific clone had the intrinsic capacity to colonise, with the rest undergoing abortive expansion.

### 5. Long-term self-renewal (LTSR) is the cell-intrinsic property

Only ~333 barcodes out of 202,701 original lineages (0.2%) were present in both F4 primary tumours (after 4 serial transplantations) and in liver/lung metastases. These F4∩Mets lineages comprised only 0.2% of the initial population yet accounted for ~96% of F4 primary tumour mass, ~99% of liver metastases, and ~89% of lung metastases. Only ~31% of LTSR-endowed cells actually disseminated, indicating LTSR is necessary but not sufficient for successful colonisation.

### 6. NMDA/glutamatergic signalling enriched in pro-metastatic subclones

Bulk RNA-seq of 3 pro-met vs. 3 non-met isogenic PATC124 subclones identified 387 differentially expressed genes. IPA showed enrichment in glutamatergic synaptic transmission (NMDA receptor, AMPA receptor) and neurovascular coupling. qRT-PCR confirmed upregulation of *GRIN2A*, *GRIA2*, and *CAMK4* in pro-met vs. non-met clones. Pro-met clones showed higher basal intracellular Ca²⁺ and greater in vitro invasion, which was suppressed dose-dependently by memantine (25–100 μM). In vivo memantine treatment (biclonal co-injection of M-1 + M-2) reduced liver metastatic burden; primary tumour volume was also reduced, preventing clean attribution to anti-metastatic effect alone.

### 7. Pro-metastatic transcriptomic signature predicts PDAC survival

A 200-gene signature (100 met-up + 100 met-down genes) applied via ssGSEA to TCGA + META-PRISM (n = 223): met_up_dominant group had significantly worse overall survival (HR = 1.31, 95% CI 1.07–1.64, p = 0.008) versus met_down_dominant (HR = 0.52, 95% CI 0.41–2.65, p < 0.001). ~13% of parental PATC124 cells (453/3397) were enriched in the metastasis signature by scRNA-seq; 4.5% of tumour cells (91/2019) in a human PDAC patient without lymphovascular invasion; 31.8% (448/1408) in a patient with histopathological lymphovascular invasion.

## Limitations

- **Immunodeficient host:** All PDX oCRT studies used NSG mice (no T or B or NK cells). Immune editing of the clonal hierarchy — which likely shapes ACD and LTSR in human tumours — is absent. KPC experiments confirmed ACD in immunocompetent settings but did not replicate the full dissemination tracking or LTSR assay.
- **Single primary tumour line (PATC124) as the main quantitative system:** PATC69 and PATC108 served as corroboration; detailed LCM/spatial/LTSR analyses were done only in PATC124. Patient-level generalisability is limited by n = 3 PDX lines.
- **Memantine confound:** In vivo memantine reduced both liver metastases and primary tumour volume, making it impossible to cleanly attribute the anti-metastatic effect to suppression of dissemination vs. suppression of primary fitness of pro-met clones.
- **LTSR ≠ metastatic potential with 1:1 mapping:** ~69% of LTSR-endowed clones did not disseminate. The additional requirement beyond LTSR is not identified.
- **Temporal resolution of dominance transition at mets:** The study shows the final (week-14) clonal composition of liver lesions, not the kinetics by which monoclonal dominance was established. Whether it arose early (clone monopolises site from first seeding) or late (multiple clones initially co-colonise, one wins) is not directly resolved.
- **Barcode does not track genetic evolution:** ACD and LTSR are measured by barcode (clonal identity), not by subclonal genetic changes over time. The genetic basis of differential fitness among barcoded subclones remains unknown except for the 6 isolated clones analysed by WES.
