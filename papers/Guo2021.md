## Key Findings

**Mutational propensity as a phenotype:**
- MPs are quasi-normally distributed, robust to sequencing bias, and correlate strongly with raw MS activities for TMB (Spearman rho=0.822) and ITH (rho=0.795). Within-cancer-type MP profiles are highly consistent between TCGA tissues and CCLE cell lines (r=0.955), validating them as a genuine signature of tumor biology.
- Tumors with higher MP diversity show significantly increased clone count (P=7.00×10⁻⁴) and are significantly associated with tumor immune subtypes.

**Candidate genetic determinants (2,314 genes):**
- Of 17,027 genes evaluated, 87.5% (N=14,903) had at least one genetic status significantly associated with MPs (FDR<0.1); 2,314 pass the dual germline+somatic significance criterion to qualify as candidate driver genes.
- 1,268 (54.8%) act at pan-cancer level; 935 (40.4%) at cancer-specific level; 111 (4.8%) at both.
- Somatic nsy-mutations dominate variance explained (significant in 12,836 genes), followed by SCNAs (11,721) and TSS methylation (9,525). Germline contributions are smaller but additive. Effect sizes are largest for MP5 (POLE-related) where somatic nsy-mutations account for 1.77%–54.1% of variance.
- Benchmark validation: candidate genes include known pathogenic MMR genes (MBD4, BRCA1/2, MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, PMS2), recapitulating known mutagenic biology (MP1 ~ MBD4 truncation P=9.17×10⁻³, adj-R²=0.467; MP3 ~ PMS2 in LUAD P=0.0431).

**E-genes (expression-mediated, N=485):**
- 485 genes influence MPs through their expression levels (IV regression, FDR<0.1 and FDR_weak<0.1). Of these, 210 affect MP1 at pan-cancer level; 23 affect MP3.
- Enriched in: metabolism of RNA, RNA Pol III transcription, cell cycle, lipid metabolism. Includes well-known cancer drivers (BRCA1 for MP2, PKR3I for MP6) and novel ones (PPIP5K2 for MP1, SNX24 for MP6).
- E-genes are significantly enriched in oncogenes (CERES < −1.2, fold-enrichment=9.18; P=0.024) and have the highest fraction of drug-predictive genes among benchmark sets (70.5%, FDR<0.1).

**I-genes (immune-mediated, N=1,427):**
- 1,427 genes influence MPs via tumor-immune microenvironment (TIME) cell fractions (IV regression). Of 1,427, 797 act at pan-cancer level.
- Enriched in: cytokine signaling, innate immune system, EMT (FDR=2.59×10⁻⁷), adhesion (FDR=1.66×10⁻²²), lipid metabolism.
- TSS methylation associates with MP2 via neutrophil activity (25.2%, N=360); somatic nsy-mutations influence MP5 via T follicular helper cells (19.1%, N=273) and M1 macrophages (9.95%, N=142).
- I-genes show stronger association with ICI (anti-PD-1) therapy response than E-genes or canonical CGCs: predictive in 9.91% (melanoma) and 4.52% (gastric) of I-gene cases vs lower fractions for other gene sets.
- CREBBP (I-gene; HR=0.415, P=5.66×10⁻⁴) and PARP1 (I-gene; HR=0.169, P=0.0123) associated with better overall survival in 1,661 ICI-treated patients.

**Overlap and pleiotropic biology:**
- 350 of 485 E-genes are also I-genes, suggesting dual roles in cell-autonomous proliferation and immune modulation. Many are known CGCs (MSH2, MSH6, BRCA1, ABL2, MAPK6, NF1).
- I-genes with pleiotropic roles include EEF2 (humoral + cellular immunity, proliferation, angiogenesis, invasion), TYRO3 (T-cell proliferation + immune response), CXCR4 (proliferation + immunosuppressive T-cell recruitment).

**mpQTL analysis (noncoding germline variants):**
- 127 unique noncoding SNPs map to 6 MPs at genome-wide significance (P<5×10⁻⁸). Effect sizes comparable to coding variants. MP2 (APOBEC) is most influenced by mpQTLs (chromosomes 20q21.21, 15q21.2, 16q12.2; N=98 SNPs), followed by MP6.
- 13 mpQTLs act in cis via mRNA expression. Example: rs6060924 (COX4I2, chromosome 20) impacts MP2 (Warburg-related); rs35413356 (ALDH5A1) also impacts MP2.
- All 127 mpQTLs are known eQTL loci in cancer, confirming cis-gene expression as the mechanistic path.

**Clinical and drug implications:**
- E-genes' expression predicts IC₅₀ of 116 drugs (FDR<0.1). MP3 associates with BRAF inhibitor sensitivity (FDR=0.0781 and 0.0240); MP4 with HSP90 inhibitors; consistent with treatment-mutability link.
- In 60 LUAD patients receiving chemotherapy/targeted therapy, somatic mutation burden in 17 E-genes showed significant interaction with EGFR-targeted therapy clinical benefit (PR vs PD/SD; OR=2.26, P=0.0361).

## Limitations

- Ancestry restriction to CEU (northern/western European) excludes ~60% of global diversity; ASW and CHB validation is reassuring but underpowered for cancer-type-specific discovery.
- Combining all gain-of-function and loss-of-function somatic variants into a binary status masks directional effects (the authors acknowledge this: variants can cause gain or loss of signature activity, and the current analysis cannot control for functional consequence consistency).
- Seven MSs derived from 13 TCGA cancer types; rarer MSs found only in specific subtypes cannot be studied without introducing imbalance — the method is explicitly acknowledged as not applicable to rare cancer-type-specific signatures without subtype-specific cohorts.
- The regression framework identifies association and uses IV analysis for causality inference, but cannot exclude confounders absent from the model (age, irradiation history, microbiome). TCGA samples received heterogeneous treatments not captured in the analysis.
- TSS-methylation-driven epigenetic effects on MPs are informative but the directionality of cause (does signature activity alter methylation, or vice versa?) is not fully resolved for all I-gene pathways.
- The clinical cohort (N=60 LUAD) is small; E-gene x EGFR-therapy interaction (OR=2.26) should be treated as hypothesis-generating.
