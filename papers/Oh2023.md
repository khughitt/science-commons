## Key Findings

### Organ-specific aging heterogeneity

- Individuals with the same conventional age gap have markedly different organ-specific profiles (Fig. 1b). Mean pairwise inter-organ correlation r=0.29; conventional and organismal age gaps are the exception (r=0.98 to each other, indicating organ-nonspecific proteins dominate the conventional signature).
- ~18.4% of the population shows a strongly organ-specific ageotype dominated by a single organ; only 1.7% are multi-organ agers.
- Dominant ageotypes: kidney agers associate most strongly with metabolic diseases (diabetes, hypertension, obesity, hypercholesterolaemia); heart agers with atrial fibrillation and heart attack; muscle agers with gait impairment; brain ageotype with cerebrovascular disease; organismal ageotype with AD.

### Organ age gaps predict disease and mortality

- 65 of 117 (56%) organ–disease associations significant at population level (FDR-corrected meta-analysis); 23 of 117 significant as ageotype associations.
- **Kidney:** hypertension → kidney age gap +1 year (P=4.05×10⁻⁴⁰); diabetes → +1.3 years (P=1.15×10⁻²⁴). Key proteins: REN (renin), KL (klotho), UMOD (uromodulin, genetically linked to CKD), KAAG1.
- **Heart:** atrial fibrillation → +2.8 years (P=5.32×10⁻²¹); heart attack → +2.6 years (P=1.77×10⁻²⁰). Key proteins: NPPB (pro-BNP), TNNT2 (troponin T), MYL7, BMP10, PXDNL. Expressed primarily in atrial and ventricular cardiomyocytes.
- **Heart failure prediction (LonGenity longitudinal):** HR=2.37 per s.d. heart age gap over 15 years of follow-up (one s.d. ≈ 4.1 years of additional heart aging → 250% increased heart failure risk; q=7.07×10⁻⁷).
- **All-cause mortality (LonGenity):** 10 of 11 organ gaps plus organismal and conventional models significantly associated with future mortality (FDR-corrected). A s.d. increase in heart, adipose, liver, pancreas, brain, lung, immune, or muscle age gap each conferred 15–50% increased risk. Conventional HR=1.54 (q=2.27×10⁻¹⁰). Hazard ratios comparable in magnitude to DNAm GrimAge (HR=1.3, 14-year mortality) despite not being trained on mortality directly.
- **Clinical chemistry associations:** 226/559 (40%) organ gap vs. biochemistry marker associations significant. Strongest: liver gap × AST:ALT ratio (r=0.25, q=6.13×10⁻¹⁷); kidney gap × creatinine (r=0.23, q=1.65×10⁻¹⁶). Kidney gap remains significant for hypertension and diabetes even after correcting for eGFR.

### CognitionBrain aging model and Alzheimer's disease

- **FIBA algorithm applied to brain aging:** Proteins were scored for their contribution to both chronological age prediction and dementia severity (CDRGLOB). FIBA+ proteins (complexin 1/2 — CPLX1/2, neurexin 3 — NRXN3, stathmin 2 — STMN2, olfactomedin 1 — OLFM1) were used to train the CognitionBrain model; FIBA− proteins (ALDOC, NPTXR, CNDP1, LANCL1) were excluded.
- **CognitionBrain model performance:** Age prediction r=0.72 in training; replicates across cohorts. AD patients had ~2 additional years of CognitionBrain aging vs. controls (P=9.23×10⁻³⁶ in meta-analysis, n=AD 1,441 / controls 2,052).
- **Dementia progression (longitudinal):** CognitionBrain age gap conferred 34% increased risk (P=1.03×10⁻¹⁵) of a clinically meaningful 2-point increase in CDR-SB over 5 years.
- **Multivariate AD biomarker model (Stanford-ADRC):** CognitionBrain gap (HR=1.57, P=8.95×10⁻³) and plasma pTau-181 are additive (combined HR=2.08; concordance index=0.917). Combined CognitionBrain gap + pTau-181 2 s.d. above mean → 75% probability of dementia progression within 5 years; 2 s.d. below → <10%.
- **CognitionBrain protein biology:** 47/49 model proteins detected in human brain scRNA-seq. Dominant cell types: neurons and oligodendrocytes. Positive-weight proteins (complexins, NRXN3, STMN2, OLFM1) increase in blood with age/AD; decrease in AD brain tissue — consistent with synaptic/neurite protein shedding from degenerating neurons. ECM proteins TNR, NCAN, HS3ST4 also implicated.

### Organ aging and early cognitive decline: vascular and pancreatic axes

- FIBA applied to all 13 aging models identified CognitionArtery, CognitionBrain, CognitionOrganismal, and CognitionPancreas gaps as independently associated with AD in both ADRCs (meta-analysis P: organismal 2.03×10⁻²⁸, artery 6.02×10⁻¹⁶, pancreas 1.11×10⁻²¹).
- **CognitionOrganismal predicts cognitive decline earliest:** Top proteins (PTN, TAGLN, WISP2, CDCP1, CHRDL1) change with age at the fastest rate and earliest age; expressed by smooth muscle cells, pericytes, and perivascular fibroblasts in brain vasculature (scRNA-seq). These cell types are depleted in AD brain. Represents molecular signal of vascular neuroprotective-function loss preceding overt disease.
- **CognitionArtery = vascular calcification signature:** Five model proteins (TNFRSF11B/OPG, SOST, MRAP2, FRZB, MGP) are all strongly implicated in vascular calcification. TNFRSF11B KO mice show increased calcium deposition; MGP mutations cause soft-tissue calcification (Keutel syndrome); SOST and FRZB negatively regulate WNT signalling and drive calcification. CognitionOrganismal + CognitionArtery proteins form a StringDB interaction network involving ECM, cartilage development, and osteoblast signalling.
- **Longitudinal prediction of MCI conversion:** CognitionOrganismal (HR=1.17, P=0.02) and CognitionArtery (HR=1.15, P=0.04) predicted 15-year risk of conversion from normal cognition to MCI in Knight-ADRC (n=226 events, 940 individuals). CognitionBrain trended (HR=1.11, P=0.14).

### Tools released

- Python package `organage` for computing organ age gaps from SomaScan plasma proteomics data.

## Limitations

- All five cohorts skewed toward American/Caucasian ancestry (Knight-ADRC, Stanford-ADRC, LonGenity, SAMS, Covance); organ age models need recalibration in ethnically and geographically diverse populations.
- SomaScan platform-specific; organ-enrichment annotation yields only 18% organ-enriched proteins, leaving 82% in the non-organ-specific organismal pool. Important biological aging signals from cell–cell communication proteins that lack organ-specific expression may be missed.
- Aging models trained primarily on older adults (Knight-ADRC mean age 75); caution warranted when applying to younger populations. Nonlinear dynamics observed in plasma proteome are not captured by LASSO.
- Cross-sectional design limits causal inference — organ age gaps may reflect reverse causation (subclinical disease elevating apparent aging marker levels) rather than upstream aging-driven increase.
- Limited organ coverage: 11 organs chosen for data availability; reproductive organs and others are excluded. Whether FIBA-optimized clocks generalize to all diseases and organs is unknown.
- Cognitive outcome data (CDR, dementia diagnosis) available in Knight-ADRC and Stanford-ADRC only; mortality follow-up limited to LonGenity. Disease associations for some organs replicated in only one or two cohorts.
- The CognitionArtery prediction of MCI conversion (HR=1.15, P=0.04) is borderline after multiple testing, and CognitionBrain did not reach significance (P=0.14) for MCI conversion despite being the strongest AD cross-sectional predictor.
