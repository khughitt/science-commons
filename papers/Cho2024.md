## Key Findings

### The case against stem cells as sole cancer origin

- Most adult organs (pancreas, kidney, liver, prostate, lung alveoli) do not have dedicated constitutively mitotic stem cell populations; differentiated cells maintain tissue census primarily by autoduplication — symmetric division of a mature cell without reverting to a stem state.
- Stem cells, when they do divide and differentiate both daughters, transfer mutations to cells destined for elimination, not accumulation. The "immortal strand" hypothesis lacks consistent in vivo support.
- Adult stem cell populations are not homogeneous rapid-cycling entities; many are long-term quiescent and variable in self-renewal strategy across organs.

### Four properties of differentiated cells that favour mutation accumulation

1. **Non-standard division:** Differentiated cells can autoduplication symmetrically, go multinucleate, or become polyploid — all without returning to a stem state. These division modes are common in hepatocytes, pancreatic acinar cells, chief cells, and salivary gland cells, among others.

2. **Long lifespan and low turnover:** Long residence time in G0/G1 means differentiated cells accumulate chromosomal abnormalities over years, yet their slow cycling limits the rate at which mutations are diluted or selected out. Pancreatic acinar cells can live months in mice; hepatocyte turnover is 200–400 days in adult humans; alveolar type I cells are mitotically quiescent and live years.

3. **Polyploidy and multinucleation — oncogenic route:** In polyploid differentiated cells, reductive mitoses can (a) produce uniparental disomy and premature loss of heterozygosity of tumor suppressors (Fig 2B), or (b) generate aneuploidy via asymmetric segregation during reduction (Fig 2C). Multinucleated cells tend to undergo more error-prone (NHEJ/MMEJ) rather than faithful (HR) repair of DSBs, because HR requires a sister chromatid present only during S/G2, while quiescent differentiated cells reside in G0/G1.

4. **Error-prone DNA damage repair (NHEJ/MMEJ over HR):** Differentiated, mitotically quiescent cells in G0/G1 preferentially use NHEJ and MMEJ (Alt-EJ) — mechanisms that introduce deletions, insertions, and loss of DNA sequence at break sites. This is contrasted with embryonic and constitutively dividing stem cells that have active HR.

### The Cyclical Hit Model

The Cyclical Hit Model (Burclaff and Mills) explains how somatic mutational burden accumulates in histologically normal tissue: (1) long G0 intervals allow mutations to build up; (2) injury recruits differentiated cells back into the cell cycle via paligenosis; (3) the now-mutated cells re-differentiate and appear histologically normal; (4) recurrent cycles increase mutational load. This accounts for the otherwise puzzling observation that chronically injured nonmalignant tissue carries a mutational burden similar to adjacent cancer tissue.

### Paligenosis — the conserved plasticity program

Paligenosis (from Greek *pali* = "return to"; *genesis* = generative cellular process) is defined as a stepwise, evolutionarily conserved cellular program that enables quiescent, fully differentiated secretory cells to return to a proliferative progenitor-like state after injury. It proceeds through three stages:

- **Stage 1 — Autodegradation/autophagy:** mTORC1 suppression (via DDIT4/REDD1) triggers massive upregulation of autophagolysosomes, dismantling differentiated-cell architecture (zymogenic granules, rough ER, Golgi). Genes: ATF3, RAB7B, IFRD1, xCT upregulated; p57, MIST1 (BHLHA15) downregulated.
- **Stage 2 — Metaplastic/embryonic gene expression:** Re-expression of embryonic/progenitor markers (CD44, SOX9) without cell cycle re-entry. p53 acts as a fidelity checkpoint at the Stage 2→3 transition, preventing proliferation of cells with unrepaired DNA.
- **Stage 3 — Cell cycle re-entry:** mTORC1 re-activation (derepressed by IFRD1 suppression of p53), DNMT1 upregulation, Ki-67/pHH3 proliferation markers. DDIT4 is degraded in Stage 2, lifting mTORC1 suppression only in cells that have passed p53 quality control.

The molecular logic: p53 enforces a genomic competency checkpoint before allowing Stage 3. Loss of p53 function (as in esophageal metaplasia, gastric cancer, pancreatic ADM, zebrafish melanoma) allows cells to enter the cell cycle with unrepaired DNA, and mutant KRAS then acts as a stronger proliferative driver in the ADM context than at homeostasis. This explains the organ-specific timing and cooperativity of p53 and KRAS mutations in PDAC.

Paligenosis has been documented or inferred in: gastric chief cells (ADM), pancreatic acinar cells (ADM), salivary gland acinar cells, lung secretory cells, kidney proximal tubular cells, intestinal Paneth cells (after irradiation), hepatocytes, and *Drosophila* enterocytes — suggesting broad conservation.

### Evolutionary perspective: multicellularity and p53

- Choanoflagellates (closest unicellular relatives of metazoans) already exhibit temporal plasticity — switching between sessile, colonial, and free-swimming phenotypes — and their cell division resembles autoduplication of differentiated cells, not stem-cell asymmetric division. Dedicated stem cells are argued to be a *late* evolutionary innovation.
- Sea sponges (Porifera) regenerate whole organs from single shed cells and show no documented evidence of malignancy, possibly because they routinely shed cells that carry mutations. Sponges lack functional p53 orthologs.
- Cnidaria (Hydra, *Nematostella*) show the earliest evidence of cancer and carry the first functionally characterized p53 orthologs (nvp63, nvpEC53, nvpVS53 in *Nematostella*). The coincidence of obligatory multicellularity, restricted shedding, differentiated-cell plasticity, and p53 emergence is presented as non-coincidental.
- Phylostratigraphic analysis: tumor suppressor gene emergence correlates with the origin of multicellularity (Domazet-Loso and Tautz, 2010; Trigos et al., 2019). p53 family proteins (p53/p63/p73) appear as early as complex unicellular organisms (choanoflagellates, filasterea) but functional p53 tumor suppressor activity (UV-induced apoptosis, DNA damage surveillance) is not documented until obligatory multicellular metazoans.
- **Central evolutionary argument:** Plasticity of differentiated cells evolved as a regenerative adaptation at or near the origin of multicellularity. The tumor suppressor p53 evolved specifically to police this plasticity program — acting as a genomic competency checkpoint for cells re-entering the cell cycle via paligenosis. Cancer co-opts this ancestral regenerative program when p53 fidelity is lost.

### Implications for the Cancer Stem Cell model

The authors argue that the Cancer Stem Cell (CSC) model has low utility in tissues where no bona fide professional adult stem cell exists (pancreas, liver, kidney, prostate). If the bulk of tumor cells are plastic differentiated cells capable of re-entering the cell cycle after injury, then:

- There is no stable CSC subpopulation to target therapeutically — nearly all cells can undergo paligenosis after therapy-induced injury.
- Cancer cells may cycle between a mitotically quiescent, differentiated-like state (using error-prone NHEJ/MMEJ) and an induced proliferative state — with the quiescent state conferring resistance to cytotoxic drugs targeting dividing cells.
- Rather than looking for CSCs, the appropriate therapeutic target is the paligenosis program itself — particularly the Stage 2 metaplastic state or the Stage 2→3 checkpoint.
- Paligenosis occurs in normal tissue only after injury, so inhibiting it may have lower toxicity to normal stem cell populations than general anti-mitotic strategies.

## Limitations

- The paper is a review/perspective, not a primary empirical study. Most mechanistic claims about paligenosis stages are sourced from the authors' own prior work in gastric and pancreatic epithelium; generalization to other organ systems rests on less complete evidence.
- The three-stage paligenosis program has been most thoroughly characterized in gastric chief cells and pancreatic acinar cells. The claim that it is broadly conserved across differentiated cell types in multiple organs is plausible but extrapolated from partial evidence in each other system.
- The evolutionary phylogenetic argument (p53 emergence coinciding with obligatory multicellularity) is correlational; the functional characterization of non-vertebrate p53 orthologs is incomplete, and it is unclear whether the absence of p53 functional studies in sponges reflects true absence of function or a research gap.
- The paper does not quantify how frequently paligenosis-mediated transformation occurs relative to stem-cell-derived transformation — it argues differentiated cells are underappreciated but does not provide direct comparative incidence data across organ systems.
- The Cyclical Hit Model's prediction that histologically normal tissue acquires mutational burden equivalent to adjacent tumor tissue is cited from existing data (Shimizu et al., 2014; Weaver et al., 2014) but the interpretation (neutral accumulation during paligenosis windows vs. early field cancerization driven by selected clones) is not resolved.
- No discussion of haematological malignancies, which may not fit the differentiated-cell-plasticity-via-paligenosis framework given the well-characterized HSC hierarchy.
