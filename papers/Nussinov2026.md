## Key Findings

- Classical cell-type labels are argued to be too static for dynamic cancer behaviors such as metastasis, transplantation-like adaptation, proliferation, and treatment response.
- The authors frame cell states as epigenetically controlled, environment-sensitive transcriptomic and functional configurations.
- They propose that biomolecular condensates are the physical intracellular structures through which cell-state functions are executed.
- They argue that phenotypic heterogeneity in untreated cancers with relatively low genetic heterogeneity highlights the importance of cell states and epigenetic regulation.
- The pharmacology section warns that directly dismantling condensates may lack specificity, whereas targeting cell-state-defining epigenetic regulators may be more selective.
- The review suggests combining single-cell transcriptomics with spatial proteomics to map cell-state dynamics to condensate composition during tumor progression.

## Limitations

The paper is a perspective review and makes broad conceptual claims.
It does not supply a test that distinguishes cell-state transition from selection in a specific tumor system.
The condensate mechanism is compelling but still difficult to measure clinically, and the authors explicitly note that detailed condensate conformational behavior remains underdeveloped.
