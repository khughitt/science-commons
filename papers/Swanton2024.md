## Key Findings

**Tumor promotion is mechanistically distinct from tumor initiation.** Many known carcinogens act not as mutagens but as inducers of inflammation that awaken and clonally expand cells carrying latent oncogenic mutations. Suspected non-mutational tumor promoters include microplastics, glyphosate, PFASs, air pollutants, and infectious agents such as H. pylori. Critically, there is currently no biological assay for non-mutagenic tumor-promoting activity; developing one is identified as a priority.

**Tissue origin, not mutation identity, determines cancer phenotype.** Activation of identical oncogenic mutations (KRas G12D + Myc) in adult lung vs. pancreas tissue produces adenocarcinomas whose stromal phenotypes exactly replicate their spontaneous human counterparts. This supports the view that oncogenic pathways "hack" the tissue-specific wound repair program of the cell of origin, explaining organotypic convergence across cancers driven by different mutations.

**Aging is the dominant cancer risk modifier.** Over 90% of cancer patients are >50 years old. Normal tissues accumulate a patchwork of cells bearing latent oncogenic mutations with age. "Youth is tumor-suppressive": healthy young tissue microenvironments limit clonal expansion; aged tissues progressively lose this capacity. Clonal hematopoiesis of indeterminate potential (CHIP) affects 20–30% of cancer patients, promotes systemic inflammation, and is linked to increased solid tumor risk and mortality.

**Systemic immune dysregulation propagates beyond the TME.** Tumor-derived signals reprogram hematopoietic progenitors in bone marrow, driving myeloid bias and immunosuppressive myeloid expansion systemically. Five mechanisms are identified for release of immunomodulatory molecules into the circulation (tumor autocrine, therapy-induced senescence, genomic instability-driven type I IFN, microbiome-derived innate signaling, stress-induced metabolites). Understanding systemic drivers of pathogenic myelopoiesis is proposed as a foundation for immune stratification.

**Senescence is bidirectional.** Therapy-induced senescence creates an inflamed microenvironment (tumor-promoting long-term via SASP). The "one-two punch" sequential approach — senescence-inducing therapy followed by senolytic therapy — is proposed as a synergistic strategy; preclinical data support senolytic clearing of senescent cells improving chemotherapy tolerability and reducing cancer incidence. Clinical proof-of-concept is still needed.

**Cancer is a systemic metabolic disease.** Tumors reprogram host metabolism in distant organs (liver, brain), driving cachexia, metastasis, and therapy resistance. Cancer-associated cachexia (CAC) affects up to 80% of advanced cancer patients [UNVERIFIED exact percentage; the paper identifies CAC as a leading cause of cancer death without a precise figure in the text reviewed] and is framed as an unresolved wound healing response. Dietary interventions — fasting-mimicking diets, precision amino acid restriction, low-carbohydrate diets — are each entering clinical evaluation. Approximately 4–8% of all cancers are directly attributed to obesity.

**Circadian disruption increases cancer incidence and may modulate therapy response.** Night shift work, jet lag, and residence at the western edge of time zones are all associated with increased cancer incidence. Chronic circadian disruption exacerbates tumor growth in mouse models via reduced immune surveillance and elevated HSF1. Emerging data from the MEMOIR study suggest checkpoint blockade responses are less robust in the evening, raising the possibility of "circadian logic" in treatment scheduling.

**Neuro-cancer crosstalk emerges as a candidate hallmark.** Peripheral nerve innervation of tumors (sympathetic, parasympathetic, sensory) drives growth, invasion, metastasis, and therapy resistance across most cancer sites via paracrine neural factors. GBM co-opts glutamatergic synaptic input and autonomous pacemaker-like activity to form tumor networks. Over 100 approved neurological drugs could be repurposed; phase 2/3 trials are ongoing (perampanel targeting AMPA receptor synapses, meclofenamate targeting cancer neural networks).

**Thromboinflammation links clonal hematopoiesis, metastasis, and cancer death.** Cancer patients have a 9-fold higher thrombosis risk than the general population. Neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) promote metastatic colonization by physically shielding tumor cells from T cells, awakening dormant cancer cells, and activating thrombosis. Factor XI inhibitors (uncoupling haemostasis from thrombosis) are entering trials.

**Microbiome evidence is strongest for gut immunotherapy response; intra-tumoral findings remain contested.** Gut microbiota composition strongly predicts immunotherapy response and adverse events. Fecal microbiota transplants have achieved immunotherapy resensitization in clinical trials. However, pan-cancer intra-tumoral microbiome findings have faced reproducibility challenges and data-analysis concerns; the field has more robust evidence in colorectal and mucosal cancers than in non-gastrointestinal solid tumors.

## Limitations

- Perspective format: arguments are framed as frontiers and priorities, not systematic evidence reviews. Specific claims vary widely in evidentiary grounding.
- Many sections explicitly acknowledge that mechanistic understanding is incomplete; the paper is prescriptive about future research rather than synthesizing resolved findings.
- Inbred mouse model limitations are discussed at length; the paper itself warns that experiments in constrained genetic backgrounds often fail to translate.
- The paper does not deeply quantify the individual effect sizes for most of the environmental and systemic axes it proposes, making it harder to prioritize which clouds of complexity carry the greatest causal burden.
- Some claims (e.g., specific metabolic interventions in clinical settings) are at early trial stages and should be treated as promising rather than established.
