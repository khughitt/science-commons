## One-Sentence Summary

Integrated Hi-C, WGS, and RNA-seq of two aneuploid MM cell lines (RPMI-8226 and
U266) reveals that CNVs bias raw Hi-C interaction matrices, CNV breakpoints
preferentially co-localize with TAD boundaries, MM genomes display ~25% more
TADs of smaller median size than normal B cells, and ~20% of genomic regions
undergo A/B compartment switching accompanied by concordant changes in gene
expression and epigenetic marks.

## Key Findings

1. **CNV bias in Hi-C data and ICE correction.** Raw Hi-C interaction counts are
   positively correlated with CNV-block copy number (F-test on regression slope,
   p < 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁶, Fig. 1d). Independently, WGS- and filtered-Hi-C-derived CNV
   estimates for RPMI-8226 show high concordance (Spearman r = 0.83,
   p < 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁶, Fig. 1c). ICE normalization reduces
   this bias more effectively than HiCNorm, producing a per-copy interaction map
   suitable for downstream analysis. The filtered Hi-C data independently reproduce
   CNV calls from WGS.

2. **Hi-C detects translocation events.** Inter-chromosomal interaction hotspots in
   Hi-C identify chimeric chromosomes arising from translocations. In RPMI-8226,
   4 of 56 WGS-detected inter-chromosomal translocations fall in the top-100 Hi-C
   interaction pairs (Fisher's exact p = 2.6 × 10⁻⁸; observed/expected = 90.9),
   including the t(16;22)(q23;q11) event involving WWOX and MAF. Combining Hi-C and
   WGS recovers additional translocation events implicating ADORA2B, FLII, AMBRA1,
   and PTPRJ in RPMI-8226, and TNIK, FBXW7, and TRIM2 in U266.

3. **CNV breakpoints co-localize with TAD boundaries.** In RPMI-8226, 7.5% of CNV
   breakpoints are TAD boundaries and 30.7% fall within 120 kb of a TAD boundary —
   significantly closer than location-randomized controls (Fisher's exact p = 0.038).
   Insulation score profiles at CNV breakpoints resemble those at TAD boundaries.
   The same co-localization is observed in normal B cells (GM12878), suggesting that
   CNV breakpoints either preferentially arise at TAD boundaries or that TAD-disrupting
   CNVs are selected against during cancer evolution.

4. **MM TAD fragmentation: more and smaller TADs.** ICE-normalized Hi-C at 40 kb
   resolution identifies 3457 TADs in RPMI-8226 and 3342 in U266, compared with 2756
   in GM12878 normal B cells (~25% increase). Median TAD size is 600–640 kb in MM
   vs. 800 kb in normal B cells. 1281 TADs are conserved across all three cell types;
   740 are shared between the two MM lines but absent from normal B cells.

5. **~20% A/B compartment switching with concordant expression changes.** At 500 kb
   resolution, ~8% of genomic regions switch A→B in one or both MM lines (associated
   with gene downregulation; t-test p < 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁶) and ~24% switch B→A (associated
   with upregulation). Genes in consistently switched regions are enriched for MAPK
   signaling, TNF signaling, cytokine–cytokine receptor interaction, N-glycan
   biosynthesis, and hematopoietic cell lineage pathways. A cytokine receptor gene
   cluster at 2q11.2-q12.1 (IL1R1, IL1R2, IL18R1, MAP4K4) switches A→B in MM with
   reduced H3K27ac, H3K4me1, and H3K4me3 marks and downregulated expression —
   without accompanying CNV, demonstrating regulation beyond dosage effects.
   (Note: the paper's abstract rounds total A/B switching to ~20%, but the itemized
   Results — ~8% A→B plus ~24% B→A — sum to ~32%; use the itemized figures for any
   quantitative reuse.)

## Methods

Two aneuploid multiple myeloma (MM) cell lines — RPMI-8226 (near-triploid) and U266 (near-diploid), both from ATCC — were profiled by in situ Hi-C (Rao et al. 2014 protocol) using two restriction enzymes (HindIII and MboI), with ~200 million reads per replicate. Reads were mapped to hg19 with Bowtie2 (first 36 bases of each paired-end read), filtered to unique, non-redundant reads within 500 bp of enzyme cut sites, and combined across replicates into 40 kb (TAD) and 500 kb (A/B compartment) resolution interaction maps. Whole-genome sequencing (50× depth; bwa-mem; Picard deduplication) and RNA-seq (poly-A selected; 3 biological replicates/line; 20M reads/replicate; TopHat2 + Cufflinks) were generated for both lines. CNVs were called by Control-FREEC at 40 kb/500 kb resolution (ploidy 3 for RPMI-8226, 2 for U266, set from karyotyping; results were similar using ploidy 2 for RPMI-8226). Raw Hi-C interaction counts were regressed against CNV-block copy number and tested for slope significance by F-test; ICE and HiCNorm normalizations were compared for CNV-bias correction, with ICE used downstream. Translocations were called from WGS by CREST (default parameters; filtered for >10% supporting-read fraction and >40 total supporting reads at both breakpoints) and cross-referenced against the top-100 highest inter-chromosomal Hi-C interaction bins (Fisher's exact test). TADs were called with matrix2insulation.pl (insulation-score valleys; TADs <200 kb or telomeric/centromeric excluded); cross-sample conservation required ≥70% reciprocal overlap (Bedtools intersectBed -f 0.70 -r). A/B compartments were assigned from the first principal component of ICE-normalized 500 kb matrices (HiTC R package), with the higher-gene-density compartment labeled A. GM12878 served as the normal B-cell comparator (public Hi-C/WGS/RNA-seq/ChIP-seq). ChIP-seq (U266: BioProject PRJEB1912/ERS333898; GM12878: UCSC Table Browser) was processed with cutadapt, Bowtie2, Picard, and MACS. Pathway enrichment used DAVID 6.7 (KEGG, human gene background); overlap/enrichment significance used Fisher's exact test.

## Limitations

- Only two MM cell lines (RPMI-8226, U266) were profiled; established cell lines may carry culture-adaptation artifacts and cannot capture heterogeneity across MM cytogenetic subtypes or primary tumor samples.
- The normal-cell comparator, GM12878, is an EBV-transformed lymphoblastoid B-cell line rather than a normal plasma cell; the authors explicitly caveat that some MM-vs.-GM12878 differences may reflect the lymphoblastoid-B-cell-vs.-plasma-cell difference rather than malignant transformation itself.
- Hi-C, WGS, and RNA-seq were performed on bulk cell populations; the authors note that intercellular heterogeneity within a line could inflate apparent TAD counts and that single-cell Hi-C would be needed to confirm this — an alternative explanation acknowledged but not tested.
- The CNV-breakpoint/TAD-boundary association (Fig. 3) is correlational: the paper proposes two non-exclusive causal directions (CNVs create new TAD boundaries vs. TAD-disrupting CNVs being selected against) but presents no experiment distinguishing them.
- Resolution is capped at 40 kb for TAD/breakpoint calling and 500 kb for A/B compartments, limiting detection of finer-scale structure.
- Hi-C-based translocation detection is restricted to events with high inter-chromosomal interaction counts; only 4 of 56 WGS-identified translocations in RPMI-8226 fell in the Hi-C top-100 interactions, so Hi-C alone would miss most true translocation events.
- The GM12878 CNV-breakpoint comparison (Fig. 3d) uses cancer CNVs from the literature-mined CNVD database (161 filtered MM records), a different ascertainment process from the RPMI-8226/U266 WGS-derived CNVs used elsewhere.
- The design is cross-sectional (single time point per line); no data address whether 3D reorganization precedes, follows, or co-occurs with CNV acquisition or transcriptional change.
- The authors themselves caution on generalizability: phenomena observed in one cancer type may not hold in others, given the diversity and heterogeneity of cancer alterations.
