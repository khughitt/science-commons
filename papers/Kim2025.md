## Key Findings

### Simulation performance

- Across 300 hyperparameter configurations, autoencoder training loss and ARI were strongly negatively correlated: lower loss → better clustering (Fig. 2a). This relationship validates unsupervised hyperparameter selection by training loss minimization on real data where ground truth is unavailable.
- BGMM with combined features consistently outperformed GMM and k-means (all ARI > 0.9; BGMM highest).
- ARI remained > 0.9 across 5 and 7 subclone settings, confirming scalability to complex tree structures (Fig. 2c).
- At 0–5% error rates: using z + ψ_1(z) maintained ARI > 0.9 up to 5% error; z alone degraded sharply above 1% (Fig. 2d–f). The decoder layer inclusion is the key design choice for noise robustness.
- **Headline comparison (Fig. 5):** CluVar outperformed both SCClone and bmVAE across all simulation scenarios A, B, C under all error rates 0–5%, on both ARI and RF distance. CluVar RF distance reached 0 (perfect tree concordance) in most simulation datasets. bmVAE showed high variability in RF at low error rates. CluVar also exhibited the shortest runtimes across all scenarios.

### ER+ breast cancer cell line (30 978 cells, CAMA1)

- CluVar partitioned cells into **7 subclones** from 300 most frequently detected variants (Fig. 3a, Table S6).
- Parsimony tree: Subclone 0 and 1 (trunk — low mutational burden, near tree root); Subclones 2–6 (branches — higher mutational burden).
- Subclones 1, 4, and 6 were overrepresented in the ribociclib-resistant condition (Fig. S5b).
- Hypergeometric test confirmed near-linear correspondence between CluVar subclone ordering (by mutation accumulation) and expression-based Leiden cluster ordering (Fig. S6a) — genetic and transcriptomic hierarchies aligned.
- Monocle3 pseudotime reproduced the mutational hierarchy (Fig. 3e).
- Pathway enrichment (Fig. 3f–g): early subclones enriched for estrogen response, cGMP/NO signaling; late subclones (high mutation burden) enriched for hypoxia, EMT, G2-M checkpoint, E2F targets, DNA replication. Subclones 3 and 5 shared oxidative phosphorylation; Subclones 1, 4, 6 shared Myc-target signature.
- Key variant: exonic EIF2S2 and NDUFS8 mutations delineated branch subclones; non-synonymous exonic mutations in EIF2S2 and NDUFS8 together defined Subclones 1, 4, 6 (resistant-enriched) from 3 and 5 (sensitive-enriched, oxidative phosphorylation high).

### Pancreatic cancer tissue (3 patients)

- 16 951 malignant cells (cell-type annotation required; non-malignant cells excluded).
- **7 subclones** identified; tree shows trunk (Subclones 0, 1) and two major lineages: {2, 4, 5} and {3, 6} (Fig. 4a).
- Subclone 6 predominantly restricted to Patient 2 and enriched in metastatic lesions (Fig. 4d); identified as metastatic-enriched subclone (Fig. S9b).
- Monocle3: bifurcated lineages along CluVar phylogeny (Fig. 4e), providing trajectory not apparent from expression alone.
- Pathway enrichment (Fig. 4f–g): early cluster (Subclone 0) enriched for DNA damage/p53/UV response; intermediate (1–4) for stress adaptation/inflammatory; late (5 and 6) for HSF1 activation, KRAS signaling, hypoxia. Subclones 3 and 6 shared EMT, S. aureus infection, TNF-α/NF-κB, metallothionein binding; Subclones 4 and 5 shared epithelial structure and gastrointestinal epithelial maintenance.
- Key variants: GSTP1 (subclones 3+6, shared) involved in TNF-α/NF-κB, antioxidant defense; LGALS1 (subclone 6 only) — immunomodulator suppressing NF-κB. Subclone 5: GSDMD, NEAT1, NCL mutations — antiviral immunity regulators.

## Limitations

- **300-variant cap:** Imposed by autoencoder stability constraints; may exclude rare subclonal mutations at low allele frequency. Potentially biases toward variants in highly expressed genes (expression-coverage bias in scRNA-seq).
- **GATK variant calling accuracy:** scRNA-seq read depth is low and coverage is transcript-biased; GATK was designed for genomic DNA. Specialized callers (SCcaller, Monovar) may be more appropriate but were not used.
- **Majority-voting collapses within-cluster heterogeneity:** Cells within a cluster lose individual genotype resolution; this matters for q019-style tests that require within-clone variance measurement.
- **No CNV integration:** CluVar operates only on SNVs/small variants, not copy-number alterations. For many cancer types (e.g., CIN-high tumors), CNVs carry more subclonal signal than SNVs.
- **Validation scope limited:** Only two cancer models tested (one cell line, one cancer type with 3 patients). Generalizability to primary early-stage tumors, low-TMB tumors, or single-sample datasets is unestablished.
- **No imputation:** Missing values handled by masking, not imputation. Majority-voting for missing values is simple but susceptible to high-dropout datasets.
- **No direct comparison to CNV-based tools (InferCNV, CopyKat, Numbat):** The benchmark covers only SCClone and bmVAE — the two most architecturally similar tools. CluVar is not positioned as a replacement for CNV-based methods; the relationship to those tools is uncharacterized.
