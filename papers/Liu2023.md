## Key Findings

**Pooled GI cancer:**
- 1–2 breakfasts/week: HR = 2.35 (95% CI: 1.28–4.32)
- Never breakfast: HR = 2.06 (95% CI: 1.47–2.89)
- P for trend < 0.001

**Site-specific (selected significant associations):**

| Cancer site | Group | Adjusted HR (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|
| Esophageal | Never | 2.72 (1.05–7.03) |
| Gastric | 1–2/week | 3.45 (1.06–11.20) |
| Colorectal | Never | 2.32 (1.34–4.01) |
| Liver | 1–2/week | 3.42 (1.22–9.53) |
| Liver | Never | 2.41 (1.23–4.71) |
| Gallbladder/bile duct | Never | 5.43 (1.34–21.93) |

**Subgroup analyses:** Significant associations in all age, sex, and diet quality score strata; no significant interactions (all P > 0.05). The signal was directionally consistent in women despite smaller numbers (never breakfast HR = 4.58, 95% CI: 1.65–12.74).

**Sensitivity analyses:** Excluding first-year cancer cases, cirrhosis cases, and HCV-infected participants did not materially alter results. The gallbladder/bile duct association was attenuated to non-significance after excluding first-year cases (3.46, 95% CI: 0.68–17.55), suggesting possible reverse causality for this rare endpoint.

**Mediation:** BMI, CRP, and TyG index explained no significant proportion of the breakfast–GI cancer association, ruling out the three most obvious metabolic intermediaries as primary pathways.

## Limitations

1. **Single self-report timepoint:** Breakfast frequency was assessed once (2014–2015) by questionnaire. Measurement error and temporal change in eating habits are uncontrolled.
2. **Male-dominated cohort:** 83% men (industrial coal-mine workforce); generalisability to women is uncertain, though the directional signal in women was stronger (HR ~ 4.6 for never-breakfast group in women).
3. **Breakfast content unknown:** Nutrient composition, caloric density, and meal quality were not captured, preventing disentanglement of "breakfast absence" from "poor breakfast quality."
4. **Helicobacter pylori not measured:** A major confounder for gastric cancer; its absence from the adjustment set leaves gastric cancer estimates uncertain.
5. **Short follow-up (median 5.6 years):** Cancer latency is typically long; many exposures may not have produced detectable tumors yet, likely attenuating the observed HRs rather than inflating them.
6. **No endoscopy:** Asymptomatic GI cancers likely missed, biasing toward incident cases with clinical presentation.
7. **No data on sleep timing or caloric intake:** The authors acknowledge that breakfast skipping is correlated with late-night behavior (staying up later → getting up later), so the confounding by sleep timing and circadian phase cannot be separated from the meal-timing effect itself.
8. **Mediation methodology:** The CAUSALMED procedure tests additive mediation under sequential ignorability assumptions; violation of those assumptions (unmeasured confounding between mediator and outcome) could mask true partial mediation.
9. **Rare endpoints (gallbladder, esophageal):** Very small case counts (3–6 events in exposed groups) produce wide CIs; gallbladder result in particular should be treated cautiously.
