## One-Sentence Summary

LLM-derived embeddings of medical concept names (OpenAI GPT text-embedding-3-small) injected into a Transformer trained on longitudinal OMOP EHR sequences improve 6-12 month pancreatic-cancer prediction AUROC from 0.60 to 0.67 at CUMC and 0.82 to 0.86 at CSMC, and achieve PPV 0.141 versus 0.004 for traditional risk factors.

## Key Findings

1. **GPT embeddings reliably improve prediction over randomly initialized baselines.** For 6-12 month prediction at CUMC, GPT (AUROC 0.673) outperformed baseline (0.592, p = 0.018); at CSMC, GPT (0.858) outperformed baseline (0.819, p < 0.05). GPT embeddings of 1,536 dimensions were the best single model; embeddings as small as 32 dimensions performed comparably or better than the 4,096-dimensional Mistral model in some intervals, suggesting that high-dimensional embeddings can introduce redundancy given the short input texts (condition names only).

2. **Excluding 0-3 month pre-diagnosis data reduces leakage and reveals cleaner signals.** Including data from the 0-3 month window inflated near-term AUROCs substantially (e.g. CUMC 6-12 month: 0.673 with 0-3m data vs 0.819 after exclusion); the exclusion exposed a large leakage effect driven by late-presenting pancreatic features (e.g. "disorder of pancreas") appearing in controls but dominating feature attribution in multi-label models. After exclusion, CUMC and CSMC 6-12 month AUROCs converged to 0.819 vs 0.893 (GPT), confirming that the raw site difference reflected control-group data-leakage rather than genuine performance heterogeneity.

3. **EHR model substantially outperforms traditional risk factors on PPV.** At CUMC 6-12 months, EHR-GPT achieved max PPV 0.232 vs 0.004 for "any traditional risk factor" (CA19-9, T2D, pancreatitis). To achieve 50% case sensitivity, EHR-GPT requires screening ~17% of the population vs ~16% for the risk-factor strategy, but the EHR strategy captures a different and largely non-overlapping set: >50% (CUMC) and >85% (CSMC) of EHR-flagged PC patients had none of the three traditional risk factors.

4. **LIME analysis reveals novel comorbidity signals beyond classical risk factors.** In binary classification models (where 0-3 month leakage is eliminated), kidney/ureter disorders and liver disease emerged as top features for both 6-12 and 12-36 month windows at both sites, consistent with emerging evidence linking renal dysregulation and NAFLD/cirrhosis to PC risk. The GPT embedding model surfaced more clinically coherent features than the baseline (which surfaced unrelated musculoskeletal conditions).

5. **Consistently identified high-risk individuals form a more confident subgroup.** Patients flagged as high-risk across all four embedding variants (baseline, GPT, RGCN, Mistral) showed significantly higher risk scores (p < 0.0001) and higher feature-similarity (p < 0.0001) than patients flagged by only some models, suggesting that model-ensemble consistency could define a targeted screening subpopulation.

## Limitations

- **Structured conditions only; no lab values, medications, or unstructured notes.** Family history of PC (a key CAPS screening criterion) is typically in unstructured notes and was not captured. The model is therefore blind to a meaningful fraction of the standard clinical risk profile.
- **Cohort prevalence far exceeds real-world PC incidence.** Study prevalence at CUMC was ~215/100,000 vs real-world ~32/100,000 (SEER 2022). PPVs will be substantially lower in deployment, requiring recalibration.
- **External validation limited to one additional institution (CSMC).** Both sites are US tertiary-care academic medical centers; generalizability to community hospitals, primary-care EHRs, or non-US health systems is unknown.
- **Multi-label leakage partially addressed but not fully resolved.** Data augmentation and 0-3m exclusion reduce leakage, but binary models with augmented data did not consistently outperform the leakage-corrected multi-label model, suggesting residual confounding.
- **LLM embeddings encode condition *names*, not clinical context.** Semantic richness from GPT pretraining reflects the general biomedical literature rather than EHR-specific coding patterns. The same OMOP concept name (e.g., "disorder of pancreas") may encode very different clinical severity levels across sites and coders.
- **No formal causal interpretation.** LIME feature rankings are post-hoc attribution, not causal discovery. The apparent precursor signals (kidney disease, liver disease) need prospective or Mendelian-randomization-style validation.
