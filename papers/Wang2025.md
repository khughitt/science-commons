## Key Findings

**Phenotypic associations with multimorbidity.**
Seven abdominal, 16 cardiac, and 107 brain traits showed significant associations. Among abdominal traits, subcutaneous fat volume, visceral fat volume, liver PDFF, pancreas PDFF, and liver volume were positively associated; liver iron and pancreas volume were negatively associated. Among cardiac traits, LV myocardial wall thickness, circumferential strain, and longitudinal strain were positively associated, while stroke volume (LA, LV, RV) and LV radial strain were negatively associated. Brain findings were extensive: ventricular and inferior lateral ventricle volumes positive; most cortical and subcortical volumes (caudal anterior cingulate, superior temporal, superior frontal) negative; white matter hyperintensity volume positive; brain microstructural measures (ISOVF, ICVF, OD) predominantly negative in major tracts (corpus callosum, corona radiata, internal capsule, posterior thalamic radiation, limbic tracts).

**Cross-organ clique network (1418 cliques).**
Two distinct functional subnetworks:
- Module 1 (Metabolic Load-Cardiac Remodeling-Brain Structural Abnormality): metabolic load (increased liver volume, visceral fat) → cardiac remodeling (increased myocardial wall thickness) → brain structural abnormalities (reduced gray matter volume, compromised white matter integrity). Positive correlations within module.
- Module 2 (Ectopic Lipid-Cardiac Dysfunction-White Matter Pathology): ectopic lipid accumulation in liver/pancreas → cardiac dysfunction (reduced stroke volume) → white matter pathology (increased hyperintensity volume, compromised corpus callosum integrity). Positive correlations between cardiac dysfunction and white matter pathology.

Most-connected abdominal traits by total (cardiac + brain) connection count: liver volume (82), subcutaneous fat (59), visceral fat (53), liver PDFF (44), pancreas volume (24), pancreas PDFF (14). LV myocardial wall thickness AHA segment 6 is the most-connected cardiac node; white matter hyperintensity volume the most-connected brain node. Network sparsity 0.84, modularity 0.15, closeness centrality 0.54.

Subgroup analyses: 609 and 707 overlapping abdomen-heart-brain cliques in CVD-depression and T2D-depression multimorbidity, respectively; central nodes consistent across disease outcomes.

**Genetic architecture (43 shared loci).**
- 78 unique genomic loci shared by 216 cliques initially; after merging overlapping positions across all 1418 cliques, 43 distinct loci (21 novel).
- Lead loci by number of cliques: chr1:27262545-27262545 → NUDC (33 cliques); chr1:27021913-27021913 → ARID1A (27 cliques); chr17:43546853-43805194 → CRHR1 (25 cliques). These loci involve traits spanning pancreas, liver, cardiac volumes, myocardial wall thickness, white matter hyperintensity volume, and white matter microstructure.
- Of candidate SNPs: 46.9% intronic, 41.1% intergenic; 5.0% CADD > 12.37; 7.4% RegulomeDB < 3; 44.8% chromatin state < 8 (transcriptionally active regions).
- Genetic correlations varied by organ pair: abdomen-heart pairs showed strongest correlations (−0.38 to 0.23); heart-brain pairs moderate (−0.20 to 0.20); abdomen-brain pairs modest but consistently negative (−0.11 to −0.06).
- 28 loci showed pleiotropic effects across multiple cliques; 33.2% concordant directional effects, 66.8% discordant (suggesting agonistic-antagonistic genetic mechanisms).

**224 protein-coding genes enriched in 39 GO biological processes** primarily involving lipid metabolism and transport, mitochondrial functions, and membrane organization — relevant to both cardiometabolic and neuropsychiatric pathology.

**Shared TWAS genes (15 unique genes in 24 tissues).**
Key genes: ARID1A (chromatin remodeling; liver-heart-brain via hepatic lipid metabolism, cardiomyocyte proliferation, neurodevelopment); CRHR1 (corticotropin-releasing hormone receptor; HPA axis; links pancreas-heart-brain through cortisol-mediated insulin resistance, cardiomyocyte hypertrophy, white matter integrity); WNT3 and RSPO3 (ligand and signal-enhancer of the WNT/β-catenin pathway); TNFSF12 and HSPA4 (TNF and HSP70 family; act on organs via inflammatory and oxidative-stress pathways); CENPW, PIGV, GPN2 (liver-heart-brain via blood-vessel expression); SYTL1, TNFSF12, NUDC (expressed in heart, brain, blood, and liver); MPDU1, NMBR (brain and adipose tissue → same three organs). All 15 genes retained significance in sensitivity analyses; 14 of 15 also observed in CVD-depression subgroup.

**Prediction modeling.**
Biochemical-MRI model outperformed Biochemical model: ΔC-statistic +0.05 (95% CI 0.01–0.10) for multimorbidity risk; C-statistic improved from 0.70 to 0.74 (95% CI 0.67–0.81). For depression risk in pre-existing CMDs: ΔC-statistic +0.06 (95% CI 0.02–0.13); Biochemical-MRI C-statistic 0.77 (95% CI 0.66–0.88) vs. MRI-only 0.72 (95% CI 0.60–0.83). Strongest individual MRI predictors across all three scenarios: liver volume, LV mean myocardial wall thickness, global gray matter volume. Retrolenticular internal capsule ICVF was key predictor specifically for multimorbidity progression.

## Methods

Observational, cross-sectional analysis in the UK Biobank, a prospective cohort of 502,536 participants aged 37–73 recruited 2006–2010 across 22 UK assessment centers. The outcome was prevalent CMDs-depression multimorbidity: co-occurrence of at least one cardiometabolic disease (CVD: ICD-10 I20–I25 coronary artery disease, I60–I64 stroke, I50 heart failure; T2D: E11) and depression (F32–F33), ascertained from hospital inpatient and death-registry first-occurrence data; participants with only one of the two conditions before MRI assessment were excluded. Three MRI subsamples were analyzed: abdominal (1.5T, N=25,344; 7 IDPs — liver PDFF/volume/iron, pancreas PDFF/volume, visceral and subcutaneous fat volume), cardiac (1.5T CMR, N=27,705; 82 traits — LV/LA/RV/RA volumes, myocardial wall thickness, strain, aortic measures), and brain (3T, N=31,246; 107 structural traits plus tract-averaged DTI and NODDI parameters); a three-region overlap subsample (N=14,122) supported cross-organ correlation. Non-random CMDs-depression co-occurrence was tested by permutation (10,000 shuffles; enrichment ratio 2.3–2.6, all p<0.001). Imaging traits associated with multimorbidity (logistic regression, FDR p<0.05) were tested pairwise (linear regression, standardized betas, FDR p<0.05) to define abdomen-heart-brain "cliques" (1418 from 2573 significant pairs) and a phenotypic network. Genetic architecture used GWAS summary statistics (UK Biobank, European ancestry) with LDSC, LAVA, and GPA for genetic correlation/overlap, and CPASSOC (SHet) for SNP-level cross-trait meta-analysis; independent loci were defined via FUMA, annotated (CADD/RegulomeDB/chromatin states), and mapped to genes (positional, eQTL, 3D-chromatin); GO enrichment used clusterProfiler; TWAS used S-PrediXcan across 24 GTEx v8 tissues. Cox proportional-hazards models (seven nested specifications; MRI composite via 10-fold cross-validated elastic net) evaluated multimorbidity/depression prediction, assessed by Harrell's C-statistic (200 bootstrap CIs), ΔC-statistic, Royston's D, IDI, NRI, and Brier score.

## Limitations

- **Predominantly European ancestry** (92.3–92.6% white race across datasets). Results may not generalize to non-European populations; genetic architecture of pleiotropy is particularly susceptible to population stratification.
- **Cross-sectional design.** Causal direction within cliques cannot be established; the directional correlation patterns (Module 1 vs. Module 2) are mechanistically suggestive but remain unconfirmed without longitudinal imaging or intervention data. (The paper performs no Mendelian randomization — every "MR" in the source refers to Magnetic Resonance imaging.)
- **"Healthy volunteer" selection bias.** UK Biobank is healthier and wealthier than the general UK population; multimorbidity prevalence is substantially underestimated (61/14,122 = 0.43% in the three-region set vs. higher population estimates). Implications: clique network may not reflect organ crosstalk under more advanced multimorbidity.
- **Limited multimorbidity cases in MRI subsample.** Only 61 confirmed three-condition multimorbidity cases in the three-region overlap set; statistical power for fine-grained subgroup analyses is limited.
- **Same-cohort model training and evaluation.** Cox prediction models were both developed and evaluated within the UK Biobank, leading to potentially optimistic performance estimates. External validation is needed.
- **Self-reported lifestyle and SES confounders.** Measurement error in these key covariates cannot be avoided; residual confounding likely.
- **GWAS summary statistics from the same cohort** used for both trait identification and genetic correlation analyses; the paper's stated concern is confounding from shared ancestry, population stratification, and regional socioeconomic factors (it does not frame this as circularity or claim inflated concordance).
- **Cross-sectional nature limits causal inference.** Despite mechanistically motivated interpretation, organ directionality (e.g., liver → heart → brain cascade) cannot be verified without prospective imaging or intervention data.
- **CMDs defined broadly** (independent observation — not stated in the paper's own limitations). CVD (ICD I20-I25, I60-I64, I50) and T2D (E11) are heterogeneous categories; lumping them as "CMDs" masks within-CMD heterogeneity in organ involvement patterns.
