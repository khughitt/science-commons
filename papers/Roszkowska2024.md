## Key Findings

- The paper groups major resistance determinants into five headline categories: tumor heterogeneity, tumor growth kinetics, undruggable drivers, selective therapeutic pressure, and tumor-immune-microenvironment interactions.
- It emphasizes cancer stem cells as a resistant subpopulation with enhanced repair capacity, altered metabolism, and slow-cycling behavior.
- It treats epigenetic mechanisms as adaptive and potentially reversible resistance layers, including DNA methylation, histone modification, chromatin accessibility, and noncoding RNA regulation.
- It argues that microenvironmental factors such as cancer-associated fibroblasts, tumor-associated macrophages, hypoxia, extracellular matrix remodeling, and metabolic constraints can create protective niches.
- It concludes that successful resistance management will require mechanism-specific combination strategies, biomarker-guided selection, and real-time resistance monitoring.

## Limitations

The paper is a broad review and contains many mechanism claims at different evidence levels.
Its mechanistic breadth is useful for taxonomy, but it does not adjudicate which mechanisms dominate in a given cancer, treatment, or timepoint.
The paper should not be used as quantitative evidence for prevalence or effect size without checking its cited primary sources.
