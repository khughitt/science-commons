## One-Sentence Summary

Multi-modal single-cell and spatial analyses reveal that MM breakout lesions
harbor a tumor-microenvironment distinct from bone marrow, featuring "immune
islands" of clonally expanded, exhausted CD39+/PD-1+ CD8 T cells that co-evolve
spatially with divergent TP53- and HOXD3-mutated tumor subclones, identifying
these lesions as bidirectional evolution hotspots with implications for
immunotherapy failure in advanced disease.

## Key Findings

1. **Breakout lesions harbor an ecosystem distinct from rBM and intramedullary
   lesions.** Multi-parametric flow cytometry across 54 matched samples from 16
   NDMM patients (23-plex identity panel: 7,609,355 cells across 54 ecosystems)
   showed that breakout lesions have mean plasma cell infiltration of 89.8% (range
   54.7–98.2%), a strongly enriched CD56bright/CD16− NK cell fraction, augmented
   Tregs, enriched CD39+/PD-1+ CD8 T cells (almost exclusively in BoL),
   mesenchymal stromal cells (MSCs), and endothelial cells. In contrast,
   hematopoietic stem/progenitor populations were severely depleted. Plasma cells
   from BoL upregulated CD16 and CD56 and downregulated CD19/CD27 compared to rBM,
   suggesting an adapted immunophenotype. Intramedullary lesions showed no such
   differences from rBM.

2. **CITE-seq reveals BoL-specific cell states and sustained tumor-immune
   interactions.** In five patients with paired BoL and rBM, CITE-seq of enriched
   cell fractions (13,057 plasma cells; 39,552 TME cells) identified: (a) a BoL-
   specific CD56bright NK subset with high exhaustion-gene expression, intermediate
   cytotoxicity, and reduced CXCR4, phenocopying NK exhaustion in solid tumors;
   (b) M2-like tumor-associated macrophages (TREM2/FOLR2) enriched in BoL;
   (c) enriched cDC1s; (d) stromal cells retaining LEPR but with reduced
   hematopoiesis-supporting factor production (CXCL12, KITL, IL7) and increased
   ECM output (COL3A1, SPARC); and (e) STAB2-negative endothelial cells with
   angiogenic signatures (ANXA1, STC1, IGFBP5) suggesting active neovascularization.
   Arterial ECs in BoL produced elevated CXCL12, likely mediating immune cell
   recruitment.

3. **Breakout lesions contain clonally expanded, likely tumor-specific exhausted
   CD8 T cells.** CITE-seq with TCR-seq identified CD39+/PD-1+ CD8 T cells in
   BoL as predominantly expanded clones overexpressing CTLA4, LAG3, TIM3 (HAVCR2),
   TIGIT, and TOX. Most expanded clones were site-enriched (not detectable in
   paired rBM) and did not match pathogen-associated TCRs, suggesting myeloma
   specificity. These cells highly expressed a neoantigen-reactive TIL gene
   signature from solid cancers. TCR diversity (Chao1 index) was reduced in BoL
   versus rBM stamps (P=0.06 trend).

4. **Spatial analysis reveals microregional heterogeneity within a single BoL.**
   Dissection of one large BoL (patient P02) into 16 spatially defined regions
   (~63,671 TME cells by identity panel; ~46,442 T cells by T cell panel; 16,462
   CITE-seq TME cells) showed that CD39+/PD-1+ CD8 T cells and cDC1s co-localize
   in the same microregions (R=0.69–0.72, P=0.002–0.004), while BoL-specific
   CD56bright NK cells anti-correlate with CD39+/PD-1+ CD8 T cells (R=−0.55,
   P=0.027). Two dominant spatial communities emerged: an antigen-experienced T
   cell cluster (with Tregs, cDC1s) and a plasma cell-dominated cluster devoid of
   antigen-experienced T cells.

5. **Tumor subclonal evolution and T cell clonal diversification are spatially
   coupled.** WGS of six spatial regions plus paired rBM in patient P02 revealed
   gain(1q21) and KRAS^G12A as shared BoL founder events (present at minor
   subclonal level in rBM). Within the BoL two divergent branches evolved: Branch 1
   (HOXD3^E333D mutation, dominant in proximal regions R1–R2) and Branch 2
   (TP53^T284fs frameshift, dominant in distal regions R10/R14/R16), consistent
   with ongoing subclonal diversification under positive selection. TCR clone
   distributions mapped to the same spatial partition: TCR clone #3 was enriched
   in Branch 1 regions; TCR clones #2 and #14 were enriched in Branch 2 regions.
   Extended bulk WGS + TCR-seq across 8 additional patients confirmed the
   association between number of BoL-enriched TCR clones and degree of spatial
   subclonal heterogeneity.

6. **Immune islands define the spatial architecture of breakout lesions.** IHC
   (CD8, CD68, MUM1) of 21 BoL and paired rBM identified focal immune infiltrates
   ("immune islands") surrounded by plasma cell-dominated areas in 9/21 BoL. No
   comparable pattern was found in rBM stamps. Multiplexed immunofluorescence
   (patient P02) revealed 14 cell types and 7 cellular neighborhoods: immune island
   centers (CN-1) contained CD4/CD8 T cells with CD39/LAG3/TIM3 co-expression and
   Ki67+ active proliferation, cDC1s in close proximity to CD39+ CD8 T cells (
   supporting antigen presentation), pericytes/endothelial cells, and blood vessels.
   M2-like macrophages (CD206+) and CD56bright GZMB+ NK cells could infiltrate
   plasma cell-dominated areas. Multiplexed imaging of 9 additional BoL confirmed
   immune islands across patients with interpatient variability in size and
   composition; BoL-specific CD56bright NK phenotype (CD7+/CD56bright/GZMB+/CD16−)
   was detected in 6/9.
